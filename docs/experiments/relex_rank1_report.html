Every two-circuit arm uses a live fast learner and an isolated delayed anchor. The anchor never optimizer-steps and never reads immediate fast weights.

The anchor contributes two distinct signals:

  1. Q: an activation-energy basis that selects the low-dimensional information retained by PowerSGD. During Q-only warmup this is the only anchor output.
  2. M: an EMA of the clean, uncompressed anchor policy gradient. After readiness, it supplies gradient signs to the compressed fast circuit.
Gcorr = 0.25 Gfast + 0.75 |Gfast| sign(Manchor)

The intended role is therefore direction repair: magnitude remains tied to the fast gradient, while the dense anchor supplies a cleaner sign reference. Because the anchor forward is uncompressed, it may also bridge the large semantic gap between dense vLLM inference and the compressed training surrogate.

Anchor-objective audit. The locked fast GRPO objective includes a reference-policy KL loss with coefficient 0.001. The completed runs on immutable pre-fix commits used that KL in the fast PPO update, but their dense anchor M contained only the ratio-one advantage-policy gradient. “Dense and uncompressed” therefore did not mean “the same objective”: the sign reference steering the fast circuit omitted one of the terms steering the fast actor. The corrected W2 validation stopped at global step 12, before its first dense anchor backward at step 20. Existing runs remain useful diagnostics, but none validates the objective-parity-complete M path and none is eligible for default promotion.
Permanent prevention rule. M_anchor must be the dense, uncompressed gradient of the same resolved actor objective, evaluated under the explicitly declared ratio-one surrogate. The implementation now binds the same actor config and mirrors rollout importance weights, entropy regularization, reference-KL type/coefficient, response mask, and loss normalization. The only intentional exceptions are compressed-policy old_log_probs, PPO importance ratio, and clipping, which are invalid between the compressed fast policy and stale/projected uncompressed anchor. Active anchors accept only vanilla plain ppo_loss; non-vanilla policy loss and distillation fail before training, while the runtime binder independently rejects an unexpected fast loss. Missing entropy or reference log-probabilities also fail closed, before any parity=PASS line can be emitted. Focused CPU tests compare the complete fast/anchor gradient at ratio one and exercise missing-input, unsupported-mode, and wrong-callable failures.
512-prompt anchor knob. Every completed comparison arm used the historical anchor.batch_scope=ppo_minibatch behavior: with locked actor.shuffle=false, each fire consumed 256 complete prompt groups × 8 responses = 2,048 sequences, exactly half of the 512-prompt actor update. Their legacy anchor_batch_fraction=1.0 meant 100% of the selected PPO mini-batch, not 100% of the actor update; new telemetry uses the full-update denominator and reports 0.5. The opt-in rollout_batch scope consumes all 512 groups/4,096 sequences, retains complete eight-response GRPO groups, and normalizes once over the DP-global valid-token count while dynamic microbatches bound peak activation memory. This shared Q+M scope is covered by focused CPU tests but was not GPU-validated. It approximately doubles anchor compute and CPU replay payload per fire but does not increase Q/M communication.

The cadence-boundary defect and correction

The audit found that anchor-owned Q was previously replaced at the start of a PPO train minibatch, after old_log_probs had already been recomputed. At global step 10 this coincided with an actor PPO-KL spike to −4.07. Commit a7252bd6 now stages and broadcasts Q as a candidate, keeps live Q frozen across all minibatches, activates it only after a successful update_actor, and discards it if that update fails. M still corrects the firing optimizer tick immediately; only Q visibility is delayed to the next paired old/current-policy step.

This was not compression inside the anchor gradient. The isolated anchor hook observes the raw dense activation to build the next Q sketch and explicitly returns that activation unchanged. The defect was only that the resulting Q candidate became visible to the fast circuit before the PPO policy pair ended.

The corrected W=2 GPU gate passed at step 10: both DP ranks staged all 7 boundaries with cross-rank deviation 0.0, then exactly one post-update activation landed. Counters were updates=1, broadcasts=1, activations=1, overwrites=0. Cadence-boundary PPO-KL was +0.0946 instead of −4.0669, while the sampled-token rollout→train KL remained 13.8568. This is the desired distinction: internal PPO pairing is repaired; the broader dense/compressed mismatch is still visible and scientifically material.

Corrected strict W=4 completed the same gate with Q update/broadcast/activation counters 10/10/10, zero stage overwrites, and zero cross-rank Q deviation. Its final compressed-communication ratio was 0.05014. This validates the Q transaction and communication path; its larger final rollout→train KL shows that transactional correctness does not remove the separate training–inference mismatch.

Why can it still learn?

  • The PPO old-policy log-probability is recomputed through the compressed PowerSGD path rather than using dense vLLM rollout log-probabilities as the PPO denominator. The corrected runs now guarantee the same frozen Q for old and current forwards.
  • Dense rollout rewards and group-relative advantages still provide task signal, even though the compressed training policy makes the update off-policy with respect to inference.
  • The uncompressed anchor gradient supplies a dense-policy directional reference, and gradient clipping bounds the very large compressed surrogate gradients. Both are plausible stabilizers, not yet isolated causes.

An anchor-off run is still required before making a causal claim about the anchor bridging this gap.

Here C=K=20, so every prediction covers one checkpoint interval. With two optimizer ticks per trainer step, that means an anchor refresh every 10 global steps. W=4 first became ready at optimizer tick 80/global step 40, using history [1, 19, 39, 59] to predict generator tick 79.

The reconstruction-error drop after global step 10 is only the first anchor-owned PowerSGD Q refresh: the candidate is built at tick 20 and becomes visible at step 11. It is not a projected anchor gradient or spectral correction. Strict W=2 has no projection, dense anchor backward, or M before global step 20; all three become ready together at step 20. Strict W=4 receives Q-only refreshes at steps 10, 20, and 30, then first projects weights, runs the dense anchor backward, and enables M at step 40. Consequently, W=4's entropy turn around step 20 cannot be caused by M—it precedes M by 20 global steps.

Under the corrected handoff, a Q candidate produced at tick 20 stays staged through the rest of that actor update and becomes live at its successful exit. The next global step recomputes old log-probability with that same Q and retains it through both optimizer ticks. This changes no checkpoint age, prediction horizon, cadence, or delay; it only restores the PPO pairing invariant.

The qboot-v2 arms add one transaction-safe Q observation before the first compressed old-log-probability: exact live fast weights and the first rollout batch produce Q1 without a backward pass, then the real old and current PPO forwards both use Q1. At global step 10, stale_correct runs the paired initial/base anchor backward and makes all-floating M available. The shared W=4/minimum-2 history then computes W2 at step 20, W3 at 30, and sliding W4 from 40. In the control, strength zero keeps the applied weights at the newest exact checkpoint and uses its paired stale trajectories; the composite applies strength one and uses current trajectories on projected fires.

  • Smaller C, fixed K: fresher Q/M and faster wall-clock refresh, but more anchor compute and a larger K/C extrapolation measured in checkpoint gaps.
  • Larger C, fixed K: cheaper anchor operation and a wider history, but the correction is held stale longer between refreshes.
  • Larger K, fixed C: the exact anchor source is older and the projector must forecast farther.
  • W=2: ready near global step 20, but exactly a two-point secant with no meaningful EVR/R² diagnostic.
  • W=4: ready near global step 40 and meaningfully estimates/denoises a rank-1 trajectory.
  • qboot-v2 progressive W=4: retains/slides at four checkpoints but becomes ready with two: secant at step 20, rank-1 OLS over two deltas at step 30, and full three-delta W=4 OLS from step 40. M already exists from the first fire at step 10, so readiness no longer gates its initial activation.

The paper establishes rank-1 trajectory smoothness and denoising, but does not define this live asynchronous cadence/delay system; that part is our adaptation. See the RELEX ablations.

Is weight projection actually correct?

The legacy pre-fix W=4 run showed strong in-window structure: mean EVR 0.958 and mean temporal R² 0.993. Those values validate the fitted history, not future forecast accuracy.

One ninth prediction remained pending when training ended because its future exact checkpoint lies beyond step 100. These results show that the secant mechanism executed correctly and became predictively useful on the sampled coordinates; they do not establish whole-tensor or multi-seed superiority.

Corrected strict W=4 resolved six causal horizons, and the four-role aggregate beat stale on all six. Across horizons, mean projected RMSE was 1.0708e−5 versus stale RMSE 1.1711e−5, mean skill was +0.1576, and mean direction cosine was 0.5800. Its final in-window fit was mean EVR 0.9695 and mean R² 0.9911. The effect was heterogeneous: embedding samples lost all six horizons, while decoder and norm samples supplied the aggregate gain. The non-trivial W=4 projector therefore executed correctly and had sampled causal predictive value, but its advantage was materially smaller and less uniform than W=2's in this single run.

The qboot-v2 composite resolved eight horizons and won three. Its mean projected RMSE was 1.2485e−5 versus stale RMSE 1.2246e−5, mean skill was −0.0580, and mean direction cosine was 0.3982. All 338 tensors were projected and transaction invariants passed, so this is evidence that the mechanism ran—not that the W2→W3→W4 predictor beat the newest exact checkpoint on the sampled coordinates. One terminal prediction correctly remains pending because its delayed exact target would arrive at global step 110.

Scope. Every full tensor is projected independently, while the causal score observes only four tensors and 16 deterministic coordinates per tensor per horizon.

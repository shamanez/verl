What is the anchor signal helping?

Corrected W=2 and the dense control are complete. The earlier W=4 result remains a legacy pre-fix diagnostic; corrected W=4 is now running. Every two-circuit arm uses a live fast learner and an isolated delayed anchor. The anchor never optimizer-steps and never reads immediate fast weights.

The anchor contributes two distinct signals:

  1. Q: an activation-energy basis that selects the low-dimensional information retained by PowerSGD. During Q-only warmup this is the only anchor output.
  2. M: an EMA of the clean, uncompressed anchor policy gradient. After readiness, it supplies gradient signs to the compressed fast circuit.
Gcorr = 0.25 Gfast + 0.75 |Gfast| sign(Manchor)

The intended role is therefore direction repair: magnitude remains tied to the fast gradient, while the dense anchor supplies a cleaner sign reference. Because the anchor forward is uncompressed, it may also bridge the large semantic gap between dense vLLM inference and the compressed training surrogate.

The cadence-boundary defect and correction

The audit found that anchor-owned Q was previously replaced at the start of a PPO train minibatch, after old_log_probs had already been recomputed. At global step 10 this coincided with an actor PPO-KL spike to −4.07. Commit a7252bd6 now stages and broadcasts Q as a candidate, keeps live Q frozen across all minibatches, activates it only after a successful update_actor, and discards it if that update fails. M still corrects the firing optimizer tick immediately; only Q visibility is delayed to the next paired old/current-policy step.

This was not compression inside the anchor gradient. The isolated anchor hook observes the raw dense activation to build the next Q sketch and explicitly returns that activation unchanged. The defect was only that the resulting Q candidate became visible to the fast circuit before the PPO policy pair ended.

The corrected W=2 GPU gate passed at step 10: both DP ranks staged all 7 boundaries with cross-rank deviation 0.0, then exactly one post-update activation landed. Counters were updates=1, broadcasts=1, activations=1, overwrites=0. Cadence-boundary PPO-KL was +0.0946 instead of −4.0669, while the sampled-token rollout→train KL remained 13.8568. This is the desired distinction: internal PPO pairing is repaired; the broader dense/compressed mismatch is still visible and scientifically material.

Why can it still learn?

  • The PPO old-policy log-probability is recomputed through the compressed PowerSGD path rather than using dense vLLM rollout log-probabilities as the PPO denominator. The corrected runs now guarantee the same frozen Q for old and current forwards.
  • Dense rollout rewards and group-relative advantages still provide task signal, even though the compressed training policy makes the update off-policy with respect to inference.
  • The uncompressed anchor gradient supplies a dense-policy directional reference, and gradient clipping bounds the very large compressed surrogate gradients. Both are plausible stabilizers, not yet isolated causes.

Here C=K=20, so every prediction covers one checkpoint interval. With two optimizer ticks per trainer step, that means an anchor refresh every 10 global steps. W=4 first became ready at optimizer tick 80/global step 40, using history [1, 19, 39, 59] to predict generator tick 79.

The reconstruction-error drop after global step 10 is only the first anchor-owned PowerSGD Q refresh: the candidate is built at tick 20 and becomes visible at step 11. It is not a projected anchor gradient or spectral correction. Strict W=2 has no projection, dense anchor backward, or M before global step 20; all three become ready together at step 20. Strict W=4 receives Q-only refreshes at steps 10, 20, and 30, then first projects weights, runs the dense anchor backward, and enables M at step 40. Consequently, W=4's entropy turn around step 20 cannot be caused by M—it precedes M by 20 global steps.

Under the corrected handoff, a Q candidate produced at tick 20 stays staged through the rest of that actor update and becomes live at its successful exit. The next global step recomputes old log-probability with that same Q and retains it through both optimizer ticks. This changes no checkpoint age, prediction horizon, cadence, or delay; it only restores the PPO pairing invariant.

  • Smaller C, fixed K: fresher Q/M and faster wall-clock refresh, but more anchor compute and a larger K/C extrapolation measured in checkpoint gaps.
  • Larger C, fixed K: cheaper anchor operation and a wider history, but the correction is held stale longer between refreshes.
  • Larger K, fixed C: the exact anchor source is older and the projector must forecast farther.
  • W=2: ready near global step 20, but exactly a two-point secant with no meaningful EVR/R² diagnostic.
  • W=4: ready near global step 40 and meaningfully estimates/denoises a rank-1 trajectory.
  • Progressive W=4: retains/slides at four checkpoints but becomes ready with two: secant at step 20, rank-1 OLS over two deltas at step 30, and full three-delta W=4 OLS from step 40. This tests a practical readiness policy, not M in isolation.

The paper establishes rank-1 trajectory smoothness and denoising, but does not define this live asynchronous cadence/delay system; that part is our adaptation. See the RELEX ablations.

These results show that the secant mechanism executed correctly and became predictively useful on the sampled coordinates; they do not establish whole-tensor or multi-seed superiority.

Corrected strict W=4 has now produced its first causal resolution at step 50. Its projected RMSE was 9.5685e−6 versus stale RMSE 1.2433e−5, giving skill +0.4077 and direction cosine 0.7299. Its current in-window fit is mean EVR 0.9663 and mean R² 0.9919. This is early but directly confirms that the corrected non-trivial W=4 projector executed and beat the stale sampled-weight baseline on its first resolved horizon.

Scope. Every full tensor is projected independently, while the causal score observes only four tensors and 16 deterministic coordinates per tensor per horizon. W=2's two-checkpoint secant is algebraically exact on its one delta; strict W=4 supplies the non-trivial rank-1 OLS comparison, and its final causal summary remains pending.

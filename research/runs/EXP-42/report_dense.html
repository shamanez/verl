How the normal GRPO weights behave: drift, projectability, and linearity

A single look at the plain GRPO run (codec off), measured directly from the per-tick weight-trajectory sketch. How much do the weights move, how many steps ahead can a linear rule predict them, how well does the run learn, and do the updates actually move roughly linearly the way the RLVR-linear result claims.

196 decoder matrices160 ticks (80 steps)count-sketch k=4096codec OFF (dense)

Headline

You can linearly project the weights about 5 global steps ahead (10 ticks) before projection stops helping. Beyond that the linear rule overshoots the true weight.

The trajectory is locally close to linear (R squared = 0.80 at one tick) but that linearity decays with distance (R squared = 0.32 at the K=10 staleness scale), which is exactly why projection helps only out to the crossover.

How the weights change over the run

By the end of the run the decoder weights have moved only about 0.057 percent of their initial norm (median matrix). The motion is tiny in magnitude: GRPO at lr 1e-6 on an already-instruction-tuned 1.5B model is a gentle nudge, not a large weight change. The right panel is the per-tick step size, a roughly steady small step. What matters for projection is the DIRECTION of this small motion, analysed next.

At the fine scale the attention matrices move slightly more linearly (attention R2 0.81 vs MLP 0.74 at 1 tick). Both families lose linearity by the K=10 scale (attention 0.32, MLP 0.31). The layer-depth terciles show whether early or late layers hold their direction longer. The takeaway is uniform: linearity is a fine-scale property across the whole decoder, and it decays with horizon everywhere, not just in one region.

Performance of the normal run

Final validation accuracy 0.7695 on GSM8K. The reward climbs early and then holds, a healthy plain-GRPO control.

Do we observe the RLVR-linear result

PARTIALLY. The claim that RLVR weight updates move roughly linearly with R squared about 0.9 holds for this run only at the finest scale: per-matrix R squared is 0.80 at one tick (in the ballpark of the 0.9 reference), and it falls to 0.32 by the K=10 scale. So the weights move almost linearly over a step or two, then the path curves. The look-ahead anchor operates at the K=10 scale, where linearity has already broken down, which is the mechanism behind the overshoot we measured. The single-scale 'weights move linearly' headline is true locally but scale-dependent: it should be stated with the scale attached.

Scope and provenance

  • Source: regimeA per-tick weight sketch, runs/EXP-42/regimeA/weights (196 matrices, 160 ticks). Decoder matrices only (q/k/v/o/gate/up/down); the embeddings, RMSNorm gains and biases are not in this run.
  • All metrics computed on the MacBook from the sketch, GPU-free. The count-sketch is linear and norm-preserving, so weight-difference norms and cosines reconstruct from it.
  • Linearity R squared = mean over ticks of cos squared between the previous and next displacement at the given scale (the regression of next displacement on previous, through the origin), then percentiles across matrices.

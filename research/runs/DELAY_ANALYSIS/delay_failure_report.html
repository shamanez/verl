Communication-efficient GRPO · Anchor-latency analysis

Why the comm-eff EMA method is stable at anchor latency K=5 but collapses at K=20

The single method under study is the EMA merger signed_ema (α=0.25, βanc=0.50). One knob — the anchor latency K — turns a working method into a collapsing one. The theory, the evidence, and the next steps.

Prepared for Head of Research Date 2026-06-18 Model / data Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct · GSM8K (accel surface) Source runs EXP-36B (K=5) · EXP-37 (K=20) · dense control

1Executive summary

The communication-efficient EMA method (signed_ema, α=0.25, βanc=0.50) works at anchor latency K=5 — val@50 0.7362, within ~0.03 of the dense ceiling (0.7657). Raising the same method to K=20 (a 10-global-step staleness window) drops val@50 to 0.6482 and then triggers a late training collapse: a length spiral over steps 93–100 drives val down to 0.4435 at step 100 — below the no-merger PowerSGD floor of 0.6300. The latency genuinely ran (anchor_backwards = 10), so this is a real result, not a config artifact.

The cause is the growing anchor-gradient staleness that K controls. The staleness error ‖g(θt)−g(θt−K)‖ grows roughly linearly in K from curvature, plus a super-linear off-policy GRPO distribution-shift term; the stale EMA correction becomes a coherent, reward-flat tangential push (a “length ratchet”) that nothing brakes on a no-KL / no-entropy objective. Within the two-knob anchor circuit, the verdict is that gradient staleness (delay_K) is PRIMARY (~70–80% of the val drop, ~90% of the ignition) and Q-refresh cadence is SECONDARY (~20–35%). The two knobs share a single firing gate (raised together), so this split is theory-estimated and not yet empirically isolated — which makes a small decoupling experiment the single highest-value next step (§6).

2Setup: K, the anchor / EMA circuit, and what changed

In this fork the dense gradient at the pipeline boundary is replaced by a compressed fast gradient G_comp (PowerSGD-projected), periodically corrected by an anchor: a no-hook clone of the model that runs forward/backward from a stale weight snapshot θt−K and produces a full-coverage dense gradient M. The EMA merger under study — signed_ema — folds M into G_comp each step as G_corr = α·G_comp + (1−α)·|G_comp|·sign(M): it keeps the fast gradient's magnitude but adopts the anchor's sign on each coordinate. With βanc=0.50 the anchor gradient itself is lightly averaged across fires.

What K means

K is the anchor latency, counted in optimizer ticks, not global steps. With the locked surface (train_batch=128, ppo_mini=64) there are 2 ticks per global step. K folds two structurally-confounded sub-knobs that we raise together through a single firing gate:

  • delay_K — how stale M is. M is the dense gradient at θt−K on the data those weights generated (paired replay).
  • cadence — how often the anchor fires: each fire refreshes M, re-fits the PowerSGD basis Q, and re-bases the held correction.

So K=5 = 2.5 global steps of staleness, with Q refreshed every 2.5 global steps; K=20 = 10 global steps of staleness, with Q refreshed every 10 global steps — 4× the lag and 4× the refresh interval. EXP-37 changed only these two knobs (5 → 20) relative to the good baseline EXP-36B, and extended the horizon to 100 steps to probe the back half. Everything else — the merger (signed_ema, α=0.25, βanc=0.50), codec (PowerSGD r=77), and surface — is identical.

4Theory: why K=5 works, why K=20 collapses

4.1  Why K=5 works

At short staleness the K-stale gradient is a good proxy for the current one: g(θt−K) ≈ g(θt), so the staleness error eK = g(θt) − g(θt−K) is small. The EMA correction stays informative and bounded: sign(M) mostly agrees with the live gradient, so the merger is a near-no-op on the coordinates that matter and the off-policy distribution shift between the K-stale weights and the current policy is small. The result is a method that tracks dense from just below — val@50 0.7362 vs the 0.7657 ceiling, a ~0.03 gap within the rollout draw band.

4.2  Why K=20 collapses

Staleness corrupts the correction through two compounding terms, and the corruption then rectifies into a length spiral. The chain has four links.

(a) The staleness error grows: a linear term plus a super-linear term

Decompose eK into:

  • Weight drift (linear in K): bounded by LH · K · η · ‖u‖ — the gradient simply moved as the weights moved. Purely geometrically, K=20 has 4× the drift of K=5.
  • Off-policy distribution shift (super-linear in K): under GRPO the loss has no fixed objective — M was computed on data sampled from a different policy πθt−K. Importance-weight variance grows ~exp(DKL); the GRPO clip truncates the worst weights, bounding that variance at the cost of a bias that grows with divergence. This is the term that makes K=20 qualitatively worse than a linear extrapolation of K=5 would predict.

Because Adam + grad-clip make the update direction load-bearing, the relevant quantity is the angular error. As ‖eK‖ grows toward ‖g(θt)‖, sign(M) increasingly points uphill on individual coordinates — the coordinate-level source of correlated wrong-sign flips in the merger.

(b) The corruption becomes a length ratchet

The merger's correction is, by construction, tangential to the live gradient (the sign-flipped magnitude on disagreeing coordinates does no first-order work against the loss). It is therefore a persistent push along the reward-flat manifold. “Correct-but-longer” is exactly such a reward-flat ridge: emitting more tokens barely changes verifier reward. With no KL and no entropy term, nothing brakes motion along it. Crucially, token-mean loss normalization makes each token's gradient weaker as sequences lengthen, so the brake on further growth weakens as length grows (dF/dℓ > 0) — the signature of a positive-feedback loop. Staleness sets the gain of this loop: larger K means a larger per-step tangential dose, moving the system from sub-critical (K=5: ratchet present but censored at the 50-step horizon) to super-critical (K=20: ignites within 100 steps).

(c) Why the onset is late, not immediate

The spiral is the integral of a small biased force, not an instantaneous instability. It must accumulate past a token-mean amplification threshold before it self-sustains. Two things back-load the damage to epoch 1: (i) cumulative drift ‖θt−θ0‖ is largest then, maximizing the off-policy term exactly when the second epoch begins; and (ii) the held correction is re-applied across the long cadence window, integrating later (more mis-aimed) fires. EXP-37's stability through ~step 58 and ignition in epoch 1 is exactly this threshold-crossing signature.

(d) The information-ceiling argument: staleness can only degrade

Any deterministic correction Φ(G_comp, M) is measurable with respect to σ(M) = σ(g(θt), g(θt−K)) and is therefore capped at dense. A K-stale M is, by the data-processing inequality, a noisier channel observation of the current gradient than a fresh M — the mutual information I(MK; g(θt)) is monotonically non-increasing in K. Staleness adds no curvature, no exploration, no cross-rank signal; it only corrupts the dense reconstruction. So the best outcome of any latency setting is “approach dense from below,” and the worst — observed at K=20 — is “fall below the no-merger floor.” There is no value of K that helps. The practical question for the async north-star (one slow anchor, fast swarm, lag always present) is therefore robustness to staleness, never exploitation of it.

5The two-hypothesis verdict

K bundles two sub-knobs. The head-of-research question is which one drives the collapse. Both analysts — one owning the staleness axis (delay_K), one owning the codec/cadence axis — converge on the same weighting.

H1 — Is the anchor gradient M too stale? (delay_K)

YES · PRIMARY

~70–80% of the val drop, ~90% of the ignition. The off-policy distribution-shift term (§4.2a) is a pure function of K, and the ratchet gain (§4.2b) scales with ‖eK‖ ∝ K. The spiral is driven by lag — and delay_K is the lag.

H2 — Is the Q correction too infrequent? (cadence)

CONTRIBUTES · SECONDARY

~20–35%. A frozen Q injects a bounded, self-healing error: warm_start pulls Q one power-iteration step toward the current subspace at each fire, and the activation geometry is slow-varying, so a 10-step-stale Q is still a good projector. Cadence is the clock, not the engine.

The structural confound

EXP-37 alone cannot cleanly separate the two, because in the code cadence and delay_K are gated by the same per-tick counter through the same anchor_should_fire(step, cadence) call: the Q-update, M-refresh, and broadcast all happen in one fire block. Raising K to 20 means “Q frozen 4× longer” and “M 4× staler” are inseparable in this single run. This is precisely why the decoupling experiment (§6) is the highest-value next step.

The clean cadence fingerprint

There is one isolable cadence signature already in the data: a pg_clipfrac spike at every Q-refresh step (10, 20, …, 100). Each fire jumps the projector Q discontinuously, mismatching the importance ratio for one step — clipfrac of ~0.10–0.19 at refresh steps vs. a ~0.004–0.03 floor between them. These spikes are bigger precisely because the jumps are rarer at cadence 20. This is the cleanest, already-available pure-cadence readout.

6Ranked next steps

Ranked by expected payoff per unit cost, all respecting the async constraint (the anchor must remain a lagging reference; corrections must be cross-rank-identical and degrade gracefully as the anchor ages).

1The 2×2 decoupling experiment

Cells: {delay_K, cadence} ∈ {5, 20} · surface: accel, 100 steps, same signed_ema (α=0.25, β=0.50)

The single experiment that settles the structural confound. Cell {delay_K=20, cadence=5} is the most informative — a short held-correction window (refresh every 5 ticks) but large per-correction magnitude. If it reproduces the ignition, the gain is unambiguously the delay_K magnitude, not Q-refresh rarity — converting the ~75/25 attribution from theory to data.

2Staleness-aware held-δ dose decay

Knob: delta_momentum_age_decay (already in the code) · value: μ ≈ 0.85–0.90

Scale the held correction by μage as it ages, fading a stale correction toward zero before the next fire. At K=20, 0.8820 ≈ 0.08, so the correction is strongest right after a fresh fire and near-zero just before the next — directly capping the integrated tangential dose (the ratchet gain in §4.2b) without touching K or the two-circuit structure. Cross-rank-identical and graceful-by-age, so async-admissible. Note: age-decay was mildly harmful as a freshness lever at K=5 (it discards good fresh signal), but at K=20 the late-window correction is bad signal — a falsifiable sub-prediction that it helps where it hurt.

Cost: config-only, no substrate change. Test inside the decoupling run.

3Decouple Q-refresh from M-staleness (q_cadence)

Knob: NEW q_cadence · value: q_cadence=5 (or per-tick) while delay_K=20

Refresh Q cheaply and often even while M stays stale. The Q update is just orth(V) on the fast-path activation sketch plus a small H×r broadcast — it needs no stale backward, so refreshing Q more often is nearly free relative to the anchor pass that produces M. This both shrinks the per-refresh projector-jump shock and is what makes the step 1 “delay-only” Cell B expressible. Engineering flag to systems: the replay ring keys its fire-aware retention on cadence (anchor.py:426, _keep_residue = (−delay_K) % cadence); a split cadence requires the ring to key on the M-refresh cadence, not the Q one. This is the one real subtlety.

Cost: new knob (off the locked surface) + ring re-key. Q-refresh itself is nearly free.

4Keep beta_anc = 0 family + adaptive-λ ratio mode with a low cap

Knobs: beta_anc=0 · adaptive_lambda_mode="ratio", lambda_cap ≈ 0.5

EMA-of-stale compounds staleness — averaging old gradients makes M even more lagging, and GRPO's clip tolerates far less of that than SFT. Keep beta_anc=0 and fight staleness with dose decay (#2), not smoothing. As a safety belt, gate the dose on measured staleness: ct = ‖δ‖/‖gm‖ is a direct proxy — a large residual relative to the live gradient means the anchor is badly aged, so the dose should drop. A low lambda_cap ensures a staleness spike cannot spike the dose. Bounded, variable-staleness-safe, cross-rank-identical.

Cost: config-only. Complements #2; can run in the same cell.

×Ruled out

Delay-compensation / extrapolation / lead. Predicting g(θt) from the stale gradient (Nesterov-style lead) is forbidden by the async target: the anchor is a single slow node that can never lead the fast swarm. Admissible levers use the anchor as a lagging reference only.

Chasing PowerSGD rank r. Reconstruction error is flat across r∈[77,102] — the codec is not coverage-starved, so raising r buys little and costs comm linearly. Do not lead with it.

7Caveats

  • n=1 draws. Every val number is a single run. Rollout nondeterminism is ~±0.024 (the dense draw band), so differences below that are within noise. The EXP-37 effects (0.088 drop at val@50, collapse to 0.4435) are far outside it; the cell-to-cell predictions in §6 should be read with the ±0.024 band in mind.
  • The cadence/delay confound limits EXP-37's standalone attribution. Because cadence and delay_K share the same firing gate and were raised together, the 70–80% / 20–35% split is a theory-grounded estimate, not a measured decomposition. The §6 decoupling run (especially Cell B) is what converts it from estimate to measurement.
  • Comm-cost accounting. The reported bytes_ratio ≈ 0.0502 counts only the fast-path Y + amortized Q; the full-dense M broadcast is a known-uncounted term and is not the total communication cost.

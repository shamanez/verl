Communication-efficient GRPO · Delay analysis

Why increasing anchor latency from 5/5 to 20/20 breaks comm-eff GRPO training

A two-hypothesis verdict (stale anchor gradient M vs. infrequent Q correction), the empirical evidence, mechanism, and ranked recommendations.

Prepared for Head of Research Date 2026-06-18 Model / data Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct · GSM8K (accel surface) Source runs EXP-36B / EXP-36C / EXP-37 (+ EXP-38 predicted)

1Executive summary

Raising the anchor latency from 5/5 to 20/20 optimizer ticks (a 10-global-step staleness window) cost ~0.088 val@50 (0.7362 → 0.6482) and triggered a late training collapse: a length spiral over steps 93–100 drove val from 0.6482 down to 0.4435 — below the no-merger PowerSGD floor of 0.6300. The latency genuinely ran (anchor_backwards = 10), so this is a real result, not a config artifact.

The root cause is primarily anchor-gradient (M) staleness — the delay_K axis — not Q-refresh infrequency. Both analysts independently weight delay_K at roughly 70–80% of the val drop and ~90% of the ignition; Q-cadence contributes a real but bounded ~20–35%. The two knobs are gated by the same firing call in the code, so EXP-37 alone cannot fully separate them — which makes a small decoupling experiment the single highest-value next step. Concrete, ranked recommendations follow in §6, and the recommended runs in §7.

2The question & setup

In this fork the dense gradient at the pipeline boundary is replaced by a compressed fast gradient G_comp (PowerSGD-projected), periodically corrected by an anchor: a no-hook clone of the model that runs forward/backward from a stale weight snapshot θt−K and produces a full-coverage dense gradient M. A merger (here signed_ema) folds M into G_comp each step. Two knobs control the anchor's lag:

  • delay_K — how stale M is. M is the dense gradient at θt−K on the data those weights generated (paired replay).
  • cadence — how often the anchor fires: it refreshes M, re-fits the PowerSGD basis Q, and re-bases the held correction.

Both are counted in optimizer ticks, not global steps. With the locked surface (train_batch=128, ppo_mini=64) there are 2 ticks per global step, so 20 ticks = 10 global steps of staleness and a refresh every 10 global steps — 4× the lag of the 5/5 baseline. EXP-37 changed only these two knobs (5/5 → 20/20) relative to the good baseline EXP-36B, and extended the horizon to 100 steps to probe the back half.

4Mechanism & theory

Stale M biases the update through two compounding terms, and the bias rectifies into a length spiral. The chain has four links.

4.1  Why stale M biases the step — a linear term plus a super-linear term

Treat M as a delayed estimate of the true current gradient. The staleness error eK = g(θt) − g(θt−K) decomposes into:

  • Weight drift (linear in K): bounded by LH · K · η · ‖u‖ — the gradient simply moved as the weights moved. Purely geometrically, K=20 has 4× the drift of K=5.
  • Off-policy distribution shift (super-linear in K): under GRPO the loss has no fixed objective — M was computed on data sampled from a different policy πθt−K. Importance-weight variance grows ~exp(DKL); the GRPO clip converts that variance into a steadily growing systematic bias. This is the term that makes 20/20 qualitatively worse than a linear extrapolation of 5/5 would predict.

Because Adam + grad-clip make the update direction load-bearing, the relevant quantity is the angular error. As ‖eK‖ grows toward ‖g(θt)‖, the merger increasingly points uphill on individual coordinates — the coordinate-level source of the ~50% sign-disagreement in signed_ema.

4.2  Why it becomes a LENGTH spiral — a tangential ratchet

The merger's correction is, by construction, orthogonal to the live gradient (the off-subspace residual, or the sign-flipped magnitude on disagreeing coordinates). An orthogonal force does no first-order work against the loss — it is a persistent tangential push along the reward-flat manifold. "Correct-but-longer" is exactly such a reward-flat ridge: emitting more tokens barely changes verifier reward. With no KL and no entropy term, nothing brakes motion along it. Crucially, token-mean loss normalization makes each token's gradient weaker as sequences lengthen, so the brake on further growth weakens as length grows (dF/dℓ > 0) — the signature of a positive-feedback loop. Staleness sets the gain of this loop: larger K means a larger per-step tangential dose, moving the system from sub-critical (K=5: ratchet present but censored at the 50-step horizon) to super-critical (K=20: ignites within 100 steps).

4.3  Why the onset is LATE (steps 50–100), not immediate

The spiral is the integral of a small biased force, not an instantaneous instability. It must accumulate past a token-mean amplification threshold before it self-sustains. Two things back-load the damage to epoch 1: (i) cumulative drift ‖θt−θ0‖ is largest then, maximizing the off-policy term exactly when the second epoch begins; and (ii) the held correction is re-applied across the long cadence window, integrating later (more mis-aimed) fires. EXP-37's stability through ~step 58 and ignition in epoch 1 is exactly this threshold-crossing signature.

4.4  The information-ceiling (σ(M)) argument — staleness can only degrade

Any deterministic correction Φ(G_comp, M) is measurable with respect to σ(M) = σ(g(θt), g(θt−K)) and is therefore capped at dense. A K-stale M is, by the data-processing inequality, a noisier channel observation of the current gradient than a fresh M — mutual information I(MK; g(θt)) is monotonically non-increasing in K. Staleness adds no curvature, no exploration, no cross-rank signal; it only corrupts the dense reconstruction. So the best outcome of any latency setting is "approach dense from below," and the worst — observed at K=20 — is "fall below the no-merger floor." There is no value of K that helps. The practical question for the async north-star (one slow anchor, fast swarm, lag always present) is therefore robustness to staleness, never exploitation of it.

5The two-hypothesis verdict

The operator asked two explicit questions. Both analysts — one owning the staleness axis, one owning the codec/cadence axis — converge on the same weighting.

H1 — Is the anchor gradient M too stale? (delay_K)

YES · PRIMARY

~70–80% of the val drop, ~90% of the ignition. The off-policy distribution-shift term (§4.1) is a pure function of K, and the ratchet gain (§4.2) scales with ‖eK‖ ∝ K. The "lag-not-dose" history (EXP-27) says the spiral is driven by lag — and delay_K is the lag.

H2 — Is the Q correction too infrequent? (cadence)

CONTRIBUTES · SECONDARY

~20–35%. A frozen Q injects a bounded, self-healing error: warm_start pulls Q one power-iteration step toward the current subspace at each fire, and the activation geometry is slow-varying, so a 10-step-stale Q is still a good projector. Cadence is the clock, not the engine.

The structural confound

EXP-37 alone cannot cleanly separate the two, because in the code cadence and delay_K are gated by the same per-tick counter through the same anchor_should_fire(step, cadence) call (transformer_impl.py:1474): the Q-update, M-refresh, and broadcast all happen in one fire block. Raising both to 20 together means "Q frozen 4× longer" and "M 4× staler" are inseparable in this single run. This is precisely why the decoupling experiment (§7) is the highest-value next step.

The clean cadence fingerprint

There is one isolable cadence signature already in the data: a pg_clipfrac spike at every Q-refresh step (10, 20, …, 100). Each fire jumps the projector Q discontinuously, mismatching the importance ratio for one step — clipfrac of ~0.10–0.19 at refresh steps vs. a ~0.004–0.03 floor between them. These spikes are bigger precisely because the jumps are rarer at cadence 20. This is the cleanest, already-available pure-cadence readout, and it should reappear at the same steps in any same-codec run regardless of merger family.

6Practical recommendations

Ranked by expected payoff per unit cost, all respecting the async constraint (the anchor must remain a lagging reference; corrections must be cross-rank-identical and degrade gracefully as the anchor ages).

1Staleness-aware held-δ dose decay

Knob: delta_momentum_age_decay (already in delayed_ef) · value: μ ≈ 0.85–0.90

Scale the held correction by μage as it ages, fading a stale δ toward zero before the next fire. At K=20, 0.8820 ≈ 0.08, so the correction is strongest right after a fresh fire and near-zero just before the next — directly capping the integrated tangential dose (the ratchet gain in §4.2) without touching K or the two-circuit structure. Cross-rank-identical and graceful-by-age, so async-admissible. Note: age-decay was mildly harmful as a freshness lever at 5/5 (it discards good fresh signal), but at K=20 the late-window δ is bad signal — a falsifiable sub-prediction that it helps where it hurt.

Cost: config-only, no substrate change. Test inside the decoupling run.

2Decouple q_cadence from delay_K

Knob: NEW q_cadence · value: q_cadence=5 (or per-tick) while delay_K=20

Refresh Q cheaply and often even while M stays stale. The Q update is just orth(V) on the fast-path activation sketch plus a small H×r broadcast — it needs no stale backward, so refreshing Q more often is nearly free relative to the anchor pass that produces M. This both shrinks the per-refresh projector-jump shock and tests the cadence hypothesis directly. Engineering flag to systems: the replay ring keys its fire-aware retention on cadence (anchor.py:426, _keep_residue = (−delay_K) % cadence); a split cadence requires the ring to key on the M-refresh cadence, not the Q one. This is the one real subtlety, and it is what makes the §7 "delay-only" cell expressible.

Cost: new knob (off the locked surface) + ring re-key. Q-refresh itself is nearly free.

3Keep beta_anc = 0

Knob: beta_anc · value: 0 (do not add EMA to "smooth" a noisy stale M)

It is tempting to smooth a noisy stale M with an EMA, but EMA compounds staleness — averaging old gradients makes M even more lagging, and GRPO's clip tolerates far less of that than SFT. β∈[0,0.5] is a flat free-averaging region at 5/5, but at K=20 any β>0 pushes the marginal staleness into the harmful regime. Fight staleness with dose decay (#1), not smoothing.

Cost: none — this is the current default; the recommendation is to not change it.

4Adaptive-λ ratio mode with a low cap

Knob: adaptive_lambda_mode="ratio", low lambda_cap · value: cap ≈ 0.5

Gate the dose on measured staleness: ct = ‖δ‖/‖gm‖ is a direct proxy — a large residual relative to the live gradient means the anchor is badly aged, so the dose should drop. A low lambda_cap ensures a staleness spike cannot spike the dose. Bounded, variable-staleness-safe, cross-rank-identical — the safety belt to #1.

Cost: config-only. Complements #1; can run in the same cell.

×Ruled out

Delay-compensation / extrapolation / lead. Predicting g(θt) from the stale gradient (Nesterov-style lead) is forbidden by the async target: the anchor is a single slow node that can never lead the fast swarm. Admissible levers use the anchor as a lagging reference only.

Chasing rank r. EXP-20 showed reconstruction error is flat across r∈[77,102] — the codec is not coverage-starved, so raising r buys little and costs comm linearly. Do not lead with it.

7.2  The live EF-at-20/20 confirmation (re-run EXP-38)

Falsifiable prediction (PREDICTED, not yet observed)

EXP-38 (ef_powersgd, best params 0.9/1.0, at 20/20) was correctly configured and launched, but its Vast box was destroyed by a concurrent-session race before step 1 — there is no live result yet. Both analysts predict EF ignites earlier and/or harder than signed_ema's step-93 onset, because EF's residual δ = M − G_comp is a coherent, fixed-direction tangential force with no sign-cancellation brake (whereas the random half of signed_ema's sign-disagreements partially cancel across coordinates).

Predicted: ignition before step ~70 (vs ~93), val@25 in the 0.55–0.62 band, terminal collapse below the 0.63 floor, and the same per-refresh clipfrac spikes at steps 10/20/…/100. The EXP-26/27 history supports this: ef_powersgd at clip=1.0 already ignited a length explosion at 5/5, and damped EF (0.5/0.5) still ignited at step ~61 ("damping delays, not prevents"). Refuted if EF stays clean past step 93 or its val@25 ≥ 0.70.

Re-running EXP-38 cleanly is the cheap confirmation of the "carrier shapes the spiral, staleness sets the ceiling" thesis: if EF ignites earlier and its val is comparable to EXP-37, that jointly confirms staleness degrades the reconstruction equally across mergers while the merger family only sets the ignition timing.

8Caveats

  • n=1 draws. Every val number is a single run. Rollout nondeterminism is ~±0.024 (the dense draw band), so differences below that are within noise. The EXP-37 effects (0.088 drop, collapse to 0.4435) are far outside it; the cell-to-cell predictions in §7 should be read with the ±0.024 band in mind.
  • EXP-38 is predicted, not observed. The earlier-ignition claim for EF rests on the EXP-26/27 ignition history and mechanism, not a live 20/20 EF run. It is presented as a falsifiable prediction and must be confirmed by a clean re-run.
  • The structural confound limits EXP-37's standalone attribution. Because cadence and delay_K share the same firing gate, the 70–80% / 20–35% split is a theory-grounded attribution, not a measured decomposition. The §7 decoupling run (especially Cell B) is what converts it from estimate to measurement.
  • Comm-cost accounting. The reported bytes_ratio ≈ 0.0502 counts only the fast-path Y + amortized Q; the full-dense M broadcast is a known-uncounted term and is not the total communication cost.

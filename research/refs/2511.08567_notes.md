# Rigorous notes — "The Path Not Taken: RLVR Provably Learns Off the Principals" (arXiv 2511.08567)

> Zhu, Zhang, Huang, Su, Liu, Zhao, Fedorov, Pirsavash, Sha, Lee, Pan, Wang, Tian, Tai (Meta AI / UT Austin), v1, 11 Nov 2025. Local PDF `/Users/shamane/Documents/verl/research/refs/2511.08567.pdf` (29 pages: main 1–13, appendices A–G 14–29).
>
> **Read for:** the parameter-space mechanism of RLVR (Three-Gate Theory), and whether a *geometry-aware, off-principal, entropy-preserving masked* GRPO update could **surpass** dense in our pipeline-parallel comm-efficient setting (Qwen2.5-1.5B + GSM8K, per-(token,channel) activation masking p=0.9 at 7 PP boundaries; EXP-18 stale-anchor blend recovered dense-level reward but sits ~0.04 below in the plateau and lags in steps 1–15).
>
> **One-line thesis of the paper.** RLVR's apparent parameter *sparsity* is a surface artifact of a **model-conditioned optimization bias**: a KL leash (Gate I) keeps each step small, the *pretrained model's geometry* (Gate II) steers that small step **off the principal singular directions** into low-curvature / spectrum-preserving subspaces, and bf16 *precision* (Gate III) hides the resulting sub-ULP micro-updates in non-preferred regions so the bias *looks* like sparsity. SFT does the opposite (targets principal weights, distorts the spectrum) and even lags RLVR; therefore SFT-era PEFT (LoRA / sparse-FT, especially principal-targeted like PiSSA) is the wrong regime for RL.

## 0. Provenance & calibration to our setup (why this paper is directly load-bearing)

Their RL recipe (Appendix C.1) is **strikingly close to ours**, which is what makes the transfer credible rather than aspirational:
- **Algorithm:** DAPO with **β = 0** (no explicit reference-KL term) — *exactly* our no-KL GRPO. They deliberately use β=0 "to eliminate the confounding effect of the KL penalty, which can obscure the intrinsic parameter update dynamics."
- **Reward:** verifiable, **+1 if the extracted final answer is correct, −1 otherwise, no separate format score** (Su et al. verifier). Ours is `math_reward` / boxed-correctness — same family.
- **Optimizer:** AdamW, **constant lr = 1e-6**, β₁=0.9, β₂=0.999 — *identical to our EXP-17/18 shape* (lr=1e-6, n=8, train_batch=128, mini_batch=64).
- **Framework:** verl (Sheng et al.) + vLLM rollouts, FSDPv2, bf16 compute with **float32 optimizer state / reductions** (`reduce_type=float32, buffer_dtype=float32`) — our exact stack.
- **Models include DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and Qwen3-4B-Base** (close to our Qwen2.5-1.5B scale) and the spectral figures are shown specifically on **DS-Qwen-1.5B** (Fig. 16) — *our scale class*.

**Caveat up front (expanded in §6):** they study models that are *already RL-distilled / instruction-tuned* (DS-R1-Distill, Qwen3-Base/-Math), with no activation masking and full inter-stage communication. Our intervention (per-element activation masking at PP boundaries) is a *different perturbation of the same optimization* — the theory tells us what RL's geometry *wants* to do; whether masking helps or hurts that is the open question (§4).

## 1. The math, precisely

### Notation (their Sec. 3, App. F.1)
- Policy `π_θ(y|x)`, prompt `x∈𝒳`, sequence `y=(y₁,…,y_T)`, vocab size `N`.
- **RLVR objective (Eq. 3):** `max_θ E_{y~π_θ, x}[ R(x,y) − β·KL(π_θ(·|x) ‖ π_ref(·|x)) ]`, `β≥0` (β=0 ⇒ clip-only DAPO).
- **One-step PG surrogate (Eq. 4):** `L_PG(θ) = − E_{x, y~π_θ}[ A^t(x,y) · log π_θ(y|x) ]`, `A^t` a normalized (optionally KL-shaped) advantage. Updates are mini-batch, not strictly on-policy; the resulting error after a small step `Δθ` is `O(‖Δθ‖²)` (Lemma F.1 — the frozen-policy surrogate is second-order tight: `f(θ_t)=g(θ_t)`, `∇f(θ_t)=∇g(θ_t)`, remainder `≤ L/2·‖Δθ‖²`).
- **Importance-weighted advantage (App. F.1, Eq. 1):** `w_{i,t} = [π_θ(y_{i,t}|x,y_{<t}) / π_old(y_{i,t}|x,y_{<t})] · Â_{i,t} · 𝟙_clip`. (This is precisely our masked-vs-masked ratio object; note **where** the ratio lives — it matters for our anchor bug, see §4.3.)

### Gate I — KL Anchor (a one-step KL *leash* on the update), Sec. 3.1
**Claim:** even with β=0, online PG imposes a per-step KL bound, which bounds the weight move.

- **Prop. 3.1 (one-step policy-KL leash, proof App. F.2.1).** Let `q(·|x)` be full-support, `q̃_β ∝ q·exp(R/β)` the soft-regularized improvement oracle, `θ⁺` the M-projection of `q̃_β` onto the policy class. For a sufficiently small one-step update,
  `D_KL(π_{θ⁺} ‖ π_θ) ≤ (1+o(1))·D_KL(q̃_β ‖ π_θ)`, where `o(1)→0` as `D_KL(q̃_β‖π_θ)→0`.
  *Proof skeleton:* local Pythagorean identity for the M-projection (Lemma F.4) + quadratic expansion of policy-KL via Fisher `F(θ) = −E_{π_θ}[∇²log π_θ]` (Lemma F.5) + Hessian–Fisher proximity under small tilt `‖H_q̃ − F‖_op ≤ L√(2 D_KL)` (Lemma F.6, via Pinsker) + cubic-remainder control (Lemma F.7).
- **Even when β=0 (DAPO), ratio clipping `r_t∈[1−ε,1+ε]` imposes a KL bound `O(ε²)` in the small-step regime** (Lemma F.9, App. F.2.4): `D̄_KL(π_{θ⁺}‖π_θ) ≤ Ê[T(x)]·max{−log(1−ε), log(1+ε)} = O(ε)·Ê[T(x)]`, tightening to `O(ε²)` with mean-zero advantage. **Empirically the per-step KL is small but *non-zero and steadily increasing* over a DAPO run (Fig. 15) — the leash limits the *per-step* drift, it does NOT pin the policy to π_ref.** (This matters: it is *not* the "RL is provably trapped near init" claim — KL accumulates; cf. their cited "invisible leash" Wu et al. 2025.)
- **Prop. 3.2 (policy-KL leash ⇒ weight bound, proof App. F.2.2).** Assume `log π_θ ∈ C³`, Fisher `F(θ)⪰μI` on the update subspace. If `D_KL(π_{θ⁺}‖π_θ) ≤ K`, then for small `K`:
  `‖Δ‖_{F(θ)} ≜ √(Δᵀ F(θ)Δ) ≤ √(2K)(1+o(1))`, `‖Δ‖₂ ≤ √(2K/μ)(1+o(1))`, and **for any weight block `W∈θ`, `‖ΔW‖_F ≤ √(2K/μ)(1+o(1))`.**
  ⇒ **Take-away 1:** RL's update has a Fisher-norm budget; the per-step weight move is small *and curvature-weighted* (large in low-Fisher / flat directions, small in high-Fisher / sharp directions). This is the seed of Gate II.

### Gate II — Model Geometry (where the KL-bounded step *goes*), Sec. 3.2
**Claim:** Gate I bounds the move but not its *direction*; a well-pretrained model has a highly structured geometry (spectral statistics, high-curvature directions) that *steers* the KL-constrained step **off the principal directions into low-curvature, spectrum-preserving subspaces** — unlike a randomly initialized model. This is the *model-conditioned* part of the bias.

- **Layerwise norm bound from the leash (App. F.3, Eq. 7).** Partition `θ=(vec(W), θ_{−W})`; define the **layer-conditioned curvature** (Schur complement of the Fisher) `S_W := F_{W,W} − F_{W,−W} F_{−W,−W}^{-1} F_{−W,W} ⪰ 0`, and the per-layer KL budget `δ_W := ½ vec(ΔW)ᵀ S_W vec(ΔW) ≤ K`. With `μ_W := λ_min(S_W) > 0` on the update subspace, **`‖ΔW‖_F ≤ √(2δ_W/μ_W)`, `‖ΔW‖₂ ≤ √(2δ_W/μ_W)`** (Lemma F.10). *Interpretation: the move into a layer is bounded by that layer's KL budget over its local curvature; sharp (high-`μ_W`) directions admit tiny moves.*

Three geometric consequences of this conservative, curvature-weighted update (perturbation theory, Stewart 1998 / Wedin 1972):

1. **Limited subspace rotation (Theorem 3.3 / Wedin sin-Θ, App. F.11).** With singular-value gap `γ_k := σ_k(W₀) − σ_{k+1}(W₀)`, for any `k` with `γ_k>0`:
   `max( ‖sinΘ(U_k(W₀),U_k(W₊))‖₂, ‖sinΘ(V_k(W₀),V_k(W₊))‖₂ ) ≤ ‖ΔW‖₂/γ_k ≤ √(2δ_W/μ_W)/γ_k`.
   ⇒ the **top-k left/right singular subspaces rotate by at most `O(√(δ_W/μ_W)/γ_k)`** — small when the move is small and the spectral gap is moderate. (Corollary F.13: the *projector* `U_k U_kᵀ` moves by the same bound.)
2. **Singular-value stability (Corollary 3.4 / Weyl–Mirsky, Lemma F.12).** `|σ_k(W₊) − σ_k(W₀)| ≤ ‖ΔW‖₂ ≤ √(2δ_W/μ_W)`, and `Σ_i(σ_i(W₊)−σ_i(W₀))² ≤ ‖ΔW‖_F² ≤ 2δ_W/μ_W`. ⇒ **the magnitudes of the principal components are preserved.**
3. **Top-k energy / Ky-Fan preservation (Corollary 3.5).** With Ky-Fan k-norm `‖·‖_{(k)}=Σ_{i≤k}σ_i`: `| ‖W₊‖_{(k)} − ‖W₀‖_{(k)} | ≤ Σ_{i≤k}|σ_i(W₊)−σ_i(W₀)| ≤ k·‖ΔW‖₂ ≤ k√(2δ_W/μ_W)`. ⇒ **cumulative energy of the top-k components is stable.**

⇒ **Take-away 2:** under the KL leash, RL **preserves the model's weight structure rather than destroying it**, naturally favoring **low-curvature directions** of the optimization landscape — which avoids dramatic behavior change. *Because directly measuring curvature on long-CoT LRMs is prohibitive, they use **principal weights** (Liu et al. 2025c) as a curvature proxy (Sec. 4.2).*

### Gate III — Precision as a lens, Sec. 3.3
**Claim:** bf16 storage (7 mantissa bits) acts as a *realization filter*, not a cause.
- **Corollary 3.6 (magnitude-dependent realization threshold).** A stored weight `W_{ij}` changes at a step iff `|ΔW_{ij}| ≳ ½ ULP_bf16(W_{ij})`. Since `ULP_bf16(x)/|x| ∈ (2⁻⁸,2⁻⁷] = (0.195%, 0.781%]` (Lemma E.2), **the minimal *relative* update that registers is ≈0.2–0.39%**; larger-magnitude weights need a larger absolute step to register.
- Because RL consistently routes **sub-ULP** micro-updates into non-preferred (off-principal, low-magnitude) regions, those stored values don't change ⇒ **apparent sparsity**. Confirmed by raising lr to push sub-ULP updates over threshold ⇒ apparent sparsity "largely disappears" (Sec. 2.3), and by the fact that **optimizer states & reductions are float32 in verl** — so sparsity *cannot* be a precision (zero-gradient) effect; it requires the optimization bias. Precision is an **amplifier**, not the cause (their "Aha Finding", p.6; sparsity probe is bf16-aware, Def. 2.1–2.2 / App. E).

### Parameter-space metrics (Sec. 4.1–4.2) — *exactly what to measure*
Comparing base weights `W₀` vs fine-tuned `W₊` per 2D matrix:

- **Spectrum drift (NSS).** `NSS(W) := ‖σ(W₊) − σ(W₀)‖₂ / ‖σ(W₀)‖₂` — normalized ℓ₂ shift of the **full singular-value vector**. (SVD of each weight matrix; compare sorted singular values.)
  - *Empirical (Fig. 4 Qwen3-8B, Fig. 16 **DS-Qwen-1.5B**):* RLVR NSS ≈ **1e-5–6e-5** per layer; **SFT NSS ≈ 1e-1** (≈ 3–4 orders of magnitude larger). RLVR singular-value profiles are "*even nearly identical*" to base.
- **Principal-subspace rotation (principal angles).** For the **top-k** left/right singular subspaces: `cosθ_i(U) := σ_i(U_{0,k}ᵀ U_{+,k})`, `cosθ_i(V) := σ_i(V_{0,k}ᵀ V_{+,k})` (singular values of the cross-Gram of the top-k bases; θ=0 ⇒ no rotation, θ=90° ⇒ fully rotated). Reported as per-k curves and **max principal angle per layer**.
  - *Empirical (Fig. 4 left/right, Fig. 8 agents, Fig. 18 RLHF):* RLVR top-k angles are **small and stable**; SFT rotates "substantially larger." Fig. 1c: "RL rotates less → preserves spectrum"; SFT's rotation grows with k.
- **Off-principal update alignment (overlap of update-mask vs principal-mask).** Build the bf16-aware **update mask** `M_ℓ^{(r)} := 𝟙[ W_ℓ^{(r)} ≉_η W_ℓ^0 ]` (positions that changed under RL, run r). Build the **principal mask** `M_princ^{(k)} := Top_α(s_{ij}^{(k)})`, `s_{ij}^{(k)} = |W₀^{(k)}(i,j)|` where `W₀^{(k)}` is the **rank-k SVD reconstruction** of `W₀` (i.e. the highest-magnitude entries of the principal-pathway weights, the curvature proxy of Liu et al. 2025c, validated because perturbing them sharply degrades reasoning). Also the **low-magnitude mask** `M_low := Bottom_α(|W₀|)`. **Overlap(M●, M) := |M● ∩ M| / |M|**, compared to the random/Bernoulli baseline `α` (the density of M●).
  - *Empirical (Fig. 5, Fig. 7):* RL update mask has **sub-random overlap with M_princ** (avoids principal weights) and **super-random overlap with M_low** (prefers low-magnitude / low-resistance weights). The residual overlap with principal weights is almost entirely the `M_princ ∩ M_low` intersection (weights both principal *and* low-magnitude); after excluding it (`M_princ ∩ M_low^c`), overlap "drops significantly." **Take-away: RL updates land off the high-curvature principal pathway.**
- **Cross-run stability (Jaccard, Table 2 / Fig. 12).** Mean off-diagonal Jaccard of update masks across 5 independent DS-Qwen-1.5B runs (different data + RL variant) ≈ **0.55–0.60 vs random baseline ≈ 0.37–0.47** ⇒ a *shared, deterministic footprint* — the bias is model-conditioned, not run-noise.

### Causal isolation of Gate II (Sec. 4.3, intervention details App. D) — *the strongest evidence*
They **scramble the pretrained geometry** with **function-preserving orthogonal transforms** and show the bias *vanishes*:
- **ROTATE (Prop. D.1, exact invariance).** Right-multiply V/O weights by block-diagonal Haar/Hadamard orthogonal `R`: `W_v' = W_v R_kv`, `W_o' = W_o R_q`. Output is *bit-for-bit unchanged* (`out' = Attn(Q,K,VR_kv)(W_o R_q)ᵀ = out`), but the singular *basis* is rotated.
- **PERMUTE.** Head/coordinate permutation `P_kv`, `P_q` on Q/K/V/O — relabels which head carries which subspace, function unchanged.
- *Result (Fig. 6):* on intervened layers (20: ROTATE+PERMUTE; 25: ROTATE) the **update-mask overlap collapses to the random baseline**, while untouched layers stay high. **ROTATE+PERMUTE eliminates the update bias; ROTATE alone removes it only for V/O.** ⇒ *strong causal evidence that the pretrained geometry IS the source of the optimization bias* — function-preserving rotations abolish update locality.

**"Provably" vs empirical, summarized.**
- **Provably shown (small-step / `C³` / Fisher-`⪰μI` assumptions):** the KL leash (Prop. 3.1, F.9), the weight-norm budget (Prop. 3.2, F.10), bounded subspace rotation (Thm. 3.3 / Cor. F.13), singular-value & Ky-Fan stability (Cor. 3.4–3.5), the bf16 realization threshold (Cor. 3.6, App. E). **These are perturbation-theory *upper bounds* parameterized by the per-layer KL budget `δ_W` and curvature `μ_W` — they bound how much RL *can* drift, given a small KL step.** They do **not** prove RL *must* go off-principal — that "steering" is the Gate-II *hypothesis*, supported empirically + causally (ROTATE/PERMUTE).
- **Empirical (the "learns off the principals" thesis itself):** spectrum preservation (NSS, Fig. 4/16/17), small principal rotation, sub-random principal-weight overlap (Fig. 5/7), cross-run Jaccard (Fig. 12), the causal interventions (Fig. 6), and the SFT-PEFT case studies (Sec. 5, Fig. 9/10/11, Tables 4/5).

## 2. What RLVR actually changes in the weight matrices vs SFT

| | **RLVR (off-principal regime)** | **SFT (principal-targeting regime)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Where updates land** | Off the top-k principal singular directions; on low-magnitude / low-curvature ("low-resistance") weights (Fig. 5: sub-random `M_princ` overlap, super-random `M_low` overlap) | On principal weights (high-magnitude, high-curvature) — Mukherjee 2025, Liu 2025c |
| **Spectrum (singular values)** | **Preserved** — NSS ≈1e-5; profiles nearly identical to base (Fig. 4/16) | **Distorted** — NSS ≈1e-1 (3–4 orders larger) |
| **Top-k subspace rotation** | Small / stable principal angles (Thm. 3.3 bound; Fig. 4 right) | Substantially larger rotation |
| **Apparent sparsity (bf16)** | High (36–92%, Table 1) — micro-updates fall sub-ULP off-principal | Low (0.6–18.8%) — large principal moves register |
| **Geometry dependence** | *Depends on* pretrained geometry; ROTATE/PERMUTE abolishes the bias (Fig. 6) | Externally-taught target; less geometry-conditioned |

**Why SFT *lags* RLVR (Sec. 5.1, Fig. 9, Tables 4/5).** They run **sparse RL fine-tuning with fixed pretrained masks** to probe which weights are "touchable" for RL:
- **`M_princ` (principal-only, top-50%):** the **worst** trajectory — KL drift rises slowly, training is throttled, final accuracy degraded (Table 4: avg 53.82 vs dense 57.44). *Forcing updates onto principal weights blocks RL's optimization.*
- **`M_low ∪ M_princ^c` ("safe mask", non-principal + low-magnitude, ~74% of params):** **most closely tracks the dense RLVR KL curve and reaches comparable final accuracy**, and on individual metrics actually **edges dense** (Table 4: **Math500 85.20 vs dense 84.20**; Table 5 500-step: **AIME24 40.30 vs dense 38.28**). *RL's effective subspace is the off-principal, low-magnitude one — and freezing the principal weights does not hurt (sometimes helps).*
- ⇒ **"Principal-targeted directions favored in SFT are *ineffective* for RL."** SFT's machinery (push hard along principal/teacher directions, distort the spectrum) is exactly what RL's KL-leashed geometry avoids; on RL's *own* objective, principal-heavy updates underperform the off-principal ones — hence SFT "even lags RLVR" (abstract; also Chu 2025 "SFT memorizes, RL generalizes", Huan 2025).

**Why SFT-era PEFT (LoRA / sparse-FT) is the wrong regime (Sec. 5.2, Fig. 10/11).**
- **Sparse-FT:** restricting updates to principal weights (an SFT prior) "yields the weakest optimization trajectory and markedly degrades performance." Updating *non-principal, low-magnitude* weights instead "closely tracks the dense RLVR trajectory."
- **LoRA:** low-rank adapters with frozen base *can* match full-parameter RL (Schulman & TML 2025) — their explanation: full-param RL updates are *already* low-rank-ish off-principal small spectral changes, and freezing the base regularizes away principal-direction moves; with appropriately scaled lr the limited adapter capacity suffices *in the short run*.
- **PiSSA (principal-targeted LoRA):** initializes the adapter on the **top-r principal** singular directions — *misaligned* with RL's off-principal dynamics. Empirically (Fig. 10 DS-Qwen-1.5B DeepMath-103K, ranks {8,32,64}, lr {1e-4,5e-5,1e-5}; Fig. 11 Llama-3.2-3B MATH500) **PiSSA gives no gain over LoRA and, at the higher lr needed to match full-param performance, forces principal-direction updates → distorts the spectrum → destabilizes / collapses early.** ⇒ *"Your old PEFT tricks may not work"* (Take-away 4): RL needs **RL-native, geometry-aware** PEFT.

## 3. The KL-anchor / entropy / curvature connection (Gate I × Gate II)

The paper does **not** measure token/sequence entropy directly (a gap we can exploit — §5), but the chain Gate I → Gate II is *implicitly* an entropy-preservation argument, and it is worth making explicit because **our whole project "is playing with entropy/geometry"**:

1. **Gate I bounds policy-KL per step (Prop. 3.1 / F.9).** Policy-KL is, to second order, a Fisher-metric distance: `D_KL(π_{θ+Δ}‖π_θ) = ½ Δᵀ F(θ) Δ + O(‖Δ‖³)` (Lemma F.5). A small KL step is a small *Fisher-norm* move.
2. **The Fisher = the metric of the output distribution.** Moving little in Fisher-norm ⇒ the *output distribution* (hence its **entropy** and its support) changes little per step. A KL-leashed update is, by construction, a *near-entropy-preserving* update — it cannot collapse `π_θ` onto a spike in one step (that would be a large KL/Fisher move). This is the formal counterpart to the intuition "the real enemy is entropy collapse": **RL's leash is an implicit brake on entropy collapse.**
3. **Curvature = Fisher (≈ Hessian of `−log π`, Lemma F.5/F.6).** `F(θ) = −E_{π_θ}[∇²log π_θ]`, and `H_q̃(θ) = (1+O(√κ)) F(θ)` near the policy (Lemma F.6). High-Fisher (high-curvature) directions are *exactly* the directions in which the output distribution (and entropy) is most sensitive. The per-layer budget `δ_W = ½ vec(ΔW)ᵀ S_W vec(ΔW) ≤ K` (App. F.3) means **the update is automatically tiny in high-curvature directions and only sizeable in low-curvature / flat ones** — i.e. it preserves the entropy-sensitive (principal) structure and does its work in the entropy-insensitive (off-principal) tail.
4. **Off-principal = spectrum-preserving = entropy-preserving.** The principal singular directions carry the dominant input→output gain; rotating/scaling them is what most changes the function and its output entropy. RL leaving them alone (small principal rotation, NSS≈1e-5) ⇒ the high-curvature, high-entropy-leverage structure is preserved; learning happens in the low-curvature complement.

**Synthesis for us:** *low-curvature ⇔ off-principal ⇔ spectrum-preserving ⇔ entropy-preserving* are, in this paper's geometry, **the same condition viewed four ways**. A correction that keeps the masked GRPO update in that subspace is simultaneously off-principal *and* entropy-preserving. This is the hinge for §4–§5.

## 4. Implications for OUR setting (the load-bearing section)

Recap of our state: per-(token,channel) activation masking p=0.9 at the 7 PP-boundary blocks `[3,7,11,15,18,21,24]`, rescale `h⊙mask/(1−p)` ON (unbiases the *activation*, not the gradient), `mask_recompute=ON`. The masked actor gradient is `g_mask = g_true + b + ξ` (`b`=systematic curvature-aligned bias from RMSNorm `1/RMS` backward compounding over 7 boundaries; `ξ`=zero-mean masking noise). EXP-18's winner: **C4/C5 = masked + convex blend `G_corr=(1−η)G_mask + η·scale·M_anchor`** toward a **clean** (ratio≡1) unmasked policy gradient from a `delay_K=5`-stale clone, `cadence=5`, `clean_cadence=0`. Result: reward 0.13→0.81–0.84 (vs ~0.13 floor), **endpoint |Δ|=0.005 (η=0.7) / 0.027 (η=0.9), slope matches dense, plateau (steps 20–50) within tol ~0.036**, but **whole-trajectory mean|Δ|≈0.07–0.077 > 0.05 driven by the steps 1–15 anchor-warmup lag** (first anchor fires at step 5; dense climbs 0.13→0.75 by step 10 before the correction can engage). The persistent plateau gap is small (~0.03–0.04) and *below* dense.

### 4.1 Does activation masking at PP boundaries bias the GRPO update TOWARD or OFF the principal directions of the affected weight matrices?

**This is the central question and the paper gives us the language to answer it. The honest answer: it depends on whether the masked-removed activation energy lives in the principal or off-principal input subspace of the boundary matrices — and that is *measurable* (§4.4).** Argue both ways:

**Argument that masking biases the update OFF-principal (the favorable case).**
- For a linear/MLP block `Y = W·h`, the parameter gradient is `∂L/∂W = δ_Y · hᵀ` (outer product of upstream grad `δ_Y` and input activation `h`). The **right singular structure of `∂L/∂W` is inherited from the input activation `h`**. Per-channel masking zeroes a random 90% of the channels of `h` each step (rescaled). Over the mini-batch this **whitens / flattens the input correlation** that the gradient sees: the high-variance (principal) input channels — which dominate `h hᵀ` and hence the principal directions of the accumulated `∂L/∂W` — are *down-weighted in expectation no more than the tail*, but their *coherent contribution is destroyed* by the random per-token masking (the cross-token, cross-channel correlations that build the top singular direction are broken). Net: **masking suppresses the principal (high-coherence) component of the gradient relative to the off-principal tail** — exactly the EXP-16/18 *orthogonality* finding (`cos(G_mask, g_true)≈0`, measured live in EXP-18 C1: cos ∈ {−0.085,−0.161,0.046,−0.001,…}). An (near-)orthogonal-to-`g_true` `G_mask` is, by definition, a gradient that has lost `g_true`'s dominant (principal-aligned) direction and retained a diffuse off-principal remainder. **If `g_true`'s dominant direction is principal-aligned, masking pushes the update off-principal — i.e. masking does *for free* what Gate II says RL wants.**
- The RMSNorm `1/RMS` backward at each boundary further reweights by inverse activation scale, *amplifying low-magnitude / low-RMS directions* — which (Fig. 5) are precisely the `M_low` directions RL prefers. This is a second mechanism pushing masked gradients toward the low-magnitude, off-principal regime.

**Argument that masking biases the update TOWARD-principal / arbitrary (the unfavorable case).**
- The bias `b` is **curvature-aligned** (it's a delta-method / second-order term — `b ~ ½ Σ_c Var(mask_c)·∂²L/∂h_c²`-flavored). Curvature-aligned means `b` has **large components in the high-Fisher / high-curvature = principal directions** (that is what "curvature-aligned" means in this paper's geometry). So `b` is *not* a benign off-principal nudge — it is a **systematic perturbation concentrated where the model is most sensitive**, i.e. it can *rotate or distort the principal subspace* (the opposite of what we want). Left uncorrected, `b` accumulates and "flips the ascent projection" (our SUMMARY) — consistent with a principal-distorting drift, the SFT-like failure mode.
- Masking is applied at **fixed boundaries**, not uniformly — so it does not whiten the network's geometry the way a function-preserving ROTATE does; it injects a *structured, layer-localized* perturbation that the network's downstream layers see as off-distribution. Whether the resulting gradient bias lands off- or on-principal of *each affected matrix* is **not determined a priori** — it depends on the alignment between the masked-removed activation energy and that matrix's input principal subspace.

**What would determine it (and how to settle it empirically — see §5/§4.4):** measure, on the 7 boundary matrices (and a few interior ones), (a) `NSS(W_masked-run) vs NSS(W_dense-run)` — does the masked run *preserve* the spectrum like RL should, or *distort* it like SFT? (b) the principal-subspace rotation (sin-Θ) of masked vs dense; (c) the **projection of `G_mask` and of `b̂` onto the top-k vs off-principal subspace of the boundary matrix's gradient** — is the masking bias concentrated in the principal block (bad, SFT-like) or the tail (benign)? The orthogonality finding (`cos(G_mask,g_true)≈0`) is *suggestive that masking destroys the principal-aligned part of `g_true`* but does **not** tell us whether `b` itself is principal-concentrated. **This is the single most important new measurement** (H1, §5).

### 4.2 Could a geometry-aware masked + correction scheme SURPASS dense (not just match)? — the mechanism

**Yes, plausibly — and the paper hands us two distinct mechanisms.** The key reframe: *dense is not the optimum of the RL geometry — dense is just the full-fidelity gradient. The paper shows RL's geometry actively prefers an off-principal, spectrum-preserving subspace, and that an explicit off-principal mask (`M_low ∪ M_princ^c`) can **edge dense** on RL's own objective (Table 4 Math500, Table 5 AIME24).* Masking is a *cheap, implicit* version of that mask. So:

**Mechanism A — masking as a free off-principal projector + correction as the de-biaser (surpass via better generalization/pass@K).**
The EXP-18 correction currently blends toward the *full* stale true gradient `M_anchor`, which re-injects `g_true`'s **principal-aligned** component (the part masking removed). That *recovers* dense (good) but also re-imports the principal-direction moves the paper says RL doesn't need and that *destabilize* training (PiSSA story). A **geometry-aware** correction would instead blend toward the **off-principal projection** of the stale true gradient: `M_anchor^⊥ := M_anchor − P_{princ}(M_anchor)` where `P_{princ}` projects onto the **top-k right/left singular subspace of the boundary matrix's *base* weights `W₀`** (the principal mask). The update then:
- supplies the missing *off-principal* ascent direction (de-biases `b`'s off-principal part, fixing the floor — like C4),
- *deliberately withholds* the principal-direction correction → keeps NSS≈0, keeps principal rotation small, keeps entropy high,
- so it learns in RL's preferred subspace **more purely than dense** (which, being the full gradient, does spend some budget on principal directions).
**Why this could surpass:** the paper's own ablation (Table 4/5) shows the off-principal-restricted RL update *edges* the full (dense) update on held-out math (Math500, AIME24) — better generalization, presumably via less spectral distortion / less overfitting of the principal pathway. If masking + off-principal correction realizes that same restriction *at p=0.9 comm savings*, it could land **at or slightly above dense on val/pass@K** while strictly cheaper. **Metric:** val/pass@K *up*, NSS and principal-rotation of the masked-run weights *≤* dense's, plateau reward `≥` dense.

**Mechanism B — masking as an entropy-preserving regularizer (surpass via avoiding entropy collapse).**
Per §3, low-KL/low-Fisher steps preserve entropy; entropy collapse is the enemy of pass@K (the latent skill RL elicits lives in the high-entropy base distribution). Per-token/per-channel masking injects **structured stochasticity into the forward pass** — a dropout-like perturbation that (i) keeps the policy from sharpening too fast (acts like an entropy bonus without an explicit entropy term, which we don't have), and (ii) the rescale keeps the activation unbiased so it's a *variance* injection, not a *mean* shift. **Why this could surpass dense:** dense no-KL no-entropy GRPO is *known* to entropy-collapse on easy data (it over-exploits the elicited mode); a masked update that holds entropy higher for longer could keep exploring → find higher-reward modes / better pass@K before collapsing. **The correction's job is then narrow:** remove only the *destructive* part of `b` (the principal-distorting drift, §4.1 unfavorable case) while *keeping* the benign entropy-preserving variance. **Metric:** token/sequence entropy of the masked policy *stays above* dense's at matched steps, **and** pass@K (k=8,16) and val end *above* dense.

**The unifying claim:** dense follows `g_true` faithfully but `g_true` (for no-KL GRPO on easy data) over-invests in principal directions and entropy-collapses; a masked+geometry-aware-correction update can be a *strictly better-conditioned* RL step — off-principal by construction, entropy-preserving by construction, de-biased only where it must be. That is the paper's "RL-native, geometry-aware learning algorithm" (their Sec. 5 call-to-action) realized inside our comm-efficient constraint. **It is not guaranteed — but it is the right place to look, and it is testable.**

### 4.3 Is EXP-18's persistent ~0.04 below-dense plateau gap a spectral / principal-rotation artifact of the stale-anchor blend?

**Plausible, and it splits into two sub-effects we can separate.** The curve data (`curves.json`) shows the gap is *not* the warmup (that's steps 1–15, mean|Δ|≈0.147); the *plateau* gap (steps 20–50) is the small residual ~0.03–0.04. Three candidate causes, in order of how I'd bet:

1. **(Most likely) Staleness-induced principal-rotation error in the blended direction.** `M_anchor ≈ g_true(θ_{t−5})`, and the blend replaces 70–90% of `G_mask` with it. Over 5 steps the **principal subspace of the policy gradient rotates** (Thm. 3.3 says the *weight* subspace rotates slowly, but the *gradient* direction on the steep part can rotate fast — dense climbs 0.13→0.75 in 10 steps). So the blended update follows a **5-step-stale principal direction**, slightly mis-aligned with the current one → a small systematic lag that persists as long as the policy keeps moving. On the plateau the policy moves *less* (reward flat) → rotation slows → gap shrinks but doesn't vanish (consistent with ~0.04). **This is a principal-rotation artifact of staleness, not of masking.**
2. **Residual `b` in the off-principal tail.** The clean-PG anchor supplies `g_true`'s direction but the blend keeps `(1−η)` of `G_mask`, which still carries `(1−η)·b`. A small uncorrected `b` ⇒ a small persistent bias ⇒ a small plateau gap. (η=0.9 vs 0.7 changed the *plateau* (tighter) but not the warmup — consistent with `b` being the plateau driver and warmup being cadence-bound.)
3. **(Less likely) Spectral distortion from the blend itself.** If the blended update distorts the boundary matrices' spectrum (NSS grows vs dense), that's a genuine geometry artifact and would *hurt generalization* — the worse case.

**How to tell (decisive diagnostics):**
- **Vary `delay_K ∈ {1,2,5}` (within the staleness constraint, never 0) and a gradient-extrapolation anchor (C4-axis: `M̂ = G_anchor(t) + [G_anchor(t)−G_anchor(t−cadence)]`).** If the plateau gap *shrinks with smaller `delay_K`* or with extrapolation → cause (1) confirmed (staleness/rotation). This is the cleanest single test.
- **Log `cos(M_anchor, g_true_fresh)`** by occasionally (cheaply, off the optimizer path) computing a fresh unmasked grad on a held-out micro-batch and dotting against the stale `M_anchor`. A cos<1 that *correlates with the plateau gap* ⇒ cause (1).
- **Log `NSS` and top-k principal rotation of the masked-run boundary matrices vs the dense-run's, at steps 25/50.** If masked-run NSS ≈ dense-run NSS and rotation ≈ dense → rule out cause (3); the gap is staleness/`b`, recoverable. If masked-run NSS *exceeds* dense → cause (3), a real geometry problem.
- **Sweep η→1 (pure stale-grad descent).** If the plateau gap *vanishes* at η=1 → cause (2) (residual `(1−η)b`); if it *persists* at η=1 → cause (1) (the stale direction itself is the limiter).

My prior: the plateau gap is dominantly **(1) staleness rotation + (2) residual `b`**, both *recoverable* (smaller `delay_K` / extrapolation / higher η), **not (3) a fundamental spectral artifact** — because C4/C5 *match dense's slope and endpoint*, which would be impossible if the update were spectrum-distorting (we'd see divergence, not a flat 0.04 offset). **This is good news for surpassing dense:** the gap is an estimator-quality problem, not a geometry dead-end.

### 4.4 Concrete metrics to LOG on Qwen2.5-1.5B during a ≤50-step run (cheap)

All computable from weights/grads we already materialize at the 7 boundary matrices (and a 8th interior control). Compute **only on the targeted 2D matrices** (~196, but a representative ~14: 2 per boundary), in **float32**, **every 5 steps** (or only at steps {1,10,25,50}) to keep cost negligible. SVD of a 1536×1536 (or 1536×8960 MLP) matrix is ~ms on H100; doing ~14 of them every 5 steps is sub-second/step amortized.

**Geometry (the paper's metrics, ported):**
1. **Spectrum drift `NSS_ℓ(t) = ‖σ(W_ℓ(t)) − σ(W_ℓ(0))‖₂ / ‖σ(W_ℓ(0))‖₂`** per boundary matrix `ℓ`, for the masked-run and a cached dense-run. *Surpass-signal: masked NSS ≤ dense NSS (more spectrum-preserving).* (Full thin SVD of `W_ℓ` — we already have `compute_basis` in `spectral_filter.py`.)
2. **Principal-subspace rotation `sinΘ_k = ‖I − U_{0,k}ᵀ U_{t,k}‖`** (or `1 − mean_i cosθ_i`), top-k (k=32 or 64) left & right singular subspaces, masked vs dense. *Surpass-signal: masked rotation ≤ dense.*
3. **Off-principal gradient alignment.** Per boundary matrix, project the *update* (or `G_mask`/`G_corr`) onto the base-weight principal subspace: `ρ_princ = ‖P_{U0,k} ΔW P_{V0,k}‖_F / ‖ΔW‖_F` (fraction of update energy in the principal block). *Surpass-signal: masked+correction `ρ_princ` < dense `ρ_princ` (more off-principal) while reward ≥ dense.* This directly tests §4.1.
4. **Live `cos(G_mask, M_anchor)` and `cos(G_mask, g_true)`** (already logged in EXP-18) — the orthogonality monitor; extend with `cos(G_corr, g_true)` to confirm the correction restores alignment.
5. **`b̂` principal fraction.** Using a C3-style stale masked-minus-unmasked `b̂ = G_anchor^mask − G_anchor`, log `‖P_princ b̂‖/‖b̂‖` — is the masking bias principal-concentrated (bad) or tail (benign)? Settles §4.1 directly.

**Entropy (the metric the paper is *missing* — our edge):**
6. **Token entropy `H_tok(t) = −Σ_v π_θ(v|·) log π_θ(v|·)`** averaged over response tokens, per step (free — it's a reduction over the logits we already compute in the forward). **Log for masked, masked+correction, and dense.** *Surpass-signal: masked policy entropy stays **above** dense at matched steps (entropy-preservation).*
7. **Sequence-level entropy proxy:** mean per-sequence `−log π_θ(y|x)/|y|` (mean token NLL of own samples) and its **dispersion across the n=8 rollouts** (rollout diversity). Rising NLL + falling dispersion = entropy collapse. *Surpass-signal: masked holds higher dispersion longer.*
8. **`pg_clipfrac` and the IS-ratio histogram** (already logged) — collapse shows up as clipfrac→0 with a sharpening ratio.

**Generalization / capability (the actual prize):**
9. **GSM8K val score** every K steps (we have it) — the headline.
10. **pass@K (K∈{1,8,16}) on a fixed 200-prompt GSM8K val slice**, masked vs dense vs *base (zero-RL)*. The paper's thesis + our SUMMARY both say RL mostly *elicits* (base pass@K ≈ RL pass@1). *Surpass-signal: masked+correction pass@8/16 ≥ dense's — i.e. it preserves more of the base's latent breadth.* Cheap: 8–16 vLLM samples on 200 prompts at temp 0.7, ~1–2 min on the box, every 10 steps or at {1,25,50}.

**Minimal viable logging set (if budget-constrained):** {1 (NSS), 3 (off-principal ρ), 6 (token entropy), 9 (val), 10 (pass@8)} on masked+correction vs a cached dense run, at steps {1,10,25,50}. That's enough to test "off-principal + entropy-preserving ⇒ ≥ dense."

## 5. Testable hypotheses / levers for the planning agent

Each respects the PP comm-efficient constraints (mask p=0.9 ON; `clean_cadence=0`; anchor `delay_K=5` stale, `cadence=5`; correction supplies the missing component, never reweights `G_mask` — HARD CONSTRAINTS 1–3 of `NEXT_RESEARCH.md`). All build on the *working* C4/C5 clean-PG-anchor blend (`exp/18-anchorcleangrad-c5d5`).

### H1 — **Measure-first: is masking already off-principal?** (the prerequisite diagnostic, not yet a lever)
- **Mechanism:** instrument a single masked+C4 run and a cached dense run with metrics §4.4-{1,2,3,5}. Compute `NSS`, top-k rotation, off-principal update fraction `ρ_princ`, and `b̂` principal fraction on the 7 boundary matrices.
- **Why it gates everything:** §4.1 has two opposite predictions; this picks one. If masked-run NSS ≤ dense and `ρ_princ` < dense → masking is a *free off-principal projector* (Mechanism A is live; pursue H2/H3). If masked-run NSS > dense or `b̂` is principal-concentrated → masking is *principal-distorting* and the correction must explicitly kill the principal part of `b` (pursue H4).
- **Surpass relevance:** indirect — it tells us *which* surpass mechanism is available.
- **Confirm/refute metric:** the sign of `(NSS_masked − NSS_dense)` and `(ρ_princ,masked − ρ_princ,dense)` at steps 25/50.

### H2 — **Off-principal correction (geometry-aware blend): blend toward `M_anchor^⊥`, not `M_anchor`.** ★ ties to off-principal geometry ★
- **Mechanism:** `G_corr = (1−η)G_mask + η·scale·(M_anchor − P_{princ}(M_anchor))`, where `P_{princ}` projects onto the top-k (k≈32) left/right singular subspace of the **boundary matrix's base weights `W₀`** (the principal mask `M_princ`, computed once at init — *no per-step SVD of the anchor*, cheap). Supply only the *off-principal* part of the stale true gradient; leave the principal block at `(1−η)G_mask` (or zero it too).
- **Why it could surpass dense:** realizes Mechanism A (§4.2). The paper's Table 4/5 shows off-principal-restricted RL *edges* dense on Math500/AIME24. This injects the off-principal ascent direction (fixes the floor) while *withholding* principal-direction moves → smaller NSS, smaller rotation, higher entropy than dense → better generalization/pass@K.
- **PP-compatible:** uses only the stale anchor; `P_{princ}` is a fixed base-weight projection (no extra comm); supplies the missing off-principal component (Constraint 3 ✓).
- **Confirm/refute:** plateau reward `≥` dense **and** `ρ_princ` < dense **and** GSM8K val / pass@8 `≥` dense. Refuted if withholding the principal correction reintroduces the floor (⇒ the principal part of `g_true` *is* needed here, contra the paper's easy-data claim).

### H3 — **Entropy-preserving masked update: keep masking's variance, de-bias only the mean.** ★ ties to entropy preservation ★
- **Mechanism:** keep p=0.9 masking as a *forward* stochastic regularizer (the variance injection), but (a) log token entropy §4.4-6, and (b) tune the correction strength `η`/cadence to the point where the **masked policy entropy stays a target margin above dense** while reward still tracks. Optionally add an explicit small entropy *floor* via a mask-rate schedule (raise p when entropy drops too fast). No explicit entropy-bonus term needed (we don't have one) — masking *is* the entropy bonus.
- **Why it could surpass dense:** dense no-KL no-entropy GRPO entropy-collapses on easy GSM8K (over-exploits the elicited mode); a higher-entropy masked policy keeps exploring → higher pass@K / val before collapse (§4.2 Mechanism B). The paper's Gate I formalizes that low-KL steps preserve entropy (§3).
- **PP-compatible:** masking is the comm-saver itself; the only knob is `η`/`p` schedule (Constraint-safe).
- **Confirm/refute:** token entropy(masked) > token entropy(dense) at steps 25/50 **and** pass@8/16 and val end *above* dense. Refuted if higher entropy comes with *lower* reward (exploration without payoff) — then masking-entropy is noise, not useful exploration.

### H4 — **Explicit principal-`b` removal (if H1 says masking distorts the spectrum).**
- **Mechanism:** C3-style stale `b̂ = G_anchor^mask − G_anchor` at `θ_{t−5}` (both passes from the stale clone), but **subtract only its principal projection**: `G_corr = G_mask − λ·P_{princ}(b̂)` (+ the C4 off-principal blend for the floor). Kills the principal-distorting drift while keeping the benign off-principal masking effect.
- **Why it could surpass dense:** if §4.1's *unfavorable* case holds (masking biases toward principal), `b`'s principal part is what would distort the spectrum SFT-style; removing exactly that part restores RL's preferred geometry → match/exceed dense with less distortion than dense itself.
- **PP-compatible:** second stale pass is a reference pass (Constraint 1 ✓), `delay_K=5` (✓), supplies `−P_princ(b̂)` (Constraint 3 ✓). Cost: doubles the anchor pass (cadence=5 ⇒ still ~1 extra unmasked pass / 5 steps).
- **Confirm/refute:** `NSS_masked` drops to ≤ dense after enabling; plateau gap closes; val ≥ dense.

### H5 — **Staleness-rotation fix: gradient extrapolation of the anchor (close the plateau gap).**
- **Mechanism:** replace `M_anchor` (heavy EMA of stale grads) with a **first-order extrapolation across two refreshes**: `M̂ = G_anchor(t) + [G_anchor(t) − G_anchor(t−cadence)]` (predict the current-step gradient from the last two stale ones), then blend. Or sweep `delay_K∈{1,2,5}` and `beta_anc∈{0,0.5}`.
- **Why it matters for surpass (not just match):** §4.3 cause (1) — the ~0.04 plateau gap is likely the 5-step-stale principal-rotation lag. Extrapolation cancels the first-order rotation → tighter tracking → removes the *only* thing keeping us below dense in the plateau, opening room for H2/H3 to push *above*.
- **PP-compatible:** uses only stale anchors (no fresh full gradient); extrapolation is arithmetic on existing signals.
- **Confirm/refute:** plateau (steps 20–50) mean|Δ| drops below ~0.02 and/or smaller `delay_K` monotonically shrinks the gap ⇒ staleness-rotation confirmed and fixed.

### H6 — **Warmup fix (orthogonal to surpass, fixes the strict mean|Δ| miss): fire the anchor sooner.**
- **Mechanism:** anchor `cadence=1` (or a 5-step warmup window at cadence=1 then back to 5) so the correction engages from step 1 and can match dense's steep 0.13→0.75 early climb. (Outside the plan's pinned cadence=5, so a *follow-on* axis — flagged because it is the *sole* reason C4/C5 miss the whole-trajectory `mean|Δ|≤0.05` despite matching endpoint+slope+plateau.)
- **Why:** the steps 1–15 lag (mean|Δ|≈0.147) entirely dominates the 0.07 whole-run miss; it is a *cadence artifact*, not a method limitation (SUMMARY/candidates §CONCLUSION).
- **PP-compatible:** raises anchor comm during warmup only (cadence-1 for ~5 steps); report net comm.
- **Confirm/refute:** whole-trajectory mean|Δ| ≤ 0.05 (strict PASS) with cadence-1 warmup; comm overhead acceptable.

**Suggested order for the planner:** H1 (diagnostic, gates the rest) → H5 (close plateau gap, cheap config sweep on the working C4) → H2 (off-principal correction — the primary surpass lever) ‖ H3 (entropy-preservation — the parallel surpass lever, share the same instrumented run) → H4 (only if H1 flags principal distortion) → H6 (strict-match cleanup). H2 and H3 are the two *surpass* bets and should be run with full §4.4 instrumentation against a cached dense reference (same EXP-17 shape, ≤50 steps).

## 6. Caveats / risks — where the paper's claims may not transfer

1. **Model lineage & scale.** Their spectral evidence is on **DS-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, Qwen3-8B/14B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B** — models that are *already heavily RL/instruction-distilled* (the "well-pretrained, highly structured geometry" Gate II requires). Our **Qwen2.5-1.5B-*Instruct*** is instruction-tuned but not long-horizon-RL-distilled; its principal geometry may be *less* sharply structured, so the off-principal steering (and thus the surpass headroom) could be **weaker**. The 1.5B DS results (Fig. 16, Table 4/5) are the closest proxy and are encouraging, but **H1 must verify the off-principal signal exists on *our* model** before betting on H2.
2. **With-KL vs our no-KL GRPO.** They run β=0 DAPO (clip-only) — *matches us*. But Gate I's *cleanest* statement (Prop. 3.1) is for β>0; for β=0 the leash is the weaker `O(ε²)` clipping bound (F.9) and KL **grows** over training (Fig. 15). So the "entropy-preservation via KL leash" argument (§3) is *softer* in our regime — masking-as-entropy-bonus (H3) may have to *carry* the entropy preservation that the (absent) KL term would otherwise provide. (This is arguably *why* H3 could matter more for us than for them.)
3. **Their RL recipe vs ours.** DAPO-Math-17k / DeepScaler+SimpleRL data, 16–32 rollouts/prompt, global batch 256, 8–32×H200, 150–320 steps, 8192–20480 context. Ours: GSM8K, n=8, batch 128, 4×H100/H200, 16384 context, ≤50 steps for the curve-match. **Our runs are much shorter** — the paper's spectrum-drift gaps emerge over hundreds of steps (Fig. 3 temporal); at 50 steps the geometry signal may be small/noisy. Mitigation: H2/H3 surpass-confirmation needs a **longer confirmation run** (the cycle's "then confirm on a longer run" step), not just 50 steps.
4. **Supervised-masking vs RL-masking is a different perturbation.** The paper's *masks* (`M_princ`, `M_low`, Sec. 5.1) are **parameter-update masks** (which weights are frozen) applied to an otherwise-full RL gradient — that is **not** our perturbation. We mask **activations at PP boundaries**, which biases the *gradient computation itself* (`g_mask = g_true + b + ξ`), not just the update support. So the paper's "off-principal = good for RL" result is *evidence for the target subspace*, but it does **not** directly tell us masking *activations* lands there — that inference is the §4.1 argument and must be measured (H1). Do not over-read Table 4/5 as "masking surpasses dense" — it says *off-principal parameter restriction* edges dense; we have to *show* our activation masking realizes an off-principal *parameter* update.
5. **"Surpass" is not in the paper.** The paper's best off-principal mask **edges dense on 1–2 metrics** (Math500 +1.0, AIME24 +2.0 at 500 steps) but is **≈ or slightly below on the average** (56.24 vs 57.44 at 320 steps; 58.37 vs 58.59 at 500). So the paper's own evidence for *surpassing* is **thin and metric-dependent** — it robustly supports *parity at lower cost / no degradation*, less robustly *strict surpass*. Our surpass goal is therefore **ambitious relative to the paper's own results**; the realistic high-confidence outcome is *match dense at p=0.9 comm savings with provably-off-principal, higher-entropy updates*, with *surpass* as an upside on pass@K / hard-subset generalization. Frame the cycle's success bar accordingly (a pass@K or held-out-slice win is a more likely "surpass" than a val-mean win).
6. **The orthogonality finding is ours, not theirs.** `cos(G_mask, g_true)≈0` (EXP-16/18) is specific to *activation* masking at p=0.9 + RMSNorm; the paper never masks activations. The geometry-aware corrections (H2/H4) lean on *our* measured orthogonality — if it weakens at lower p (the p-sweep), the complement-projection vs full-injection distinction (C2 vs C1 in `candidates.md`) re-matters.
7. **bf16 precision lens (Gate III) interacts with our rescale 9× inflation.** Our `G_mask` is rescale-inflated ~9×; the paper's sub-ULP argument (Cor. 3.6) assumes *natural* gradient magnitudes. The inflation could push *more* of our masked update over the bf16 ULP threshold than dense → *less* apparent sparsity / different realization pattern. Minor, but worth noting if we ever compare sparsity to the paper's numbers (we shouldn't — judge on val/entropy/pass@K, per SUMMARY).

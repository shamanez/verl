# Debate B — Do fixed-vs-variable-delay results survive variable/unknown delay AND non-stationary RL?

**Format:** adversarial cross-challenge between **async-sgd-scholar** (§02) and **multitimescale-rl-scholar** (§03), refereed.
**Referee role:** cross-challenge referee. Does NOT inherit prior conversation; written self-contained from `02-async-delayed-lit/notes.md`, `03-multitimescale-rl-lit/notes.md`, and the theorist's `01-off-policy-theory/notes.md`.
**Date:** 2026-06-22.
**Scope of the question (from `00-plan.md` §6, Debate B):** do the *fixed-known-delay* correction forms (Nesterov-async, DC-ASGD, PipeMare) and the *timescale-separation* conditions survive when delay is **variable & unknown** AND the objective is **non-stationary RL** (GRPO)? What is the weakest assumption each relies on?

> **Naming used throughout.** `K` = realized anchor staleness (in optimizer ticks; 2 ticks ≈ 1 global step). `τ` = the policy-drift horizon, the lag at which `ρ(k)=cos(g(θ_t),g(θ_{t−k}))` falls below a threshold `c⋆` (`01-off-policy-theory/notes.md` §3.1). `σ(M)` = the σ-algebra generated by the stale + current dense gradient means `{g(θ_t), g(θ_{t−K})}`. **Gap (a)** = parameter-point gap `‖g(θ_{t−K})−g(θ_t)‖ = O(K·‖Δθ‖·L_H)` (curvature-bounded, slow). **Gap (b)** = distribution / off-policy gap `𝔼_{π_{θ_t}}[∇ℓ_{θ_{t−K}}] − 𝔼_{π_{θ_{t−K}}}[∇ℓ_{θ_{t−K}}]` (NOT smoothness-bounded; dominates the measured decorrelation; `01` §1.2, §3.3). Measured: `τ_GSM8K ≈ 3–4` ticks, `τ_Big-Math ≈ 0` (`01` §3.3; `runs/EXP-38/verdict.md`).

## Pre-debate: the one axis that separates the two literatures

Both scholars independently converge on a single discriminating fact, and the entire debate turns on it:

- **§02's classics give guarantees for STALE GRADIENTS, but only on STATIONARY objectives** (DC-ASGD, Nesterov-async, PipeMare, Basis-Rotation, Mishchenko, EF-framework are all proven on a fixed `∇f`).
- **§02's 2025–26 async-LLM-RL cluster gives guarantees on NON-STATIONARY objectives, but the object they bound is NOT a re-used stale gradient** — it is live-rollout *trust weighting* / *LR scaling* / *gradient projection* that **controls** staleness rather than **corrects** a stale gradient (GAC, M2PO, VCPO).
- **§03's multi-timescale theory gives the only rigorous slow-circuit license, but via a step-size RATIO `b_n/a_n→0`** (a vanishing *dose*), on a **fixed equilibrium / GAS ODE**, and its working stabilizers (EMA/Polyak/mean-teacher/SlowMo) use a **smoothed-current** slow signal (bounded `O(ε)` distance to `θ_t`), **not a pure lag** `θ_{t−K}` (distance unbounded in K).

The two scholars do **not** actually disagree on facts. The apparent tension ("§02 says non-stationary guarantees exist; §03 says they don't transfer to a lag") dissolves once you ask *what object each guarantee binds*. The referee's job below is to make that precise per tension and to confirm there is no smuggled optimism.

## T1 — Do the fixed-known-delay correction FORMS survive variable & unknown delay?

**Claim at issue.** Nesterov-async (Ajanthan 2025, arXiv:2505.01099) assumes a *fixed, known* per-stage delay and tunes a `(1−γ)`-discount to it; DC-ASGD (Zheng 2017, arXiv:1609.08326) compensates a (possibly variable) delay via a first-order Taylor term `H·Δθ` using the *measured* `Δθ`. The question: which forms degrade gracefully under variable/unknown delay, and is "variable but BOUNDED" enough to rescue any of them?

**Strongest challenge (async-sgd-scholar steelman).** A correction's *form* and its *calibration* are separable. DC-ASGD's correction is `g(θ_t) ≈ g(θ_{t−K}) + λ·diag(g⊙g)·(θ_t − θ_{t−K})`: the magnitude is driven by the **measured displacement** `Δθ = θ_t − θ_{t−K}`, which is observable *at apply time regardless of how K was produced*. So DC-ASGD is intrinsically variable-delay-robust — it never needs to *know* K, only to read the current-vs-stale weight pair (`02` §2, brittle/admissible split). The same logic upgrades the others: take the *brittle-set form* (the seed's look-ahead, PipeMare's discrepancy correction, Basis-Rotation's eigenbasis rotation) and replace its **hardwired τ** with a **function of all-reduced, slow-varying sufficient statistics** (`02` §2 closing move). Variable-but-bounded staleness is then plenty: GAC (Xu 2026, arXiv:2603.01501) proves convergence under exactly a *bound*, not a known value (`02` §1D), and SSP (Ho 2013) / Ringmaster (Maranjyan 2025, arXiv:2501.16168) show a bounded/discard age-gate is even *optimal*. So the forms survive — re-parameterize, don't re-derive.

**Strongest rebuttal (multitimescale-rl-scholar + referee).** This conflates "the *arithmetic* of the form is computable under variable delay" with "the *guarantee* survives." Three distinct degradation modes:

1. **Fixed-τ-calibrated forms break outright under variable/unknown delay.** Nesterov-async's `(1−γ)` discount and PipeMare's per-stage schedule are *tuned to one delay value*; the moment realized lag deviates from the design point the discount is mis-calibrated and the correction is wrong-signed/over-applied (`02` §2: "any correction that is a fixed function of a single τ is mis-calibrated the moment the real lag deviates"). These are the **brittle set**. They do not survive as-is; they survive only after the re-parameterization the challenge proposes — which is *new work*, not a transferred guarantee.

2. **DC-ASGD survives the *delay variability* but dies on a *different* axis — the diagonal trap.** Its Hessian approximation is `diag(g⊙g)`, a **diagonal** preconditioner. Per `00-plan.md` §4 kill-check 2 and `01` §5, the dense control is Adam (diagonal `v_t`); a diagonal `H·Δθ` collapses to a per-coordinate rescale that is σ(`v_t`)-measurable — "a better Adam diagonal" — and caps at parity. So DC-ASGD's *form* is variable-delay-robust but **cannot raise the staleness budget beyond what gap (a) costs**, and gap (a) is the *small, slow* gap (`01` §3.3). It corrects the gap that doesn't dominate.

3. **There is an identifiability gap unique to the swarm.** DC-ASGD presumes you can read the *exact* `(θ_{t−K}, θ_t)` pair that produced `M`. In a single-slow-anchor-serving-a-fast-swarm deployment with *unknown* lag, you may not know *which* `θ_{t−K}` generated the anchor gradient (`02` §2, DC-ASGD row). Variable-but-bounded does not fix this — bounded-but-unknown still leaves `Δθ` ambiguous unless the anchor *stamps* its source weight version (which is a systems requirement, again new work).

**Resolution.**
- **Forms that survive variable & unknown delay (degrade gracefully):** DC-ASGD's `H·Δθ` *displacement* form (magnitude is data-driven, not τ-driven) and the **age-gate / discard** family (SSP, Ringmaster) — these need only a *bound* or an *observed* staleness, never a fixed known value. The RL-native projection/trust forms (GAC, M2PO second-moment, VCPO ESS-LR) also live in this class (`02` §2 admissible set).
- **Forms that break:** every correction that is a **fixed deterministic function of a single τ** — Nesterov-async's `(1−γ)` discount, PipeMare's per-stage schedule. They are usable only after their τ-parameter is replaced by an all-reduced slow statistic, which is a *design contribution*, not an inherited guarantee.
- **"Variable but bounded" is enough to rescue the *form* but not the *guarantee*.** It rescues DC-ASGD's computability and licenses GAC's bounded-staleness proof, but (i) DC-ASGD's diagonal form still caps at parity and corrects only the subdominant gap (a), and (ii) every surviving *guarantee* in this list (GAC, SSP, Ringmaster, Mishchenko) is **stationary** except the RL cluster, whose guarantee is about staleness *control*, not stale-gradient *correction* (see T2). **Net: the correction FORMS port; their convergence CERTIFICATES do not, and the one form whose certificate is variable-delay-and-RL-native (GAC) is a controller, not a corrector.**

## T2 — Does the two-timescale `b_n/a_n → 0` condition survive a NON-STATIONARY objective?

**Claim at issue.** The multitimescale scholar (`03` §1, §4.1(iii)) says **no**: `b_n/a_n→0` (Borkar 1997; Konda–Borkar 1997; Vidyasagar 2026, arXiv:2603.14481) assumes (i) martingale-difference (zero-conditional-mean) noise, (ii) bounded iterates, and (iii) a **globally asymptotically stable ODE equilibrium** — a *fixed target*. GRPO supplies none: the anchor signal is biased (non-zero-mean), and the gradient field is over the *current* policy's own samples, so the equilibrium *moves*. The async scholar points to the 2025–26 RL cluster (M2PO/GAC/VCPO) as evidence that *non-stationary* guarantees do exist. **Reconcile: are those RL "guarantees" about reusing a STALE GRADIENT, or about live-rollout trust-weighting / LR scaling — a different mechanism that CONTROLS staleness rather than CORRECTS a stale gradient?** This is the crux.

**Strongest challenge (async-sgd-scholar steelman).** The 2025–26 async-LLM-RL papers are, by construction, convergence/stability results on a *non-stationary RL objective under stale data*. They are not toy. M2PO (Zhao 2025, arXiv:2510.01161) proves stable training to 256+ stale iterations by constraining the second moment of IS weights; GAC (arXiv:2603.01501) gives a convergence guarantee under bounded staleness for LLM RL; VCPO (Huang 2026, arXiv:2602.17616) matches sync accuracy at 2.5× speed under heavy staleness via ESS-scaled LR. So the blanket claim "no non-stationary guarantee for stale signals exists" is simply false — the field has spent 2025–26 building exactly these. If they can certify stability under heavy staleness, the `b_n/a_n→0` *spirit* (let the slow/unreliable contribution be down-weighted) clearly survives into non-stationary RL.

**Strongest rebuttal (multitimescale-rl-scholar + referee — this is the decisive analysis).** Be precise about *what object each RL guarantee binds*. The async scholar's own §02 notes and the theorist's §01 §4.0–§4.2 establish that **none of M2PO/GAC/VCPO corrects a transported aggregated stale gradient.** They bind a *different* object:

- **VCPO (ESS-scaled LR).** The lever is a **scalar learning-rate multiplier** computed from the *effective sample size of live importance weights*, which VCPO has because it **keeps the rollouts and re-scores them with the current policy** (`01` §4.2; `02` §1D). ESS-LR is precisely the *scalar dose* — the only post-hoc lever the theorist's aggregation obstruction (`01` §4.0, eqs. 11–12) permits. **It CONTROLS how much of a (live, re-scored) update to trust; it does not re-aim a stale gradient.** It bounds `K`-influence by shrinking the step, the σ(M)-measurable shrink that caps at parity (`02` §1A, Zhang-2015 row; `01` §4.0).

- **M2PO (second-moment IS constraint).** Constrains `𝔼[w²]` of **per-sample importance weights**, suppressing only extreme outliers (`02` §1D; `01` §4.1 CV row). This is a *trust region on live re-scored ratios* — it down-weights unreliable samples *before* aggregation. M2PO's own framing — "stale data is as informative as on-policy *if exploited properly*, but training **without a trust region eventually collapses**" — is the external statement of `K<τ ⇒ stable / K>τ ⇒ collapse`. That is a **staleness-budget / prevention** statement, not a recipe for making a `K>τ` gradient correct.

- **GAC (gradient-alignment projection).** This is the one that *does* act on a gradient — but its lever is to **project out / damp the stale-aligned component** of the *live* stale gradient (`02` §1D, §4; `01` §4.2). Projection-and-damp is a *restriction* of the update (it removes a direction), not a *reconstruction* of `g(θ_t)` from `g(θ_{t−K})`. It prevents the overshoot that the correlated stale push causes; it does not convert the stale gradient into the current one. Damping a direction is still σ(M)-measurable (it is a deterministic `Φ(G_comp, M)`), hence parity-capped per `00-plan.md` §4.1.

So the reconciliation is exact and asymmetric: **the RL cluster's guarantees are guarantees about staleness CONTROL — trust-weighting, LR scaling, projection — over LIVE, un-aggregated, re-scored rollouts. None is a guarantee about a re-used aggregated stale gradient.** And critically, *every one of them needs the fresh-policy re-scoring of retained trajectories* (`01` §4.0, §4.2) — the exact thing the anchor, having discarded its trajectories and DP-mean-all-reduced its scores, **cannot supply**. The mechanism that earns the non-stationary certificate is the mechanism the anchor lacks.

Meanwhile the `b_n/a_n→0` condition *itself* does **not** survive non-stationarity in the sense that matters: two-timescale SA proves convergence *to the equilibrium of a coupled ODE*, conditional on that equilibrium **existing and not moving** (`03` §1, §4.1(iii)). Under GRPO the equilibrium moves, so even a perfectly dosed (`b_n/a_n→0`) stale anchor converges toward "the equilibrium of a defunct policy's gradient field" — the bias is **down-weighted, not removed** (`03` §1, translation paragraph). This is the *formal* origin of EXP-37's additive-merger DRAG: a vanishing dose buys **boundedness / stall-avoidance, not parity-beating signal** (`03` §4.1(iii)).

**Resolution.**
- **The `b_n/a_n→0` condition survives non-stationarity only as a *boundedness* device, not as a *correctness* device.** It can keep a biased stale contribution summable so the fast circuit dominates (→ no ignition), but it converges to a *moving* target's down-weighted equilibrium — it cannot make the stale gradient point the right way. Its faithful analogue is a **small/decaying anchor DOSE, scaled with K** (`03` §4.1(ii)), NOT a small staleness. This is consistent with EXP-31's δ-momentum null (`02` §1A) and EXP-37's additive DRAG.
- **The 2025–26 RL "guarantees" are real but bind a different object.** They certify stability of **staleness control** (VCPO scalar ESS-LR dose, M2PO live second-moment trust, GAC live gradient projection) over **live re-scored rollouts** — not the correction of a transported aggregated stale gradient. They therefore do **not** rescue `b_n/a_n→0` for our anchor, because (i) their object isn't a stale gradient and (ii) their enabling input (fresh-policy re-scoring of retained trajectories) is exactly what the anchor discards.
- **The async scholar is right that non-stationary guarantees exist; the multitimescale scholar is right that they don't license a lagged-gradient term.** Both notes already say this; the debate confirms there is no contradiction — the guarantees are about *controlling K / down-weighting live updates*, and the dose-separation buys boundedness only.

## T3 — Mishchenko "delays don't matter": friend or false friend?

**Claim at issue.** Mishchenko, Bach, Even, Woodworth 2022 (arXiv:2206.07638) prove async SGD beats minibatch under *arbitrary* (unbounded, unknown) delays via delay-adaptive stepsizes. The async scholar flags (`02` §1A, §3, §4) that this must **NOT** be cited as evidence anchor staleness is benign. **Confirm/refute: precisely which assumption does gap (b) violate, and is there ANY regime of our problem where its optimism applies?**

**Strongest challenge (steelman of the optimistic reading).** Mishchenko is the strongest possible "delays are cheap" result: it handles *arbitrary* delay (the hardest delay model, matching our variable/unknown case), needs no bounded-delay assumption, and its convergence is anchored to the *number of workers*, not the delay magnitude. If delays of *any* size barely matter for the rate, surely our finite K≈10 is benign — the pessimism about high-K is overblown, and we should just tune the stepsize (the delay-adaptive `η`).

**Strongest rebuttal (async-sgd-scholar + referee).** Mishchenko's optimism is **load-bearing on a stationary objective with a fixed minimizer**, and that is *exactly* the assumption gap (b) violates. The mechanism of the proof: a stale gradient `∇f(θ_{t−K})` is still a descent direction "toward the same `θ*`" because `∇f` does not move — the *virtual-iterate* analysis works precisely because the target is fixed (`02` §1A Mishchenko row, §3 table). Formally, every such bound rests on `⟨g(θ_{t−τ}), ∇f(θ_t)⟩ > 0` holding via smoothness of a **single fixed** `f` (`02` §3 first table row). Under GRPO the descent direction *itself rotates each step* because the rollout distribution `d_{π_θ}` is regenerated every update; gap (b) is exactly the term `𝔼_{π_{θ_t}}[∇ℓ_{θ_{t−K}}] − 𝔼_{π_{θ_{t−K}}}[∇ℓ_{θ_{t−K}}]` (`01` §1.2) that a stationary proof *cannot see* — in its world the inner product stays positive by smoothness; in ours it decays to 0.18 @K=5 and ≈0 @K=10 on GSM8K (`01` §3.3; `runs/EXP-38/verdict.md`). **The precise violated assumption is the fixed minimizer / stationary `∇f` (equivalently: a non-moving GAS equilibrium), the same assumption two-timescale SA needs (T2) and DQN's lagged target needs (`03` §2.1).** "The gradient is old but still points at the same optimum" is *true* for Mishchenko and *false* under gap (b).

**Is there ANY regime where Mishchenko's optimism applies to us?** Yes — and naming it sharpens the boundary:
- **Gap (a) in isolation** is curvature-bounded and behaves like a stationary stale gradient (`01` §1.1, §3.3): along a smooth trajectory `g(θ_{t−K})` is a genuine lagged estimate of the *same* descent field. If gap (b) were zero, the anchor would be latency-tolerant and telescoping — *which is exactly the SFT case* (`01` §1.3, BRIEF.md §1; SFT works at high K). So Mishchenko's optimism is the formal reason **SFT tolerates staleness**.
- **τ_GSM8K ≈ 3–4 ticks > 0** means there is a *narrow* low-K window where gap (b) has not yet dominated and the stale gradient is still mostly co-aligned (`ρ(k1)=0.507`); this is the `K<τ` stable regime (EXP-37 5/5). In this window the Mishchenko-style "stale-but-aligned" picture *approximately* holds — which is why low-K works at all. On **Big-Math, `τ≈0`** (`01` §3.3), there is **no such regime** — gap (b) is at the noise floor from k=1, and Mishchenko optimism never applies.

**Resolution.** **False friend for the headline claim, with a precisely bounded grain of truth.** Mishchenko 2022 must **not** be cited as evidence that anchor staleness is benign at the operating point — its result is voided by gap (b)'s violation of the *fixed-minimizer / stationary-`∇f`* assumption (`02` §3; `01` §1.2, §3.3). The *only* regimes where its optimism transfers are (i) **SFT** (gap (b) ≡ 0, the stale gradient is a lagged estimate of the same direction) and (ii) the **`K<τ` low-staleness window** on tasks with `τ>0` (GSM8K), where gap (b) has not yet dominated. It offers **nothing** for the actual target — large/variable K on a non-stationary objective — and on Big-Math (`τ≈0`) it offers nothing at any K.

## T4 — The synthesis: does ANY result give a stability guarantee for a STALE gradient under BOTH variable/unknown delay AND non-stationary RL?

**Claim at issue.** Either (A) some result certifies a lagged gradient stable under variable/unknown delay *and* non-stationary RL, or (B) the honest conclusion is that the field offers only **stationary guarantees for stale gradients** and **non-stationary guarantees only for staleness-CONTROL** (trust-region / dose / projection that bound or down-weight K) — i.e. the literature can *keep K<τ* or *shrink the dose*, but cannot make a `K>τ` stale gradient *correct*. Tie to the theorist's "trust region is preventive, not corrective."

**Strongest challenge (last steelman for (A)).** Assemble the strongest hybrid: GAC acts on a *gradient*, handles *bounded variable* staleness, and is proven on *non-stationary LLM RL* (arXiv:2603.01501) — that is gradient-space + variable-delay + non-stationary, all three boxes. AsyncMesh (arXiv:2601.22442) adds a *cross-rank EMA correction on a stale aggregate*, the structurally closest published thing to our setting. Stack GAC's projection onto AsyncMesh's cross-rank EMA and you arguably have a stale-gradient stability guarantee meeting both conditions.

**Strongest rebuttal (referee, synthesizing both notes).** The hybrid fails the definition of "corrective" on inspection:
- **GAC is a *controller*, not a *corrector*** (established in T2): it *projects out / damps* the stale-aligned direction of a **live** gradient to prevent overshoot. It bounds the damage of correlated staleness; it never reconstructs `g(θ_t)` from `g(θ_{t−K})`. Its certificate is for **bounded** staleness — i.e. it presumes `K` is *kept small enough*, which is prevention. And it operates on live gradients with fresh-policy access, not a transported aggregate.
- **AsyncMesh is *stationary*** (`02` §1B, §3) and its EMA-on-aggregate is a deterministic look-ahead (parity-class, σ(M)-measurable). It is *smoothed-current* in `03`'s sense, not a pure-lag corrector, and its guarantee dies on the same non-stationarity rock as every §02 classic.
- Stacking a *stationary* corrector (AsyncMesh) with a *non-stationary controller* (GAC) does **not** yield a *non-stationary corrector*: the corrective part loses its certificate under drift, and the part that keeps its certificate is non-corrective. The product is, at best, a non-stationary *controller* — which is option (B).

A full sweep confirms no single result lands in cell (A):

| Result class | Stale-GRADIENT correction? | Variable/unknown delay? | Non-stationary RL? | Verdict |
|---|---|:--:|:--:|---|
| DC-ASGD `H·Δθ` (`02` §1A) | ✓ (corrector) | ✓ (data-driven Δθ) | ✗ (stationary; + diagonal trap) | corrector, **stationary** |
| Nesterov-async / PipeMare (`02` §1B) | ✓ (corrector) | ✗ (fixed τ) | ✗ | corrector, **stationary + fixed-τ** |
| Basis-Rotation (`02` §1B) | ✓ (curvature corrector) | ✗ (depth-linear) | ✗ | corrector, **stationary** |
| Mishchenko / Ringmaster / SSP (`02` §1A) | ✗ (stepsize/discard, not correction) | ✓ (arbitrary) | ✗ | control, **stationary** |
| Two-timescale SA `b_n/a_n→0` (`03` §1) | ✗ (dose, not correction) | mechanism is dose | ✗ (fixed GAS equilibrium) | dose-control, **stationary** |
| DQN/DDPG/Lee-2026 target (`03` §2) | ✗ (lagged *label*, not gradient) | resync/track | ✗ (fixed contraction / linear op) | control, **fixed operator** |
| EMA-PG (`03` §3.1) | ✗ (KL proximity reference) | smoothed-current | ✓ (GRPO!) | control via **smoothed-current calibrator** |
| GAC / M2PO / VCPO (`02` §1D; `01` §4.2) | ✗ (projection / 2nd-moment trust / ESS-LR dose on **live** rollouts) | ✓ (bounded/variable) | ✓ (LLM RL) | **control**, non-stationary |
| **(A): stale-gradient corrector, variable+unknown delay, non-stationary RL** | — | — | — | **EMPTY** |

The pattern is exhaustive and clean: **the gradient-CORRECTING results are all stationary; the NON-STATIONARY results are all staleness-CONTROL** (dose, trust-region, projection, or a smoothed-current proximity calibrator — never a re-aimed transported stale gradient). Cell (A) is empty in *both* literatures.

This is precisely the theorist's "**trust region is preventive, not corrective**" (`01` §4.1, TRPO/PPO row): a trust region "bounds `K/τ` by bounding policy drift per update so the on-policy assumption is approximately preserved. It does **not** repair a *given* stale gradient. … It cannot raise the budget at *fixed* large K; it can only *forbid* large K." The synthesis lifts this from one method to the whole field: **every non-stationary guarantee in the literature is preventive** (it keeps `K<τ`, shrinks the dose, or projects out the bad direction); **none is corrective** (none takes a `K>τ` aggregated stale gradient and makes it the current gradient). And the deepest reason it *cannot* be corrective is upstream of all of this — the theorist's **aggregation obstruction** (`01` §4.0, eqs. 11–12): correcting a transported stale gradient would require a per-sample importance ratio, which cannot be applied post-hoc to a DP-mean-all-reduced, trajectory-discarded vector. There is no `w_i` left to multiply. The only admissible post-hoc lever is a *scalar dose* — which is control, not correction.

**Resolution: option (B), unambiguously.** **No result in either literature gives a stability guarantee for a STALE (lagged, transported, aggregated) gradient under BOTH variable/unknown delay AND non-stationary RL.** The field offers exactly two disjoint things:
1. **Stationary guarantees for stale-gradient *correction*** (DC-ASGD's `H·Δθ`, Nesterov-async look-ahead, Basis-Rotation eigenbasis) — these supply *correction FORMS* but lose their certificate under non-stationarity and (for the fixed-τ ones) under variable delay; DC-ASGD additionally caps at parity via the diagonal trap.
2. **Non-stationary guarantees for staleness *CONTROL*** (GAC projection, M2PO second-moment trust, VCPO ESS-LR dose, two-timescale `b_n/a_n→0` dose, EMA-PG smoothed-current calibrator) — these are **preventive**: they keep `K<τ`, shrink/decay the dose, or project out the stale-aligned direction, all over *live re-scored rollouts* or *smoothed-current* signals. **None makes a `K>τ` stale gradient correct.**

The literature can **keep `K<τ`** (trust region / age-gate) or **shrink the dose** (`b_n/a_n→0`, ESS-LR), but it **cannot make a `K>τ` stale aggregated gradient correct** — by the aggregation obstruction, the only post-hoc lever is a scalar dose, which is prevention. Trust region is preventive, not corrective; so is everything else with a non-stationary certificate.

## Implications for the report

**What the combined literature DOES license for our setting (variable staleness + non-stationary RL + cross-rank-identical):**
1. **Staleness CONTROL, with non-stationary certificates.** A **bounded-staleness age-gate** that *discards* the anchor gradient once realized `K` exceeds a budget (SSP/Ringmaster; cross-rank-identical because the bound is a shared scalar) — this is the *optimal* known handling of unbounded-but-observable delay, and it is *preventive* (`02` §1A). A **scalar staleness DOSE that decays with K** (two-timescale `b_n/a_n→0` → `03` §4.1(ii); VCPO ESS-LR → `02` §1D) — cross-rank-identical (one all-reduced scalar), buys **boundedness / stall-avoidance** but converges to a down-weighted *moving* equilibrium (caps at parity; cannot beat dense). A **second-moment / variance trust constraint** (M2PO; `02` §1D) and a **stale-aligned-direction projection** (GAC; `02` §1D) — both cross-rank-shareable, both *control* the correlated stale push, both preventive.
2. **Demotion of the anchor to a smoothed-current, slowly-varying CALIBRATOR rather than a re-injected lagged gradient** (EMA-PG's EMA-anchor-as-KL-reference, `03` §3.1; mean-teacher / Asadi proximity role, `03` §2.3). This is the literature, in *our exact algorithm* (GRPO ~1.5B), choosing smoothed-current over a pure lag and *proving it stable* — the complement of our lagged-gradient instability. It matches the on-file EXP-38 recommendation to recast the anchor as a slow cross-rank-identical **Q/codec calibrator** (BRIEF.md §6; `00-plan.md` §8).
3. **Correction FORMS — but only as un-certified templates to be re-validated empirically.** DC-ASGD's `H·Δθ`, the Nesterov look-ahead, and (most relevantly) **Basis-Rotation's Hessian-eigenbasis rotation** are *geometric* statements that remain *meaningful* under drift even though their convergence certificates do not transfer (`02` §3 row 4). Lifting them is the project's *contribution*, not an inherited guarantee, and each must clear the **diagonal trap** (DC-ASGD's diagonal Hessian does **not**; Basis-Rotation's off-diagonal eigenbasis is the credible *surpass*-route precedent — `02` §1B, §4).

**What it does NOT license:**
1. **A `K>τ` stale aggregated gradient cannot be made correct by any published method.** Cell (A) of T4 is empty. The aggregation obstruction (`01` §4.0) is upstream: no per-sample importance ratio survives DP-mean-all-reduce, so the only post-hoc lever is a scalar dose (= control, not correction).
2. **Fixed-known-delay correctors cannot be transferred as-is** to variable/unknown delay (Nesterov-async, PipeMare break on calibration; T1). Re-parameterizing their τ-dependence into all-reduced slow statistics is *new work*.
3. **No timescale-separation or trust-region argument removes the non-stationarity bias.** `b_n/a_n→0` converges to a *moving* target's down-weighted equilibrium — it buys boundedness, not parity-beating signal (T2; `03` §4.1(iii)). Trust regions, projections, and doses are all **preventive** — they keep `K` small or down-weight it; **none is corrective** (T4; `01` §4.1).
4. **Mishchenko-style "delays don't matter" optimism must not be invoked at the operating point** — it is voided by gap (b)'s violation of the fixed-minimizer assumption, and applies only to SFT or the narrow `K<τ` window (T3).

**One-sentence resolution for the report's "Limitations & open questions."**
> The async-optimization literature supplies stale-gradient *correction forms* but only *stationary* certificates (and the fixed-τ ones break under variable delay); the 2025–26 async-LLM-RL and multi-timescale literatures supply *non-stationary* certificates but only for staleness *control* — trust-region, decaying dose, gradient projection, or a smoothed-current calibrator — so **no result certifies a transported `K>τ` aggregated stale gradient stable under both variable/unknown delay and non-stationary RL; the field can keep `K<τ` or shrink the dose but cannot make a `K>τ` stale gradient correct, exactly because (theorist `01` §4.0) the only post-hoc lever on an aggregated gradient is a scalar dose, which is preventive, not corrective.**

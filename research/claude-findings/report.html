1Executive summary

The wall, precisely. A communication-efficient pipeline-parallel GRPO trainer folds a clean full gradient M = g(θt−K) — computed on K-tick-stale weights — into the fast compressed gradient via a "merger". At low staleness (K ≈ 2.5 global steps) this is stable and ≈ dense parity; at high staleness (K ≈ 10) both merger families fail — the sign-replacing merger ignites (length explodes, val crashes below the no-merger floor) and the additive merger drags (sub-baseline plateau, grad-norm creeps as the held stale correction tracks policy drift). One cause, two symptoms.

The finding. The binding constraint is not curvature/parameter-drift — it is the off-policy distribution gap, which is unbounded by smoothness and dominates the measured gradient decorrelation. Three results follow, and they converge on one design: (1) no importance-sampling correction can re-aim a stale, already-aggregated gradient (the aggregation obstruction); (2) any curvature/extrapolation correction repairs only the small, slow part of the error and is definitionally blind to the dominant part (the reach bound); (3) across both the distributed-optimization and the async-RL literatures, no result certifies a transported large-K stale gradient stable under variable delay + a non-stationary objective — the field can only keep K small or shrink the dose.

The recommendation is therefore to stop transporting a stale gradient. Transport a slow, cross-rank-identical statistic instead — the activation/codec basis (and, for surpass, a cross-rank second moment) — for which staleness is provably a non-problem in the existing data. Concretely:

  • Primary Parity-at-larger-K (robust): recast the anchor into an activation-space slow calibrator — it maintains the projection basis / curvature geometry and a KL/codec reference, but never folds a gradient into the step. Its core empirical claim is already true in the existing captures: the transported forward subspace does not decorrelate with lag (overlap ≈ 1.0 flat across k = 1…40), whereas the gradient direction decorrelates in ≈ 3 ticks. This is the only candidate for which "large K" is not even a problem, and it carries no async-admissibility tension. EXP-38
  • Secondary Surpass (aspirational): if beating dense is the goal, the only route the analysis does not cap is a cross-rank second moment used as an objective (disagreement-as-objective, SAM-style) — information outside σ(M) and outside Adam's diagonal, provided it is added as a gradient term (different fixed point), not a g/√Var step (which collapses to the diagonal trap).
  • Demoted: the learned gradient-extrapolation route (RK ≈ H·Δθ), the project's own most-recent seed direction, is reach-capped; its cheap offline cosine-lift kill-test is the falsifier and is predicted to fail/barely-pass on the easy task and ≈ 0 on the hard task.

Scalar staleness-dose, trust-region, and alignment-projection levers (GAC/M2PO/VCPO-style) are Parity robustifiers of the existing low-K regime — worth wiring as guardrails, but they do not raise the budget at large K, because at large K the stale gradient is already almost pure carrier (GSM8K cos@k20 ≈ 0).

2Problem & system

The substrate is settled and is not relitigated here (it is judged sound by prior experiments EXP-29/30; see Provenance). The trainer has two circuits:

  • Fast circuit — the online policy. It compresses pipeline-boundary activations with a PowerSGD-style rank-r=77 frozen-basis projector (hidden size H=1536). Its gradient Gcomp is biased but functional. Established
  • Slow "anchor" circuit — at cadence C, one unmasked full forward/backward from a delay_K-tick-stale weight snapshot θt−K, on the paired stale rollouts those weights generated (ratio ≡ 1, a clean PG loss). This yields a clean reference gradient M = g(θt−K; Dt−K), DP-mean all-reduced to full coverage. The anchor also owns the projection basis Q. A merger folds M into the fast gradient before optimizer.step(). With the method off, training is byte-identical to dense GRPO. Established

delay_K and the cadence are counted in optimizer ticks (2 ticks per global step on this surface), so "5/5" ≈ 2.5 global steps of staleness and "20/20" ≈ 10 [staleness_theorist §1.1]. The two production mergers:

The open question Design a robust, theoretically-grounded way to use the anchor's full gradient at large / variable K without ignition or drag — i.e. raise the usable staleness budget — while respecting the σ(M) ceiling (explicit parity-vs-surpass label per idea), the diagonal trap, and the async constraint. The primary target is parity-at-larger-K; surpass is a secondary, must-be-labelled aspiration.

3Mathematical analysis

3.1 The two-gap decomposition

Write g(θ; D) for the GRPO surrogate gradient evaluated at weights θ over data drawn from distribution D — weights and data decoupled on purpose. Let Dt = D(πθt) be the rollout distribution the policy at tick t generates, and gt = g(θt; Dt) = ∇J(θt) the live on-policy gradient. The error the merger actually injects is the difference between what it transports (M = g(θt−K; Dt−K)) and what the optimizer should follow (gt). Inserting the mixed term g(θt−K; Dt) splits the staleness error uniquely into a weight-move part and a data-move part:

(1) eK = gt − M = [ g(θt;Dt) − g(θt−K;Dt) ] (a) parameter-point gap  +  [ g(θt−K;Dt) − g(θt−K;Dt−K) ] (b) distribution gap Δdist

The two terms are governed by entirely different mathematics. Theory

  • Gap (a) is curvature-bounded. Holding the data fixed, it is an integral of the Hessian, so by β-smoothness (‖∇²θg(θ;D)‖ ≤ LH):
    (2) ‖eKparam‖ = ‖∫₀¹ H(θt−K+sΔθ; Dt) Δθ ds‖ ≤ LH‖Δθ‖ ≤ LH·K·η·‖u‖,   Δθ ≜ θt−θt−K
    It grows at most linearly in K along a smooth trajectory: the stale gradient is, to first order, a rotated version of the current one, and the rotation is exactly the Hessian–vector product H·Δθ. This is the only gap a curvature/extrapolation correction can ever address.
  • Gap (b) is NOT bounded by smoothness. Writing it under the old policy with an importance weight w(τ) = πθt(τ)/πθt−K(τ):
    (3) Δdist = 𝔼τ∼πθt−K[ (w(τ)−1)·∇θℓθt−K(τ) ]
    The object that moved is the measure, not the evaluation point, so no smoothness constant bounds it. For an autoregressive policy over an O(10²)-token response, w(τ) is a product of hundreds of per-token ratios: small per-token KL compounds into a heavy-tailed weight far from 1, and the stale trajectories can sit off the current policy's support entirely.

What SFT deletes. Under supervised fine-tuning the data is an exogenous frozen dataset D₀ with no policy in the loop, so Dt = Dt−K = D₀ and Δdist ≡ 0 identically. The stale SFT gradient is then a lagged estimate of the same descent direction on the same objective — error bounded by curvature, telescoping under error feedback. That single deleted term is the entire difference between "the method works in SL" and "it ignites/drags in GRPO." Theory (mechanism-grounded; no in-program SFT run — flagged) [01 §1.3].

3.2 Off-policy as bias, not variance

There are two ways to use old trajectories, and they trade along opposite axes. Textbook importance-sampled off-policy PG reweights to stay unbiased for the current gradient, paying variance that grows exponentially in divergence (the 2-Rényi second moment of the weight, d₂(πt‖πt−K); Metelli et al. 2018/2020). The anchor does the opposite trade: with no importance weight (paired replay makes M an exact, low-variance gradient of the defunct policy πθt−K), it is a precise, low-variance estimate of the wrong gradient:

Why low variance + persistence is worse than noise. The optimizer integrates its corrections. A zero-mean noise term's partial sums grow like a random walk, O(√T·σ) — it cancels relative to the O(T) signal. A persistent biased term with a stable direction integrates linearly:

(4) carrier condition ‖Σt correctiont‖ ~   {   O(√T·σ) zero-mean noise (cancels)  ;  λ·T·‖e‖ persistent biased pull (integrates)   }

The decisive empirical corroboration: the cross-run collapse discriminator is merger-carrier presence, not gradient noise — plain PowerSGD (same noisy-gradient class, no merger) trains cleanly on the identical substrate while the merger spikes [why-grpo-fails §0.5, claim 9]. Lowering M's variance (what paired replay did) makes the carrier cleaner and more coherent — a more reliable wrong-direction pull, not a fix. The two symptoms are two projections of this one biased carrier: sign-replacement rectifies it into a length-hacking ratchet (the killer is length, not entropy; the token-mean loss gives ~86× tail amplification along the reward-flat "correct-but-longer" manifold), while the additive merger deposits it as a wrong-direction drag whose magnitude tracks ‖θt−θt−K‖ (EXP-37E grad-norm 2→55 at flat length) [why-grpo-fails §2].

3.3 The K>τ stability condition and the task-dependent budget

Define the policy-drift horizon τ(c⋆) = min{ k ≥ 1 : ρ(k) < c⋆ }, where ρ(k) = cos∠(gt, gt−k). The stale-anchor merger is stable iff K < τ, with failure probability monotone in K/τ; the misaligned (carrier) component of M grows as ρ(K) falls, and once its integral (4) crosses the length-ratchet's self-sustaining threshold the run ignites or drags — a threshold-crossing of an integral, which is why onset is late (steps 50–100), not immediate. If only gap (a) were present, τ would track the slow weight clock (O(16 ticks)). It does not. Therefore Δdist (gap b) dominates the staleness error, not curvature·‖Δθ‖. EXP-38 H2 [01 §3.3]

3.4 The aggregation obstruction

Can any off-policy correction re-aim the stale gradient with no fresh samples from πθt? Every importance-sampling family — vanilla/per-decision IS, truncated/clipped IS, V-trace, Retrace(λ) — forms a per-sample ratio and reweights the summand before aggregation. The anchor delivers neither input: M is a single vector whose per-sample scores are already summed and DP-mean all-reduced (the trajectories are gone), and we have no current-policy probabilities. Reweighting is nonlinear in the summands:

(5) Σi wi xi ≠ ( (1/N) Σi wi ) · Σi xi    unless wi is constant across i

So a per-sample ratio cannot be applied post-hoc to an aggregated gradient — there is no wi left to multiply each xi. The only post-hoc lever is a single scalar w·M, which is a staleness-scaled dose — it shrinks the wrong-direction vector, it does not re-aim it. The obstruction is informational and upstream of the IS variance blow-up (we never get to pay the variance). Theory — the key result of the off-policy analysis [01 §4.0]. The 2025–26 async-LLM-RL methods that do stabilize stale-rollout training (GAC, VCPO, M2PO, VESPO, GIPO) confirm this from the other side: without exception they operate on live, re-scored trajectories and apply scalar/sequence trust weighting, LR scaling, or direction projection — never a reweight of an aggregated gradient (§4).

3.5 The σ(M) ceiling, the diagonal trap, and the reach bound

Let σ(M) = σ(gt, gt−K) be the information in the current and stale dense gradient means, with dense-Adam as the control. σ(M) ceiling: any deterministic Φ(Gcomp, M) is σ(M)-measurable, hence caps at dense parity; surpass needs information outside σ(M) — (i) curvature/2nd-order, (ii) conversion-positive exploration that moves the greedy argmax, or (iii) a cross-rank 2nd moment. Every admissible post-hoc lever on M — a scalar dose, a trust-region clip, a direction projection, a past-gradient finite-difference extrapolation — is such a Φ, hence Parity-at-larger-K. This is why the prior frontier sweeps (perturbation, δ-momentum, adaptive-λ, control-variate, sub-basis, β-averaging) were all null for surpass [SUMMARY.md / GOAL.md].

The diagonal trap The control is dense-Adam, whose vt is already a per-coordinate (diagonal) second moment. Any correction that collapses to a per-coordinate rescale is "a better Adam diagonal" and stays at parity, regardless of how much it improves. Surpass requires genuinely off-diagonal structure — a true rotation H·Δθ with cross-coordinate terms, or a variance-as-objective term with a different fixed point.
The reach bound — definitional, not merely empirical Curvature/extrapolation RK ≈ H·Δθ repairs only gap (a). The Hessian H = ∇²θ is defined with the data measure held fixed, so it is constitutively blind to Δdist (gap b) — no operator on the weight-space gradient field can touch a moved measure. Since gap (b) dominates the decorrelation (§3.3), even a perfect off-diagonal un-rotation cannot restore ρ(K) past τdist (≈ 3–4 ticks GSM8K, ≈ 0 Big-Math). The surpass-reach of any curvature route is the gap-(a) fraction of eK — small on GSM8K, ≈ 0 on Big-Math. Theory The cross-challenge sharpened this from "empirical-on-the-measured-tasks" to definitional [01 §3.3/§5.2; Debate A T3].

4Literature review

Two literatures bear on the problem, and they divide cleanly along an axis that turns out to be decisive. Distributed optimization supplies stale-gradient correction forms, but every guarantee is stationary; the 2025–26 async-LLM-RL line supplies non-stationary guarantees, but only for staleness control — never for re-using a transported stale gradient. The screening axes are: space (gradient / weight / activation); delay model (fixed-vs-variable, known-vs-unknown — ours is variable & unknown); objective (stationary vs non-stationary RL — ours is non-stationary); and cross-rank identity (must derive from all-reduced sufficient statistics). The multi-timescale distinction (the load-bearing transfer fact). Nearly every working slow/fast stabilizer — Polyak/EMA, mean-teacher, Lookahead, SWA, SlowMo, DDPG soft target — uses a slow signal that is a low-pass of the CURRENT iterate, a convex combination including θt, so ‖θ̄t−θt‖ = O(ε) is bounded. Our anchor is a pure lag θt−K whose distance is unbounded in K; those convex-combination contraction arguments do not transfer. The only rigorous license for a slow circuit (two-timescale SA) is the step-size ratio bn/an→0 — a small/decaying dose, not a small staleness — and even that assumes a fixed equilibrium, which a non-stationary RL objective violates [03 §1, §4].

The empty cell (Debate B, exhaustive sweep) Across both literatures, the gradient-correcting results are all stationary (DC-ASGD, Nesterov-async, Basis-Rotation, PipeMare); the non-stationary results are all staleness-control (GAC projection, M2PO 2nd-moment trust, VCPO ESS-LR dose, two-timescale dose, EMA-PG smoothed-current calibrator). The cell "stale-gradient corrector × variable/unknown delay × non-stationary RL" is empty. The field can keep K<τ or shrink the dose, but cannot make a K>τ aggregated stale gradient correct — which is exactly the aggregation obstruction (§3.4): the only post-hoc lever is a scalar dose, and a scalar dose is prevention, not correction. (i) Staleness-scaled / age-decayed dose  Parity-at-larger-K

Down-weight the correction by realized lag (λ(K)·δ), e.g. the in-code μ^age held-correction decay or ratio-gated adaptive-λ; external realization = VCPO's ESS-scaled LR. It is the only post-hoc lever the aggregation obstruction permits, and it is fully async-admissible (keys on realized lag, cross-rank-identical). Kill-test: the misaligned-energy fraction m(k) = √(1−ρ(k)²) on the EXP-38 tensors; since GSM8K m(5)=0.984 and m(20)≈1.000, the dose is already near-flat in carrier energy at large K ⇒ to match the stable 5/5 tangential dose at K=20 requires λ→0. Honest negative result: at large K the only safe dose is ≈ 0 — dosing recovers the no-merger floor, not parity. Consistent with the prior δ-momentum/adaptive-λ nulls.

(ii) Trust-region / alignment cap  Parity-at-larger-K

Project out / clip the wrong-direction component of the correction before merging — the principled version of GAC's gradient-projection and M2PO's 2nd-moment trust. Async-admissible only if the projection direction and threshold are formed from all-reduced quantities (the shared M, Gcomp). Kill-test: retained useful fraction R(k) = |ρ(k)|; worth GPU only if |ρ(K)| ≥ 0.10 at target K. GSM8K passes at K=5 (0.176) but fails at 10/20 (0.023 / 0.008); Big-Math fails at K=1 (0.018). It robustifies the existing 5/5 regime to roughly K≈5–8 on GSM8K and does nothing on Big-Math — alignment-capping can only preserve alignment that still exists.

(iii) IS-reweighted anchor  DEAD-END

Removed from contention by the aggregation obstruction (§3.4): no per-sample ratio survives DP-mean all-reduce, and we have no fresh πθt probabilities. The only re-entry — keep the stale per-sample scores + logits and do one extra current-policy forward pass over the stale trajectories (the "missing old logits" fix) — re-incurs the IS variance blow-up at K>τ and collapses the comm-eff premise into ordinary async off-policy GRPO (the VCPO/VESPO/GIPO line), a different research program. Recorded so the report can state crisply why the entire IS/V-trace/Retrace toolbox is off the table.

(iv) Learned extrapolation RK ≈ H·Δθ  Parity (fixed/diagonal) / Surpass (off-diagonal)

The project's own most-recent seed direction: un-rotate the stale gradient by modelling the Hessian-vector product, in three rungs — (a) fixed-linear (DC-ASGD diagonal Taylor / Nesterov-async look-ahead), (b) learned on the anchor's (θ,g) trajectory, (c) learned + error-feedback against the periodic ground-truth refresh; with Basis-Rotation's Hessian-eigenbasis as the concrete off-diagonal + cross-rank-identical realization. This is the candidate where the diagonal trap bites: a diagonal Ĥ is "a better Adam diagonal" = parity regardless of cosine recovered; only an off-diagonal lift is a surpass candidate. Kill-test (the central GPU-free gate): fit RK on the EXP-38 tensors, evaluate the stale→live cosine lift on held-out lags against the threshold GSM8K cos@k5: 0.176 → ≥ 0.40, and decompose the achieved lift into diagonal vs off-diagonal energy (a purely-diagonal lift is parity-only). The reach bound predicts this largely fails — a perfect un-rotation removes only the small slow gap-(a) part and leaves the dominant fast gap-(b) part; on Big-Math (cos@k1 = 0.018) the lift is predicted ≈ 0. A failure is productive: it simultaneously kills (iv) and strengthens (vii). Async-admissibility is conditional — see §6.

(v) Multi-timescale optimizer with explicit separation  Parity / stall-avoidance

Treat the anchor as the slow recursion and enforce bn/an→0. The literature-faithful reading is decisive: the rigorous license is a vanishing dose, not a small staleness — so this is candidate (i)'s theoretical justification, sharpened to decay the dose with K (e.g. dose ∝ 1/K, or gate off above τ). It buys boundedness / stall-avoidance, not a parity raise — a pure lag breaks the "slow sees fast as equilibrated" premise, and the fixed-equilibrium assumption fails under a non-stationary objective. Kill-test: verify a decaying-dose schedule keeps the carrier partial-sum (4) O(√T)-like while a fixed dose grows O(T).

(vi) Activation-space recast / anchor-as-Q-(or-curvature)-calibrator  Parity-at-larger-K (robust)

Stop transporting a stale gradient; transport a slow, cross-rank-identical statistic instead, so the off-policy gap Δdist never forms. The anchor maintains the projection basis / curvature geometry (and a KL/codec reference, EMA-PG-style) but never folds a gradient into the step. Two layers: forward link — a low-rank (rank-1/few) activation codec with a slowly-refreshed Q on its own fast cadence, decoupled from delay_K (refreshing Q is nearly free — no stale backward — and Q is already cross-rank-identical via sync + broadcast); backward link — a separate, higher-rank codec sized per task (≥105 GSM8K / ≥180 Big-Math). The optional curvature variant transports the Hessian eigenbasis (Basis-Rotation) as the slow shared statistic. The structural win: a statistic is staleness-tolerant in a way a gradient is not — the forward subspace lives in the slow gap-(a)/geometry regime, not the fast gap-(b)/policy regime. Kill-test: largely already PASSED by EXP-38 H3 — forward h is rank ≈ 1 (top-1 energy 99.1%/98.6%) and top-1 subspace overlap = 1.0 flat for k = 1…40 on both tasks, so a frozen/slow Q is staleness-tolerant; the budget question dissolves for the forward codec. Residual offline checks (runnable now): the backward-grad_h overlap-vs-lag curve and a calibrator-sufficiency decomposition of the merged update into its Q-projection vs M-folding components.

(vii) Cross-rank 2nd-moment / disagreement-as-objective  Surpass (not reach-capped)

Use the disagreement between concurrent same-θ rank gradients (data-variance, NOT cross-θ staleness) as a signal. Required form: variance-as-objective — add a term whose gradient is ∇θVarr (a SAM-style ascent-descent, or a λ·R(Varr) penalty) so the fixed point itself changes; not a g/√Var step (which reduces to a per-coordinate diagonal = the diagonal trap). Varr over concurrent rank gradients is not a function of the gradient means (gt, gt−K) ⇒ outside σ(M), and it injects a different fixed point rather than an un-rotation ⇒ not capped by the reach bound — so it may dominate curvature precisely on gap-(b)-dominated tasks like Big-Math. Async-admissible if resolved from all-reduced sufficient statistics (cross-rank-identical, slow-varying by construction). Honest gap: the clean kill-test (does same-θ cross-rank disagreement predict descent error?) needs a cross-rank ensemble that EXP-38 (n=1) lacks — it requires a cheap multi-rank capture, the one place the recommendation is not fully GPU-free.

6Recommended approach

Raise the budget by recasting the anchor into an activation-space slow calibrator (candidate vi) — the robust, async-clean, theory-and-data-supported budget-raiser — and IF surpass is wanted, the only credible non-reach-capped bet is cross-rank disagreement-as-objective (candidate vii), with the explicit caveat that the gradient-extrapolation route (iv) is gap-(b)-capped and should be gated on its cheap offline cosine-lift falsifier before any GPU spend.

6.1 Why (vi) is the most defensible primary

  1. It removes the off-policy gap at its source. Every gradient-space candidate inherits the dominant, unbounded Δdist and can at best manage it. (vi) transports a statistic, and EXP-38 H3 proves that statistic is staleness-tolerant on both tasks (forward subspace overlap flat across lag, incl. Big-Math). The thing that decorrelates in ≈ 3 ticks is no longer transported; the thing transported does not decorrelate within the measured window. For the forward codec, "large K" is provably a non-problem.
  2. It is async-clean with no open tension. The anchor is demoted to a slow, cross-rank-identical calibrator that never provides a step direction — exactly EMA-PG's calibrator role, proven stable in GRPO at ~1.5B. The lead-vs-continuation admissibility question (which gates iv) never arises.
  3. It is the convergent recommendation of the prior work. It is EXP-38's own next-method recommendation (activation-space, split fwd/bwd budgets, demote the anchor to a slow Q/codec calibrator, set K & backward rank per task), now cross-checked against the theory (the gradient route is obstructed + reach-capped) and the multi-timescale literature (working slow circuits are smoothed-current or calibrators, never re-injected lagged gradients).

6.2 The falsifiable offline kill-test

The primary's core claim is already empirically true; the residual claims are cheap to check on the existing captures. No GPU required.

  • Forward-codec staleness-tolerance — PASS. Q-overlap at the target K ≥ 0.95 × overlap at K=5. Already true: overlap flat across k = 1…40 (EXP-38 H3). This is the headline — the budget-raiser's core claim holds.
  • Backward-codec sizing — measured, must be honored. grad_h rank-for-90% = 105 (GSM8K) / 180 (Big-Math); set the separate backward codec rank ≥ these. Caveat (from Debate A): EXP-38 established overlap-flatness only for the rank-1 forward h; the rank-105/180 backward subspace and the eigenbasis variant are expected-but-not-proven staleness-tolerant (full rank-77 overlap is 0.77/0.71, flat-but-not-1.0). The backward-grad_h overlap-vs-lag curve must be measured before the backward claim is stated as fact.
  • Calibrator-sufficiency — runnable now. Decompose the current merged update into its Q-projection vs M-folding components; the recast is justified if the M-folding component's alignment with gt is ≤ the stale-gradient cosine (≈ 0.18 at k5, → 0 at k≥10) — i.e. the gradient-folding is mostly carrier and safe to drop.

For the surpass aspiration, run candidate (iv)'s cosine-lift falsifier first (it is fully laptop-only and bounds the gap-(a) reach): GSM8K cos@k5: 0.176 → ≥ 0.40 with an off-diagonal lift. The recommendation predicts it fails or barely passes on GSM8K and is ≈ 0 on Big-Math; a failure kills (iv) and strengthens (vii). Candidate (vii)'s clean test (the fixed-point probe: a non-zero update where ∇J ≈ 0 is the signature of a different fixed point, which a diagonal preconditioner cannot produce) needs a short multi-rank capture and is the one step that is not fully GPU-free.

6.3 Parity-vs-surpass call

The primary (vi) is Parity-at-larger-K — robust. It raises the budget; it does not by itself surpass dense (the forward-Q recast injects no information dense-Adam lacks). That is the correct, honest target for the open question's primary objective. Surpass is only credible via (vii) — and only in its variance-as-objective form. The gradient-extrapolation route (iv), the seemingly-obvious surpass bet from the project's own seed, is gap-(b)-capped by a definitional argument (the Hessian holds the data measure fixed); even Basis-Rotation does not rescue it as a staleness-repair lever — Basis-Rotation is a separate, K-independent conditioning win that is diagonal-trap-governed parity unless it is genuinely off-diagonal and withheld from the dense control.

6.4 Async-admissibility resolution

  • Primary (vi): no tension. The anchor is a slow calibrator that never forecasts or leads; Q is cross-rank-identical and EXP-38-flat across lag. Fully admissible under the async constraint as written.
  • Secondary (vii): admissible iff the disagreement correction is resolved from all-reduced sufficient statistics (its native form) — cross-rank-identical, slow-varying, no lead.
  • (iv), if pursued: the trajectory-continuation-vs-anchor-lead question is a project-policy ruling, not a theorem. The cross-challenge proposes a clean two-part decision rule:
    Admissibility rule for any extrapolation correction (Debate A T4) (R1) Observed-displacement-only. Extrapolate only in quantities already realized and all-reduced at correction time — Δθ = θt − θt−K where θt is the live circuit's current (not predicted) weights. No model of a future θt+m, no velocity forecast of where the swarm will go (the line between continuation and DANA-style lead). (R2) Cross-rank-identical + variable-staleness-tolerant. Ĥ (or the eigenbasis) fit from all-reduced sufficient statistics, across a range of realized lags, not a hardwired τ.
    Under this rule, continuation to the observed present is admissible; forecasting a future state is not. This gates only the reach-capped route (iv), not the primary (vi) or secondary (vii) — so even an operator ruling that (iv) is inadmissible leaves the recommendation untouched. The ruling can be deferred until (iv)'s offline falsifier passes (predicted to fail), which may make it moot.
Bottom line Raise the budget via (vi) — its core claim (forward-codec staleness-tolerance) is already empirically true. If surpass is wanted, the only credible bet is (vii) cross-rank disagreement-as-objective, because (iv) gradient-extrapolation is gap-(b)-capped. The dose / cap / multi-timescale levers (i)/(ii)/(v) are parity-only robustifiers of the existing low-K regime — worth wiring as guardrails, but they do not raise the budget at large K because there the stale gradient is already almost pure carrier.

7Cross-challenge resolutions

Two adversarial debates were run before synthesis; both upheld the recommendation and sharpened it. [05/debateA, 05/debateB]

7.1 Debate A — the diagonal trap & parity-vs-surpass (theorist vs architect)

  • T1 (does vi raise the budget or relocate the signal?) — UPHELD, qualified. The budget raise is principled: the transported subspace lives in the slow gap-(a)/geometry part of the error, not the fast gap-(b)/policy part. But the "overlap 1.0 flat" keystone is rank-1-only; the high-rank backward codec and eigenbasis variant inherit only a flat-but-0.77/0.71 overlap and are expected-but-not-proven staleness-tolerant. The report states this honestly rather than carrying a rank-1-to-all-rank overgeneralization.
  • T2 (does vii clear the diagonal trap?) — UPHELD. The objective-vs-step distinction is sound: the diagonal trap governs preconditioners (rescaling a fixed direction); the objective-form adds a gradient term ∇θVarr that survives where ∇J=0 — a place no diagonal can reach. The discriminating test is the fixed-point probe, not the weak temporal-dispersion proxy.
  • T3 (is iv truly gap-(b)-capped, even as Basis-Rotation?) — UPHELD, strengthened to definitional. The Hessian holds the data measure fixed, so it is constitutively blind to gap (b); no curvature route beats the reach bound on a gap-(b)-dominated task. Basis-Rotation changes the subject (conditioning, not staleness-repair) and is itself diagonal-trap-governed.
  • T4 (continuation vs lead) — ADMISSIBLE under R1+R2 (continuation to the observed present is not lead); inadmissible if θt must be forecast. Gates only the capped route (iv).

7.2 Debate B — fixed-vs-variable-delay survival (async vs multitimescale)

No factual disagreement between the scholars — only an apparent one that dissolves once you name what object each guarantee binds. The fixed-known-delay forms split (DC-ASGD's data-driven H·Δθ and age-gates degrade gracefully; Nesterov-async's (1−γ) discount and PipeMare's per-stage schedule break); the two-timescale bn/an→0 condition survives only as a boundedness device (converges to a moving target's down-weighted equilibrium = stall-avoidance, not parity); the 2025–26 RL guarantees (M2PO/GAC/VCPO) bind staleness control over live re-scored rollouts, never a transported aggregated stale gradient; Mishchenko is a false friend voided by gap (b). Conclusion: the empty cell (§4) — no result certifies a K>τ aggregated stale gradient stable under variable delay + non-stationary RL. The field can keep K<τ or shrink the dose, but cannot make a K>τ stale gradient correct.

The one-sentence synthesis The async-optimization literature supplies stale-gradient correction forms (DC-ASGD's H·Δθ, the Nesterov look-ahead, Basis-Rotation's eigenbasis) but only stationary certificates; the 2025–26 async-LLM-RL and multi-timescale literatures supply non-stationary certificates but only for staleness control — trust-region, decaying dose, gradient projection, or a smoothed-current calibrator — so no result makes a K>τ aggregated stale gradient correct, because (the aggregation obstruction) the only post-hoc lever on an aggregated gradient is a scalar dose, which is preventive, not corrective. The robust way to raise the staleness budget is therefore to stop transporting a stale gradient and transport a staleness-invariant statistic instead — which is exactly what the data already supports and what the literature, in our own algorithm, has independently chosen and proven stable.

## Notation and standing assumptions

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `θ_t` | online (fast-circuit) policy parameters at optimizer tick `t` |
| `K` | anchor staleness, in **optimizer ticks** (2 ticks / global step; batch 128 / mini 64). `delay_K=5` ⇒ K≈2.5 global steps; `delay_K=20` ⇒ K≈10 (`runs/DELAY_ANALYSIS/staleness_theorist.md` §1.1; `[[anchor-cadence-delayk-unit-optimizer-ticks]]`) |
| `π_θ` | the autoregressive policy induced by `θ` |
| `D_t ≜ D(π_{θ_t})` | the rollout distribution generated by the policy at tick `t` (the trajectories + their group-relative advantages) |
| `J(θ)` | the GRPO objective; `∇J(θ) = g(θ; D(π_θ))` is the **on-policy** gradient |
| `g(θ; D)` | the GRPO surrogate gradient evaluated **at weights `θ`** over data drawn from distribution `D` (data and weights decoupled on purpose) |
| `M ≜ g(θ_{t−K}; D_{t−K})` | the anchor gradient: the **clean, full, DP-mean** gradient at the stale weights `θ_{t−K}` on the **paired** stale rollouts those weights generated (β_anc=0 ⇒ M is the instantaneous stale gradient; `runs/DELAY_ANALYSIS/staleness_theorist.md` §1.1, `[[anchor-gradient-ema-beta0-grpo]]`). Ratio≡1 clean PG loss because data and weights are paired (EXP-29 paired replay). |
| `G_comp(t)` | the fast circuit's rank-r-compressed gradient at `θ_t` (biased but functional) |
| `g_t ≜ g(θ_t; D_t) = ∇J(θ_t)` | shorthand for the live on-policy gradient |
| `H(θ) = ∇²_θ 𝔼_{τ∼D}[ℓ_θ(τ)]` | Hessian of the surrogate **w.r.t. the explicit `θ` argument**, holding the data distribution fixed (see A2) |
| `η` | optimizer step size; `ū` | mean per-tick parameter update norm (the Adam-preconditioned, grad-clipped update) |

**A1 (gradient smoothness in the weight argument).** For a fixed data distribution
`D`, the map `θ ↦ g(θ; D)` is `L_H`-Lipschitz, i.e. `‖∇²_θ 𝔼_{τ∼D}[ℓ_θ(τ)]‖ ≤ L_H`
locally along the training trajectory. This is the standard `β`-smoothness used in
non-convex SGD analysis. It bounds **only** the parameter-point gap, never the
distribution gap (which is the whole point of §1).

**A2 (the two derivatives are distinct).** GRPO's gradient has two `θ`-dependencies:
(i) the explicit `∇_θ log π_θ` factor in the score, and (ii) the *sampling*
dependence — the trajectories and the group-relative advantages `A(τ)` are drawn from
`π_θ`. `H` in A1 is the curvature of (i) **with the data held fixed**. The sampling
dependence (ii) is *not* a smoothness object; moving it is the off-policy term `Δ_dist`.
Conflating the two is the central error this note exists to prevent.

**A3 (paired-replay validity).** Because the anchor replays the exact batch `θ_{t−K}`
generated, the importance ratio inside `M` is identically 1: `M` is the *exact,
self-consistent, low-variance* on-policy gradient of the **defunct** policy
`π_{θ_{t−K}}` — a valid PG estimate of `∇J(θ_{t−K})`, not a noisy one
(`reports/comm-eff-grpo/why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html` §0; EXP-29). Validity is real;
it is validity *of the wrong thing*.

**A4 (no in-program SFT run).** Claims contrasting GRPO with SFT rest on the method's
prior SL-validation plus the structural argument that SFT's data distribution is
exogenous and frozen; there is no in-house SFT training run
(`reports/comm-eff-grpo/why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html` claim #17). Flagged as
mechanism-grounded, not run-backed, wherever used.

## §1 Two-gap decomposition (rigorous)

We want the error the merger actually injects: the difference between the gradient the
merger transports (`M`, a function of the *old* policy) and the gradient the optimizer
*should* be following at `θ_t` (`g_t = ∇J(θ_t)`). Define the **anchor staleness error**

$$
e_K(t) \;\triangleq\; g_t - M \;=\; g(\theta_t; D_t) \;-\; g(\theta_{t-K}; D_{t-K}).
$$

Insert and subtract the **mixed term** `g(θ_{t−K}; D_t)` — the gradient at the stale
weights but evaluated on *current-policy* data. This is the unique split that isolates
the weight move from the data move:

$$
\boxed{\;
e_K \;=\;
\underbrace{\big[\,g(\theta_t; D_t) - g(\theta_{t-K}; D_t)\,\big]}_{\textbf{(a) parameter-point gap }\;e^{\mathrm{param}}_K}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\big[\,g(\theta_{t-K}; D_t) - g(\theta_{t-K}; D_{t-K})\,\big]}_{\textbf{(b) distribution gap }\;\Delta_{\mathrm{dist}}}.
\;}
\tag{1}
$$

These two terms are governed by **completely different mathematics**. (a) is bounded by
smoothness; (b) is not.

### §1.1 Gap (a) — the parameter-point gap is curvature-bounded

Term (a) holds the data distribution `D_t` fixed and moves only the weights from
`θ_{t−K}` to `θ_t`. By the fundamental theorem of calculus applied to the
(data-fixed) gradient field `θ ↦ g(θ; D_t)` (which is the integral-of-Hessian form):

$$
e^{\mathrm{param}}_K
= g(\theta_t; D_t) - g(\theta_{t-K}; D_t)
= \int_0^1 H\!\big(\theta_{t-K} + s\,\Delta\theta;\, D_t\big)\,\Delta\theta\; ds,
\qquad \Delta\theta \triangleq \theta_t - \theta_{t-K}.
\tag{2}
$$

Taking norms with A1 and bounding the cumulative parameter motion by the per-step
updates:

$$
\boxed{\;
\big\| e^{\mathrm{param}}_K \big\|
\;\le\; L_H\,\|\Delta\theta\|
\;\le\; L_H \sum_{j=t-K}^{t-1}\|\theta_{j+1}-\theta_j\|
\;\approx\; L_H\, K\, \eta\, \overline{\|u\|}.
\;}
\tag{3}
$$

So gap (a) grows **at most linearly in K** along a smooth trajectory: a stale gradient
is, to first order, a *rotated* version of the current one, and the rotation is the
Hessian-vector product `H·Δθ` (`reports/anchor-future-projection/theory-and-literature-2026-06-22.md`
§3 makes exactly this identification: "the rotation IS the Hessian-vector product").
**This is the only gap SFT has, and it is the only gap any curvature/extrapolation
correction can address** (the architect's R_K ≈ H·Δθ route attacks *this* term — see §5).

> **Consistency check against EXP-38.** EXP-38 measures `cos(g_t, g_{t−k})` with
> **norm ratio ≈ 1.0 at every lag** (`runs/EXP-38/verdict.md` H1; `reports/anchor-future-projection/…`
> §1: "(almost) a pure rotation, not a rescale"). A norm-preserving rotation is exactly
> the signature of (2) when `H·Δθ` is dominated by its *skew* action on `g` rather than
> its symmetric (rescaling) action — consistent with gap (a) being a rotation. But the
> rotation is *large for a tiny* `‖Δθ‖` (`‖θ_t−θ_{t−1}‖≈0.0009` already drops cosine to
> 0.51; same source), which says `L_H` is large/ill-conditioned. A large `L_H` makes
> the bound (3) loose, **but it does not by itself explain the speed of decorrelation**
> — see §3.3, where gap (b) is shown to dominate.

### §1.2 Gap (b) — the distribution gap is NOT bounded by smoothness

Term (b) holds the weights fixed at `θ_{t−K}` and moves only the data distribution
from `D_{t−K} = D(π_{θ_{t−K}})` to `D_t = D(π_{θ_t})`:

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{dist}}
= g(\theta_{t-K}; D_t) - g(\theta_{t-K}; D_{t-K})
= \mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim \pi_{\theta_t}}\!\big[\nabla_\theta \ell_{\theta_{t-K}}(\tau)\big]
- \mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim \pi_{\theta_{t-K}}}\!\big[\nabla_\theta \ell_{\theta_{t-K}}(\tau)\big].
\tag{4}
$$

This is the **off-policy term**. There is **no smoothness constant** that bounds it,
because the object that moved is the *measure* the expectation is taken under, not the
point of evaluation. Writing it as a single expectation under the old policy with an
importance weight `w(τ) = π_{θ_t}(τ)/π_{θ_{t−K}}(τ)`:

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{dist}}
= \mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim \pi_{\theta_{t-K}}}\!\Big[\big(w(\tau)-1\big)\,\nabla_\theta \ell_{\theta_{t-K}}(\tau)\Big].
\tag{5}
$$

`Δ_dist` vanishes only if `w ≡ 1` (the policy did not move) or if the integrand is
orthogonal to `(w−1)`. Neither holds in GRPO: the policy moves every tick, and for an
**autoregressive** policy over an `O(10²)`-token response the per-trajectory ratio
`w(τ) = ∏_{s} π_{θ_t}(a_s|·)/π_{θ_{t−K}}(a_s|·)` is a product of hundreds of factors,
so even a small per-token KL compounds into a `w` that is heavy-tailed and far from 1.
The trajectories `π_{θ_{t−K}}` produced may lie **off the support** of `π_{θ_t}`
(a different mode), so `M` rewards/penalizes sequences `π_{θ_t}` would never emit
(`reports/comm-eff-grpo/why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html` §0.2). **Gap (b) is the term
that makes the anchor an off-policy gradient, and it is unbounded by curvature.**

### §1.3 What SFT deletes: gap (b) ≡ 0

Under supervised fine-tuning the data distribution is an **exogenous frozen dataset**
`D_0`: there is no policy in the sampling loop. Then `D_t = D_{t−K} = D_0` for all `t`,
so by construction

$$
\Delta^{\mathrm{SFT}}_{\mathrm{dist}}
= g(\theta_{t-K}; D_0) - g(\theta_{t-K}; D_0) = 0,
\qquad
e^{\mathrm{SFT}}_K = e^{\mathrm{param}}_K \;\;\le\; L_H K \eta \overline{\|u\|}.
\tag{6}
$$

The stale SFT gradient is a *lagged estimate of the same descent direction on the same
objective*, error bounded by curvature and telescoping under error feedback. **The
single deleted term `Δ_dist → 0` is the entire difference between "the method works in
SL" and "it ignites/drags in GRPO"** (`reports/comm-eff-grpo/why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html`
§0.4, claim #17; mechanism-grounded per A4). Note this is a statement about the *data
sampling loop*, not about non-stationarity per se: a non-stationary but *exogenous*
target (e.g. curriculum SFT) still has `Δ_dist=0` for the anchor because the data does
not depend on `θ`. GRPO's distinguishing feature is that `θ` **closes the loop on its
own data**.

### §1.4 The honest caveat on `e^{param}` vs `Δ_dist` decomposition

The split (1) is exact but the *labelling* of "(a) = curvature-bounded, (b) = off-
policy" requires A1–A2. In particular `e^{param}` as defined uses the **current** data
`D_t`; one could equally split through `g(θ_t; D_{t−K})`, giving a different (a)/(b)
attribution. I chose the `g(θ_{t−K}; D_t)` mixed term because it makes (a) a *pure
weight move under fixed data* — the only form to which A1's smoothness applies cleanly.
The alternative split leaks policy-dependence into (a). This choice is stated so the
architect's curvature route is attacking the well-defined term (2), not an artifact of
the decomposition.

## §2 Off-policy as bias, not variance (rigorous)

The decisive distinction is **what kind of error** staleness becomes. There are two
ways to use old trajectories; they trade along opposite axes.

### §2.1 Textbook IS-corrected off-policy: unbiased for `∇J(θ_t)`, high variance

The standard off-policy policy gradient corrects the behavior/target mismatch with an
importance weight, yielding an estimator that is **unbiased for the current gradient**:

$$
\widehat{g}^{\mathrm{IS}}_t
= \mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim \pi_{\theta_{t-K}}}\!\Big[\, w(\tau)\,\nabla_\theta \log \pi_{\theta_t}(\tau)\,A(\tau)\,\Big]
= \nabla J(\theta_t),
\qquad w(\tau)=\frac{\pi_{\theta_t}(\tau)}{\pi_{\theta_{t-K}}(\tau)}.
\tag{7}
$$

The price is variance. The variance of a (self-normalized or ordinary) IS estimator is
controlled by the divergence between behavior and target. The canonical bound (Metelli
et al., POIS, NeurIPS 2018 / *Importance Sampling Techniques for Policy Optimization*,
JMLR 2020) states the variance in terms of the **exponentiated 2-Rényi divergence**:

$$
\mathrm{Var}\big[\widehat{g}^{\mathrm{IS}}\big] \;\propto\; d_2\big(\pi_{\theta_t}\,\|\,\pi_{\theta_{t-K}}\big) - 1,
\qquad
d_2(p\|q) = \mathbb{E}_{q}\!\big[(p/q)^2\big] = \exp\big(D_2(p\|q)\big),
\tag{8}
$$

i.e. the **second moment of the importance weight**, which is `exp` of the 2-Rényi
divergence (verified via web search of the POIS/Metelli line:
`https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume21/20-124/20-124.pdf`,
`https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.06098`; the exact constant is the standard result, not
re-derived here). The practical heuristic `Var ~ exp(D_KL)` follows: as the policy
drifts, IS variance blows up *exponentially* in divergence. Clipping/truncating `w`
(PPO, V-trace) **bounds the variance at the cost of reintroducing bias**, and that bias
itself grows with divergence (`runs/DELAY_ANALYSIS/staleness_theorist.md` §1.3 point 1).
**But throughout, the estimand is `∇J(θ_t)` — the *current* gradient.** Staleness has
been converted into *variance about the right target*.

### §2.2 The anchor: low variance, biased for the WRONG target

The anchor does the **opposite trade**. It applies **no** importance weight (A3). So it
is not a noisy estimate of `∇J(θ_t)`; it is a *precise, low-variance* estimate of
`∇J(θ_{t−K})` — the gradient of a **defunct** policy. Formally, taking expectations,
the anchor's **bias** as an estimator of the current gradient is exactly the staleness
error of §1:

$$
\mathbb{E}[M] - \nabla J(\theta_t) = g(\theta_{t-K}; D_{t-K}) - g(\theta_t; D_t) = -\,e_K,
\qquad
\mathrm{Var}[M] \approx 0 \;\text{(paired replay, A3).}
\tag{9}
$$

Contrast the two error budgets as *bias–variance decompositions of the MSE about the
current gradient* `𝔼‖·−∇J(θ_t)‖²`:

| estimator | bias (about `∇J(θ_t)`) | variance | error type |
|---|---|---|---|
| IS-corrected (7) | 0 | `∝ exp(D_2(π_t‖π_{t−K}))` | **variance** (zero-mean, averages out across ticks) |
| clipped IS (PPO/V-trace) | grows with divergence | bounded by clip | mixed |
| **anchor M** (A3) | `‖e_K‖` (grows with K, §1) | `≈ 0` | **bias** (fixed-direction, does **not** average out) |

### §2.3 Why "low variance + persistence" is worse than noise — the carrier condition

The optimizer integrates its corrections. Over `T` ticks the cumulative drift the
correction injects is `Σ_{t} (correction_t)`. For a **zero-mean** noise term `ξ_t`
(the IS case) the partial sums grow like a random walk, `‖Σ ξ_t‖ = O(√T · σ)` — it
**averages out** relative to the `O(T)` signal. For a **persistent biased** term with a
stable direction (the anchor case), the partial sums grow **linearly**:

$$
\Big\|\sum_{t=1}^{T} \text{correction}_t\Big\|
\;\sim\;
\begin{cases}
O(\sqrt{T}\,\sigma), & \text{zero-mean noise (cancels),}\\[4pt]
\lambda\, T\, \|\bar e\|, & \text{persistent biased pull (integrates).}
\end{cases}
\tag{10}
$$

The lower line is the **carrier condition**: a fixed-direction force of per-tick size
`λ‖ē‖` summed over `T` ticks produces an `O(T)` displacement of the optimizer along the
biased direction (`reports/comm-eff-grpo/why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html` §2: heuristic
`‖Σ_t e_t‖ ~ λ·T·‖G‖`; issue #30 carrier law). The decisive empirical fact is that the
**cross-run collapse discriminator is *merger-carrier presence*, not gradient noise**:
plain PowerSGD (same noisy-gradient class, *no* merger folding M in) trains cleanly on
the identical substrate, while the merger spikes (`[[canonical-anchor-comm-eff-base]]`
post-mortem; `why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html` claim #9). That isolates the damage to the
*persistent, low-variance, biased carrier* M injects — exactly the lower line of (10) —
not to the variance of the compressed gradient.

This is the formal content of "low variance plus persistence is more dangerous than
noise": **noise is the upper line of (10) and self-cancels; a precise estimate of the
wrong gradient is the lower line and accumulates.** Lowering the variance of M (which
paired replay did, EXP-29) makes the carrier *cleaner and more coherent*, i.e. moves it
*further* toward the lower line — a more reliable wrong-direction pull, not a fix
(`why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html` §0.5, claim #14, marked Hypothesized there; here it is
the direct corollary of (10)).

### §2.4 The two symptoms are two projections of the same biased carrier

The merger geometry decides where the accumulated bias (10) is deposited; the bias
itself is shared (`reports/comm-eff-grpo/why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html` §2.1). With the
GRPO surface offering a reward-flat "longer-response" manifold (verifier-only reward,
group-normalized advantage, no-KL no-entropy, token-mean aggregation with ~86× tail
amplification, `[[exp25-collapse-gradient-flow]]`):

- **`signed_ema` (sign-replace), `G_corr = α·G_noisy + (1−α)·|G_noisy|·sign(M)`.** On
  coordinates where `sign(M) ≠ sign(G_noisy)`, the merger flips magnitude onto the
  *stale* sign, injecting `(2α−1)|G_noisy|` of reversed force per disagreeing
  coordinate. At small K the disagreements are a near-symmetric coin-flip of two
  estimators of a near-zero gradient and **cancel across coordinates** (upper line of
  (10)); at large K the *systematic* `Δ_dist` term adds **correlated, same-direction**
  flips that **integrate** (lower line) — a sign-SGD-with-stale-sign sharpening spiral
  → ignition (`runs/DELAY_ANALYSIS/staleness_theorist.md` §2.2).
- **`delayed_ef` (additive), `G_corr = G_comp + λ·δ`.** Direction-preserving, so it
  cannot sharpen into a sign-spiral; the persistent biased `δ` instead acts as a
  wrong-direction *drag* whose magnitude tracks `‖θ_t − θ_{t−K}‖` (this is the EXP-37E
  `grad_norm` 2→55 with flat length: `why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html` claim #19).

Same `e_K`; sign-replace rectifies it into a ratchet, additive deposits it as drag.

## §3 The `K>τ` stability condition (formal) tied to the measured budget

### §3.1 Definition of the policy-drift horizon τ

Both gaps in (1) grow with K, but only gap (b) is unbounded, so τ should be defined off
the *gradient distribution's* decorrelation, which is dominated by (b) (shown in §3.3).
Operationalize via the directional agreement of the stale and live gradients:

> **Definition (policy-drift horizon).** Fix a tolerance `c⋆ ∈ (0,1)`. The policy-drift
> horizon is
> $$ \tau(c^\star) \;\triangleq\; \min\{\,k \ge 1 : \rho(k) < c^\star\,\}, \qquad
> \rho(k) \triangleq \frac{\langle g_t,\, g_{t-k}\rangle}{\|g_t\|\,\|g_{t-k}\|} \;=\; \cos\angle(g_t, g_{t-k}). $$
> τ is the lag at which the stale dense gradient's *direction* has decorrelated past
> `c⋆`. (Equivalently one may define it from a per-token KL or 2-Rényi threshold on
> `π_{θ_t}‖π_{θ_{t−k}}`; the cosine form is what EXP-38 measured.)

`ρ(k)` is the natural staleness statistic because the Adam preconditioner and grad-clip
make the **direction** of the step load-bearing, not its magnitude
(`runs/DELAY_ANALYSIS/staleness_theorist.md` §1.2; `[[exp25-collapse-gradient-flow]]`).
A complementary **sign-agreement** statistic `s(k) = 𝔼[𝟙{sign(g_t)=sign(g_{t−k})}]`
is what `signed_ema` actually consumes; `s(k)→0.5` is the merger's "pure noise" point.

### §3.2 The stability condition

> **Stability condition (informal-formal).** The stale-anchor merger is stable iff the
> realized staleness stays inside the horizon, `K < τ(c⋆)`, for an appropriate `c⋆`. As
> `K → τ` from below the system is *near-critical* (transient self-correcting
> excursions); for `K > τ` the merger injects a force that is decorrelated-to-anti-
> correlated with the descent direction (`ρ(K) ≤ 0`), and the failure probability is
> **monotone increasing in `K/τ`.**

The mechanism behind monotonicity: the per-tick carrier dose is the *misaligned*
component of M, whose size grows as `ρ(K)` falls (a near-orthogonal M is almost all
carrier). The integrated displacement (10) is therefore increasing in `K/τ`, and once
it crosses the length-ratchet's self-sustaining threshold the run ignites
(`signed_ema`) or fails to converge (`delayed_ef`). This is a *threshold-crossing of an
integral*, which is why onset is **late** (steps 50–100), not immediate: the small
biased increment must accumulate past the token-mean dilution threshold before the loop
self-sustains (`runs/DELAY_ANALYSIS/staleness_theorist.md` §2.3; `why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html`
§2). A larger `K/τ` raises the per-tick increment, so the integral crosses *within* the
100-step horizon at 20/20 where it stays sub-threshold (censored) at 5/5.

I deliberately leave `c⋆` and the threshold *unquantified as a universal constant*: the
EXP-38 data (next) shows τ is **task-dependent**, so a single numeric `c⋆` would
over-claim. The condition's content is the **ordering** `K<τ ⇒ stable, K>τ ⇒ fail` and
the **monotonicity** in `K/τ`, both of which the measured wall obeys.

### §3.3 Tying τ to EXP-38 — and resolving the weight-vs-distribution paradox

EXP-38 (`runs/EXP-38/verdict.md`; numbers independently reproduced from raw tensors per
its §"Independent verification") gives the directional decay directly. Lag `k` in
optimizer ticks:

| `k` (ticks) | GSM8K `ρ(k)` | GSM8K sign `s(k)` | GSM8K ‖·‖ratio | Big-Math `ρ(k)` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1  | **0.507** | 0.63 | 0.89 | **0.018** |
| 2  | 0.464 | 0.61 | 0.99 | 0.011 |
| 5  | **0.176** | 0.54 | 0.92 | 0.011 |
| 10 | 0.023 | 0.51 | 1.01 | 0.005 |
| 20 | −0.008 | 0.50 (chance) | 0.98 | 0.004 |

**Reading off τ (GSM8K, easy):** with a natural `c⋆ ≈ 0.35`, `ρ` is already below it by
`k≈3` (interpolating 0.464→0.176 between k2 and k5), so **τ_GSM8K ≈ 3–4 ticks**
(≈1.5–2 global steps). At the *stable* operating point `K=5` the anchor sees only
`ρ≈0.176` — already weakly aligned — which is why 5/5 is *near*-dense but not dense, and
sits right at the edge of the horizon. At `K=20`, `ρ(20) = −0.008` and `s(20)=0.50`:
the stale gradient is **directionally dead / anti-aligned and sign-random** — pure
carrier — which is exactly the `K>τ` regime the stability condition predicts must fail
(`runs/EXP-38/verdict.md` "Located unsafe-staleness knees": "5/5 survived because the
small frequent dose rode a still-weakly-aligned (cos≈0.18) gradient; 20/20 broke
because it injected a near-orthogonal/biased (cos≈0) force").

**Reading off τ (Big-Math, hard):** `ρ(1) = 0.018`, `s(k)≈0.5` at every lag — the
gradient is **decorrelated at lag 1**. So **τ_Big-Math ≈ 0**: there is *no* usable
staleness window; even a fresh 1-tick-old anchor is already off-policy noise. This is
why GSM8K parity did **not** transfer to Big-Math (`[[bigmath-second-dataset-readiness]]`):
the staleness budget is genuinely task-dependent, not a tunable constant.

**The paradox and its resolution.** The *weight* trajectory drifts **slowly** — weight
half-drift ≈ **7.9 global steps ≈ 16 ticks** (`runs/EXP-38/verdict.md` H2) — yet the
*gradient direction* decorrelates in **≈3 ticks** (GSM8K) or **<1 tick** (Big-Math),
and the GRPO behavior signals `pg_clipfrac / ppo_kl / response_length` reach half-drift
in **≈1 global step** (same source, H2). If the only gap were (a), `‖e^{param}_K‖ ≤
L_H K η ‖ū‖` would track the *slow* weight drift and τ would be `O(16 ticks)`. It is
not — τ is `≪` the weight half-drift. **Therefore gap (b) `Δ_dist` dominates the
staleness error, not curvature·‖Δθ‖.** EXP-38 states this as its H2 verdict
("the dangerous term is the **distribution gap** (gap 2), not curvature×‖Δθ‖").

This is the single most load-bearing empirical fact for the whole program, so state its
logic precisely:

1. Weights move a tiny amount per tick (`‖Δθ‖≈0.0009`), and slowly cumulatively
   (half-drift 16 ticks). Gap (a) is bounded by this and is therefore *small and slow*.
2. The gradient's *direction* nonetheless decorrelates in ~3 ticks. A small slow weight
   move cannot produce that via (a) alone unless `L_H` is enormous — and even an
   enormous `L_H` produces a *rotation that tracks `‖Δθ‖`*, i.e. still slow.
3. The fast-decorrelating component must therefore come from the term **not** bounded by
   `‖Δθ‖`: the data distribution `D(π_θ)`, which reshapes (new modes, new advantage
   normalization) far faster than the weights themselves move. That is `Δ_dist`.
4. **Corollary for the architect (critical).** A curvature/extrapolation correction
   `R_K ≈ H·Δθ` can only repair gap (a). Since gap (b) dominates the measured
   decorrelation, **even a perfect curvature correction cannot restore `ρ(K)` to ≈1 at
   `K>τ`** — it removes the small slow part and leaves the large fast part. This caps
   what *any* deterministic un-rotation of M can buy and is the hand-off to §5 / the
   diagonal-trap debate. (EXP-38's own next-method recommendation draws the same line:
   "Do **not** reweight/accumulate/error-feedback a stale gradient … compress in
   **activation** space"; `runs/EXP-38/verdict.md` §"Next-method recommendation".)

> **Honest caveat on the EXP-38 numbers.** n=1 per task, 75 steps; small-lag (k≤5)
> cosines are sampled only in *early* training (a capture-schedule confound disclosed in
> every EXP-38 report). The *ordering* (τ_GSM8K ≈ few ticks ≫ τ_Big-Math ≈ 0; gap (b)
> dominates) is robust and independently reproduced; the exact `c⋆`-dependent τ value
> is approximate. I therefore make claims about ordering and dominance, not about a
> universal numeric τ.

## §4 Off-policy PG correction survey — the central question

**The load-bearing question (from the charter):** can any off-policy correction produce
a *principled reweight* of a **stale on-policy gradient** when we have **no fresh
samples from `π_{θ_t}`**?

Before surveying methods, isolate **the structural obstruction**, because it kills most
of the field at once and is the actual finding.

### §4.0 The aggregation obstruction (the key result of this section)

Every importance-sampling family — vanilla IS, per-decision IS, truncated/clipped IS,
V-trace, Retrace(λ) — forms a **per-sample (or per-step) ratio** and reweights the
**summand before aggregation**:

$$
\widehat{g}^{\mathrm{corrected}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \underbrace{w_i}_{\text{ratio}} \cdot \underbrace{\nabla_\theta \log \pi_{\theta_t}(\tau_i)\,A_i}_{\text{per-sample score}}.
\tag{11}
$$

This requires, for **each** trajectory/timestep `i`: (i) the *current* policy's
probability `π_{θ_t}(a_i|s_i)` to form `w_i` (and, for V-trace/Retrace, to form the
target as well), and (ii) the **un-aggregated per-sample score**.

**The anchor delivers neither.** `M` is a *single gradient vector* — the per-sample
scores have **already been summed and DP-mean all-reduced** (and PowerSGD-compressed for
the fast circuit). The trajectories are gone; only their aggregate gradient survives.
Formally, reweighting is a **nonlinear** operation on the summands,

$$
\sum_i w_i\, x_i \;\ne\; \Big(\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i w_i\Big)\sum_i x_i
\quad\text{unless } w_i \text{ is constant across } i,
\tag{12}
$$

so a per-sample ratio **cannot be applied post-hoc to an aggregated gradient** — there
is no `w_i` to multiply each `x_i` by once the `x_i` are summed. The only post-hoc
correction available is a **single scalar** `w̄` applied to the whole vector `M`. A
scalar rescale is precisely a *staleness-scaled dose* (Zhang et al. 2016 staleness-aware
ASGD; `paper-bibliography.md` "Staleness-Aware Async-SGD"), and it is in the architect's
candidate set — but it is **not** an importance correction: it shrinks the wrong-
direction vector, it does not re-aim it. And critically, the IS variance blow-up (8) is
**not even the obstruction here** — we never get to pay variance because we cannot form
the per-sample ratio at all. The obstruction is **informational** (the un-aggregated
scores and the fresh-`π_{θ_t}` probabilities are absent), upstream of the variance
problem.

> This is the precise, rigorous answer to the charter's "be rigorous about whether the
> ratio can even be applied post-hoc to an aggregated gradient": **no**, by (12), unless
> the ratio is constant across samples — and a constant ratio is a scalar dose, not an
> IS correction.

Two further structural points sharpen this:

- **We have no fresh `π_{θ_t}` samples by design.** The whole premise of the comm-eff
  setting is that the fast circuit moved on; the anchor is the *only* full-gradient
  signal and it is computed on `π_{θ_{t−K}}`'s trajectories. Even if we *kept* the
  un-aggregated stale scores, forming `w_i` still needs `π_{θ_t}(τ_i)` — a forward pass
  of the *current* policy over the *stale* trajectories. That is an extra full forward
  pass we do not do (and it is exactly the "missing old/new logits" problem flagged by
  Guan et al. 2026, `paper-bibliography.md`).
- **GRPO's advantage is itself group-relative on the stale batch.** Even with logits,
  `A_i` was computed by normalizing within the stale group; re-deriving the *current*
  policy's group baseline would require re-rolling out under `π_{θ_t}` — fresh samples
  again. So even per-sample IS would only fix the *score* factor, not the *advantage*
  factor. (Yao et al. 2025/2026 "Group-Relative REINFORCE Is Secretly Off-Policy",
  arXiv:2509.24203 — verified abstract — shows GRPO's within-group-mean baseline *does*
  admit an off-policy interpretation, but their remedies are **regularizing the update
  and shaping the data distribution**, i.e. controlling `D` going forward, **not**
  reweighting an already-aggregated stale gradient. This *confirms* the obstruction:
  the literature's fix for off-policy GRPO is data/regularization control, not post-hoc
  gradient reweighting.)

### §4.1 Method-by-method

I state, for each: the mechanism, what it *requires*, and the verdict on the charter
question. "Requires fresh `π_{θ_t}`?" and "Applicable to an aggregated gradient?" are the
two decisive columns.

| Method | Mechanism | Needs current-π probs? | Needs un-aggregated samples? | Verdict for stale **aggregated** anchor gradient |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vanilla IS / per-decision IS** (Precup 2000; standard) | reweight each (sub-)trajectory by `w(τ)` resp. prefix ratio `∏_{s≤k} w_s` | **yes** | **yes** | **NO.** (11)+(12): no per-sample ratio survives aggregation; needs a fresh forward pass for `π_{θ_t}`. PDIS is *worse* for autoregressive LLMs — prefix products are even higher-variance (Lei et al. 2026, "A Step Back: Prefix Importance Ratio…", arXiv:2601.22718, warns token-level/prefix ratios are unstable under large off-policyness). |
| **Truncated / clipped IS** (PPO clip; `min(ρ, ρ̄)`) | clip `w_i` to bound variance, accept bias | **yes** | **yes** | **NO** for the same reason; clipping is a *modification of `w_i`*, which we cannot form. | **V-trace** (IMPALA, Espeholt et al. 2018, arXiv:1802.01561) | truncated IS weights `ρ_s=min(ρ̄, π/μ)`, `c_s=min(c̄, π/μ)` correct an **actor-learner policy lag**; corrects the *value target* and the PG | **yes** (ratios are `π_target/μ_behavior` per step) | **yes** (per-step trajectory) | **NO** for the aggregated gradient. ### §4.2 What the 2025–2026 async-LLM-RL line actually does (and why it agrees with §4.0)

The very recent async-RL-for-LLMs papers in `paper-bibliography.md` are the most direct
precedent for *stable training under stale rollouts*, so it matters what lever they
pull. Without exception (per their verified abstracts) they operate on **live,
un-aggregated, per-token logprobs of retained trajectories** and use **scalar/sequence
trust weighting or LR scaling**, never a post-hoc reweight of an aggregated gradient:

- **VCPO / "Stable Asynchrony"** (Huang et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.17616, abstract
  verified): diagnoses collapse via **effective sample size (ESS)** and stabilizes with
  **ESS-scaled learning rate** + a closed-form min-variance baseline. ESS-scaled LR is a
  *scalar dose* — exactly the only post-hoc lever §4.0 permits — and it is computed from
  the *live* importance weights, which they have because they keep the rollouts and
  re-score with the current policy.
- **VESPO** (Shen et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.10693, `paper-bibliography.md`): **sequence-
  level** IS reshaping for stale LLM RL — again a per-sequence ratio on retained
  trajectories, motivated by the instability of token-level/prefix ratios (cf. Lei et
  al. 2026).
- **GAC** (Xu et al. 2026, arXiv:2603.01501, `paper-bibliography.md`): diagnoses
  **stale-aligned gradient overshoot** and applies **gradient-alignment projection
  control** — i.e. projects/clips the *direction* of the (live) stale gradient. This is
  notable because it is the one method that acts on a *gradient* rather than per-sample
  ratios, and its lever is **projection/alignment control**, not importance reweighting
  — consistent with §4.0 (you cannot IS-correct an aggregated gradient; you can only
  rescale/project it).
- **GIPO** (Lu et al. 2026, arXiv:2603.03955): smooth (Gaussian) **trust weighting** as
  a soft alternative to hard clipping for stale replay — again a per-sample soft ratio.
- **Tyurin et al. 2025/2026** "Asynchronous Policy Gradient Aggregation"
  (arXiv:2509.24305): aggregates **policy gradients** across async workers; the abstract
  (verified) does not disclose the staleness-correction mechanism, so I cannot confirm
  whether it IS-corrects or scalar-doses. **Flagged: mechanism unverified from abstract;
  the architect/async-scholar should fetch the full PDF.**

**Net for the survey:** the entire toolbox that *could* re-aim a stale gradient (IS,
PDIS, V-trace, Retrace) is **inapplicable to an aggregated gradient with no fresh
`π_{θ_t}` samples** (§4.0, (12)). The methods that *do* stabilize stale-rollout RL keep
the trajectories live and apply **scalar/sequence trust weighting, LR scaling, or
direction projection** — none of which is a principled reweight of `M`; they are
*dose/clip/project* operations, which is the architect's territory (staleness-scaled
dose, trust-region cap, alignment projection). **There is no off-policy PG correction
that re-aims the anchor gradient under our constraints.** The honest options are:
(1) shrink the dose as `K/τ` grows (scalar, admissible); (2) project/clip the wrong-
direction component (GAC-style, admissible, but still σ(M)-bounded — §5); (3) repair the
*curvature* part of gap (a) by extrapolation (the only route that could exceed a scalar
dose — §5); or (4) **change what is transported** so the off-policy gap never forms —
the EXP-38 recommendation to move to activation-space compression and demote the anchor
to a slow Q-calibrator (`runs/EXP-38/verdict.md`).

### §4.3 A note on "keep the logits" (the one way IS could re-enter)

The obstruction in §4.0 has two halves: aggregation (12) and missing fresh-`π_{θ_t}`
probabilities. One *could* in principle break the second half by storing the stale
per-sample scores and the stale logits, then doing **one extra forward pass of `π_{θ_t}`
over the stale trajectories** to form per-token `w_i` (the "missing old logits" fix,
Guan et al. 2026, arXiv:2605.12070, `paper-bibliography.md`). But this (a) re-introduces
the full per-sample machinery the comm-eff setting exists to *avoid* (it is no longer a
single transported gradient — it is full off-policy GRPO), (b) re-incurs the IS variance
blow-up (8) that scales `exp(D_2(π_t‖π_{t−K}))`, which at `K>τ` (where `ρ→0`,
`D_2` large) is exactly the regime where clip-induced bias returns, and (c) is **not the
problem this team is scoped to solve** — the substrate is settled as a transported
aggregated gradient. I record it as the *theoretical* escape from §4.0's second half,
note it collapses the comm-eff premise, and defer to the architect on whether a
*partial* logit-keeping variant is worth a kill-test. My verdict: it converts the
problem into ordinary async off-policy GRPO (the VCPO/VESPO/GIPO regime), which is a
different research line, not a correction to *this* anchor.

## §5 σ(M) interaction — the parity ceiling and the curvature hand-off

### §5.1 Pure reweighting / correction stays inside σ(M)

Let `σ(M) ≜ σ(g(θ_t), g(θ_{t−K}))` be the sigma-algebra generated by the current and
the K-stale dense gradient means (the only dense information the anchor circuit
exposes), and let dense-Adam be the control. The σ(M) ceiling
(`[[surpass-dense-sigma-m-ceiling-and-routes]]`; `reports/anchor-future-projection/…`
§4) states:

> **Any deterministic `Φ(G_comp, M)` is σ(M)-measurable, hence its expected update is a
> measurable function of `(g_t, g_{t−K})`, and therefore caps at dense parity.**

Every correction surveyed in §4 that is *admissible* for the aggregated gradient is such
a `Φ`:

- a **scalar staleness dose** `w̄(K)·M` is σ(M)-measurable (it is `M` times a constant);
- a **trust-region cap / clip** on the correction is a deterministic clamp of
  `Φ(G_comp, M)` — σ(M)-measurable;
- a **direction projection** (GAC-style) `P·M` with `P` formed from `(G_comp, M)` is
  σ(M)-measurable;
- a **fixed-linear extrapolation** `M + β(g_{t−K} − g_{t−2K})` (DC-ASGD-style finite
  difference using only past dense gradients) is σ(M)-measurable — it is a deterministic
  function of past dense means.

**All of these are PARITY-at-larger-K levers**: at best they let the merger reconstruct
the dense direction it can *still see through the staleness channel*, raising the usable
K toward but not beyond dense. They cannot inject information dense-Adam lacks. This is
why the EXP-31/#33 tournament (perturbation, δ-momentum, adaptive-λ, control-variate,
sub-basis, β-averaging) was **all-null for surpass** (`.claude/GOAL.md`; `00-plan.md`
§8): each was a σ(M)-measurable `Φ`.

### §5.2 The only escape my analysis sees: curvature / 2nd-order — and its ceiling

To **surpass** dense one must inject information **outside** σ(M)
(`[[surpass-dense-sigma-m-ceiling-and-routes]]` enumerates the three categories:
curvature/2nd-order; conversion-positive exploration that moves the greedy argmax;
cross-rank 2nd moment). From the off-policy-theory vantage, the route that *also* repairs
the staleness error is **curvature/2nd-order**:

- The parameter-point gap (2) is *exactly* `H·Δθ` to first order. A correction that
  estimates `H` and adds `H·Δθ` back uses the **Hessian**, which dense-**Adam's diagonal
  `v_t` does not contain** (off-diagonal curvature). That is information outside σ(M) (it
  is not a function of the gradient *means* alone; it is the gradient's *local
  derivative*). This is route **R5 (beyond-diagonal curvature)** of the surpass memo and
  the `R_K ≈ H·Δθ` extrapolation idea (`reports/anchor-future-projection/…` §3–4).

- **But §3.3 caps it hard.** Curvature repairs only gap (a), and gap (b) **dominates**
  the measured decorrelation. So a curvature/extrapolation correction can:
  - **raise the budget toward τ_param** (where curvature-bounded gap (a) would set the
    horizon, `O(16 ticks)`) — a genuine PARITY-at-larger-K gain on a task where gap (b)
    is mild; but
  - it **cannot restore `ρ(K)` past `τ_dist`** (the gap-(b) horizon, ≈3 ticks GSM8K,
    ≈0 Big-Math), because it does not touch `Δ_dist`. On Big-Math, where τ≈0 from gap
    (b) alone, curvature buys essentially nothing.

  So curvature is the only route that is *theoretically* outside σ(M), but its *reach is
  bounded by how much of the staleness error is gap (a) vs gap (b)* — which is task-
  dependent and, on the measured tasks, dominated by gap (b).

### §5.3 The diagonal-trap hand-off to the architect

The σ(M)-escape via curvature is *necessary but not sufficient*; it must also clear the
**diagonal trap**:

> **Diagonal trap (the kill-check for the architect).** The control is dense-**Adam**,
> whose `v_t` is already a *per-coordinate (diagonal) second moment*. If the curvature
> correction `Ĥ·Δθ` collapses to a **per-coordinate rescale** (diagonal `Ĥ`), it is "a
> better Adam diagonal" and stays at **parity** — it injects no information beyond what
> Adam's diagonal preconditioner already captures. **Surpass requires genuinely
> *off-diagonal* curvature** — a true rotation `H·Δθ` with non-trivial cross-coordinate
> terms (`reports/anchor-future-projection/…` §4: "a diagonal `H` can only rescale
> per-coordinate, it cannot rotate"; the measured norm-ratio-≈1 *rotation* is precisely
> the off-diagonal signature).

I hand the architect two theory constraints that bound any curvature route:

1. **(from §3.3) Reach bound.** Off-diagonal curvature can only repair gap (a). Quantify,
   per task, the fraction of `e_K` that is gap (a) vs gap (b) — e.g. via the offline
   kill-test: fit `Ĥ·Δθ` on the EXP-38 `(θ, g)` tensors and measure the cosine *lift*
   `ρ(k)` → `ρ'(k)`. If the lift saturates well below `ρ=1` even with a full (non-
   diagonal) `Ĥ`, that residual **is** gap (b), and it is the structural ceiling on the
   curvature route. The proposed threshold (`00-plan.md` §5; `reports/anchor-future-projection/…`
   §7) — GSM8K `cos@k5: 0.176 → ≥0.40` — is a *gap-(a)-reach* test: passing it means
   curvature recovered enough of gap (a) to matter; failing it means gap (b) already
   dominates at k=5 and no un-rotation helps.
2. **(from §4.0/§5.1) Diagonal-trap probe.** Decompose the fitted `Ĥ` (or the correction's
   per-coordinate gain) into diagonal vs off-diagonal energy. A purely-diagonal lift is
   **PARITY-only** and must be labelled so up front, *regardless of how much cosine it
   recovers* — because a diagonal `Ĥ` is σ(M)-adjacent to an Adam rescale. Only an
   off-diagonal lift is a SURPASS candidate, and even then only up to the reach bound
   (1).

**My parity-vs-surpass call for the theorist's own territory:** every *off-policy
correction* (the §4 toolbox) is **PARITY-at-larger-K at best** and mostly inapplicable;
the *only* lever that could be SURPASS is **off-diagonal curvature/extrapolation**, and
its surpass-reach is bounded by the gap-(a) fraction of `e_K`, which is task-dependent
and small on the measured tasks. The cross-rank-2nd-moment route (disagreement-as-
objective) is the *other* σ(M)-escape but lies outside this note's off-policy scope; I
flag it to the architect as the route that does **not** depend on gap-(a) reach (it
injects variance-as-objective, a different fixed point) and may therefore be the more
robust surpass bet on gap-(b)-dominated tasks like Big-Math.

### §5.4 Async-admissibility (the constraint every lever must also pass)

The async north-star (`.claude/GOAL.md`: single SLOW anchor serving a fast SWARM ⇒
anchor **always lags, never leads**; corrections **cross-rank-identical** from
all-reduced sufficient statistics; **tolerate variable staleness**) interacts with the
above:

- A **scalar staleness dose** keyed to *realized* lag (not a fixed schedule) is the
  cleanest admissible lever: it tolerates variable staleness by construction and is
  cross-rank-identical if the lag and the dose are computed from DP-identical quantities
  (`runs/DELAY_ANALYSIS/staleness_theorist.md` R-S1/R-S2: `μ^age` age-decay and the
  ratio-gated adaptive-λ both already exist in the code and are cross-rank-identical).
  It is PARITY-at-larger-K (§5.1) but it is *robust* and *admissible* — the strongest
  candidate for "raise the budget without surpass."
- A **curvature/extrapolation** correction is admissible **only if** it is
  trajectory-*continuation* (use the anchor's own observed `(θ, g)` history), **not**
  forecasting the swarm's future state — and **only if** `Ĥ` is fit from all-reduced
  sufficient statistics so it is cross-rank-identical, and across a *range* of lags
  (variable staleness), not one fixed τ (`reports/anchor-future-projection/…` §6 raises
  exactly this as open-question #1; GOAL.md line 62 lists "no delay-compensation /
  anchor-lead"). **I flag this as an unresolved tension for the lead to adjudicate**: the
  off-policy math is *agnostic* to whether continuation counts as "lead" — it only says
  curvature repairs gap (a). The admissibility ruling is a project-policy decision, not a
  theorem.

### §6.2 Open questions (for the cross-challenge and the lead)

1. **Async-admissibility of trajectory-continuation extrapolation.** The off-policy math
   is agnostic; GOAL.md line 62 forbids "delay-compensation / anchor-lead"; the 2026-06-22
   discussion argues continuation ≠ lead. **This is a project-policy ruling, not a
   theorem** — it gates whether the only SURPASS-capable off-policy-adjacent lever
   (curvature) is even on the table. (Hand-off to lead + Debate A.)
2. **Gap-(a) vs gap-(b) fraction of `e_K`, per task.** §3.3 argues gap (b) dominates the
   *decorrelation*, but the precise fraction is the ceiling on the curvature route and is
   only inferred (EXP-38 H2), not directly decomposed under a live stale anchor. The
   offline kill-test (fit `Ĥ·Δθ`, measure cosine lift) is the cheap way to bound it.
   **Hand-off to architect.**
3. **Does keeping logits + one extra `π_{θ_t}` forward pass (Guan 2026) ever beat a
   scalar dose?** §4.3 argues it collapses the comm-eff premise into ordinary async
   off-policy GRPO. Worth a kill-test only if a *partial* (sequence-level, sparse) logit-
   keeping variant can stay comm-cheap. **Hand-off to architect.**
4. **Tyurin et al. 2025/2026 (arXiv:2509.24305) mechanism.** Abstract did not disclose
   the staleness-correction mechanism; could be a scalar dose (consistent with §4.0) or
   something new. **Needs full-PDF read — hand-off to async-scholar.**

   note's off-policy scope; flagged to architect/lead.

  canonical results I could not re-verify from a live formula source (arXiv abstract

\subsection{\new{The Stateless Alternative: TAH-Quant}}
\label{app:rlvr-tahquant}

\new{AQ-SGD's premise is that an example recurs so its activation can be
differenced against a stored visit, and Section~\ref{app:rlvr-aqsgd} above
gives the reason that premise sits badly with on-policy RLVR. TAH-Quant
\citep{he2025tahquant} is the natural control for exactly that objection: it is
the successor method in the same setting, pipeline parallelism over a slow
network, and it is \emph{stateless}. Every decision it makes, which coordinate
is the pivot, whether to rotate a tile, how many bits the tile receives, is
recomputed from the tile being sent. It keeps no buffer, no residual and no
error feedback, so the assumption RLVR violates is simply absent. Two
properties follow for free in our harness: the reconstruction is a
deterministic function of the current activation, so the three passes of one
optimizer step agree by construction and the policy ratio still starts at one,
and there is no shared basis, so the anchor has nothing to own.}

\new{The codec cuts each token's width into contiguous tiles. Each tile is
ranked against the other tiles of its sample by the entropy of its normalised
magnitude profile, and the highest-entropy fraction receives INT4 while the
rest receive INT3, on the reasoning that a tile whose energy is spread evenly
has no single coordinate a rotation could isolate. A tile whose largest
magnitude exceeds its second largest by more than a threshold $\tau$ is treated
as holding an outlier: the outlier is permuted to the first coordinate and the
tile is rotated by a scaled Hadamard matrix, which spreads that coordinate's
energy across the tile so the affine quantization range is no longer consumed
by it. Each tile then carries its own scale and zero point.}

\new{Three deviations from the published method are forced here, and we state
them because each one is a place a reader could otherwise assume fidelity.
First, the paper's entropy expression is printed without a leading negative
sign, which makes it the negative of Shannon entropy and inverts it against the
paper's own description of when the quantity is large; implementing it as
printed would award the extra bits to precisely the tiles the rotation already
repairs, so we use Shannon entropy. Second, the paper states no rounding mode,
and its convergence assumption bounds relative bias rather than requiring an
unbiased quantizer, so we run deterministic rounding as the faithful default
and stochastic rounding as a separate arm; the two are otherwise identical, and
the pair asks a question the published analysis leaves open. Third, and most
consequential, the published operating point is far above our payload.}

\new{That third point is worth an explicit number, because it is the reason the
arm is configured the way it is rather than as published. At the paper's
default the codec spends about $4.41$ bits per activation element inclusive of
metadata, which at $H=1536$ is roughly $6773$ bits per token per boundary,
about $5.5\times$ the $1232$ bits the mask sends. The paper itself describes its
INT4/INT3 setting as the lowest-bit configuration that remains consistently
stable. Our operating point is therefore a factor of five below the stability
floor of the method we are comparing against, and we reach it by spending bits
on few coordinates rather than few bits on all of them. To hold the payload we
apply the codec to a keyed subset of coordinates per token, the same device the
other two quantizing arms use, which costs nothing to signal because both ends
derive the subset from the shared key. With $242$ of $1536$ coordinates at tile
size $32$, the cost is $242\times3.8$ bits of payload plus eight tiles of $39$
bits of metadata, or $1231.6$ bits per token per boundary: $0.99968$ of the
mask's $1232$, which is tighter than the memoryless quantization arm's
$1.0004$.}

\new{The tile size deserves a word, since it is not the published one. Metadata
width is what decides it. Reproducing the paper's $4.41$ figure leaves a unique
$39$ bits per tile, of which a half-precision scale and zero point take $16$
each. At the published tile size of $64$ the pivot index needs six bits and the
budget closes exactly, leaving nothing for the one bit a receiver needs in order
to know whether the rotation was applied. The published accounting therefore
cannot pay for the conditional rotation the method itself specifies, and it
enumerates neither the pivot index nor that flag among its metadata. At tile
size $32$ the pivot index needs five bits and the flag fits at the same $39$
bits, so the conditional rotation is preserved and paid for. We measured the
rotation's benefit to be insensitive to tile size, reducing reconstruction
error by $3.60$, $3.72$ and $3.69$ times at two bits for tile sizes $16$, $32$
and $64$ on tiles holding a single dominant coordinate, so nothing is given up;
$32$ is also a tile size the paper ablates.}

\new{Three arms separate three questions at this payload. The primary arm runs
the method as described. A second sets $\tau$ to infinity so no tile is ever
rotated, which isolates outlier suppression from the entropy-guided bit
allocation; without it a difference could be credited to the wrong half of the
method. A third substitutes stochastic rounding, which isolates quantizer bias.
A fourth arm, reported separately because it is deliberately off the payload
budget, runs the published full-width rate and asks whether the payload or the
codec is what binds.}

\new{One diagnostic decides whether these arms measure the method at all, and
it is readable at the first step rather than at the horizon. The rotation only
helps a tile that holds a dominant coordinate, and a keyed subset scatters a
model's few outlier channels rather than concentrating them in fixed contiguous
tiles the way full-width tiling does. We therefore log the fraction of tiles
whose ratio clears $\tau$. Near zero, the arm has degenerated to per-tile
affine quantization and reports a payload-allocation result rather than a test
of TAH-Quant; near one, the threshold is doing no work and the arm is the
published $\tau=0$ endpoint under another name. We report the measured fraction
alongside the accuracy rows so that a null result can be read as the mechanism
not engaging rather than as the mechanism failing.}

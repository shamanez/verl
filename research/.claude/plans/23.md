## Hypothesis

A `delay_K=5` **stale** full-gradient re-anchor — full GRPO forward/backward from
weights theta_{t-5}, EMA'd into `M_anchor`, combined with the live PowerSGD-compressed
gradient via `spectral.correction_mode=inject` (delayed error feedback) or `=blend`
(convex combine toward the stale full gradient), with the fresh clean step OFF
(`clean_cadence=0`) — recovers most of the fresh-clean benefit for PowerSGD r=77.
Falsifiable thresholds (judged on the GSM8K full-test val curve `val-core/openai/gsm8k/acc/mean@1`
at step 50, corroborated by the train-reward trajectory `critic/score/mean`):
**(Success)** `max(val@50(A2), val@50(A3)) >= 0.7315` (within ~1.0 pt of A0's
0.7415) **AND** `>= val@50(A1) + 0.05` (clearly above the no-refresh floor). If the
A1 floor itself lands within 0.01 of A0 (i.e. PowerSGD needs no refresh at all),
that is a stronger PASS of the parent direction and is recorded as such.
**(Falsified)** `max(val@50(A2), val@50(A3)) <= val@50(A1) + 0.02` (stale re-anchor
indistinguishable from no refresh) — staleness breaks the re-anchor; emit STOP with
the next-lever readout (error-feedback codec vs smaller delay_K).

## Correction mechanism — `reweight` (old SVD) vs `inject` vs `blend` (why this experiment uses inject/blend, NOT the old reweight)

The `comm_eff.spectral` circuit exposes THREE `correction_mode`s
(`verl/workers/comm_eff/spectral_filter.py`). This experiment deliberately tests
the two ADDITIVE modes — `inject` (A2) and `blend` (A3) — and NOT the old SVD
`reweight` mode, because project evidence (EXP-21) already proved `reweight`
**inert** on this circuit. All three combine the live compressed gradient `G`
(here `G = P·g`, the PowerSGD-projected boundary grad) with `M`, the EMA of the
**stale full-rank gradient** evaluated at θ_{t−K} (K=5, never compressed). They
differ in HOW they combine them:

| Property | `reweight` (old SVD spectral) | `inject` (A2) | `blend` (A3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formula | `G_proj = α·G + (1−α)·U·diag(d)·(Uᵀ G V)·diag(d)·Vᵀ`, `d=s/(s+τ)` | `G_corr = G + γ·scale·(M − proj_G(M))` | `G_corr = (1−η)·G + η·scale·M` |
| Operation | Tikhonov-reweight of G **projected into M's SVD subspace** | **ADD** the part of M orthogonal to G (the missing direction) | **CONVEX-MIX** G toward the scale-matched M |
| Needs an SVD? | Yes — thin SVD of M per refresh (costly) | No — dot/norm products only (cheap) | No — dot/norm products only (cheap) |
| Can it add a direction ABSENT from G? | **No** — projection is information-non-increasing | **Yes** — injects M's orthogonal complement | **Yes** — mixes M in directly |
| `‖G_corr‖` when G⊥M | ≤‖G‖ (and `G_filt≈0` ⇒ `≈α·G`) | ~√2·‖G‖ at γ=1 (the documented "C1 collapse" cause) | `‖G‖·√((1−η)²+η²) ≤ ‖G‖` (bounded) |
| scale-matched to ‖G‖? | no (operates in G's own scale) | yes (`scale=‖G‖/‖M‖`) | yes (`scale=‖G‖/‖M‖`) |
| Behaviour when `cos(G,M)≈0` (codec rotated G away) | `UᵀGV≈0` ⇒ `G_filt≈0` ⇒ `G_proj≈α·G` — **INERT** (only shrinks) | `complement≈M` ⇒ full scale-matched injection of stale grad | mixes in scale-matched M regardless |
| Knob | `alpha`, `tau` | `inject_gamma` (γ); A2 uses γ=1.0 | `blend_eta` (η): 0=pure G, 1=scale-matched M; A3 uses η=0.5 |
| Verdict on the **mask** codec | **INERT** (EXP-21: cos~0.5, `G_filt≈0`, rel_change~0.5) | live | live (cos 0.81 vs reweight 0.14 on the SAME anchor) |

**Why `reweight` (the old SVD path) is the wrong tool — and what inject/blend fix.**
`reweight` is a *filter*: a two-sided Tikhonov projection that can only keep or
attenuate components of `G` that already lie in `M`'s SVD subspace. A filter is
information-non-increasing — it can NEVER re-introduce a direction the codec
destroyed. The premise "RL updates are sparse + high-rank" needs splitting to see
why this matters: the per-step boundary **gradient** is empirically **LOW-rank**
(issue #21 — its energy concentrates in a few singular directions; that is exactly
why PowerSGD r=77 is near-lossless). What is **high-rank is the codec ERROR**: a
per-element mask scatters the gradient across many coordinates and rotates `G`
nearly orthogonal to the true low-rank subspace (`cos~0.5` ⇒ `UᵀGV≈0` ⇒ reweight
no-ops). So the fix cannot be a better filter of `G`; it must **ADD** the missing
true-gradient component, sourced from the stale full-grad EMA `M`. That is precisely
what `inject` and `blend` do — and the only way to put back a direction projection
cannot. (NB this experiment's codec is PowerSGD, NOT mask: PowerSGD is LOW-bias —
it preserves the dominant subspace — so we expect `cos(G,M)` HIGH and the missing
complement SMALL; the open question is whether a STALE K=5 `M` still aligns enough
to help at all.)

**`inject` vs `blend`, theoretically — which is "best"?**
- `inject` is the more *principled* operator for a low-bias codec: it is a delayed,
  EMA'd **error-feedback** term — it adds back exactly the component of the stale
  full gradient that `G` is missing and leaves the already-aligned part untouched.
  This is the direct attack on issue #21's finding that **"NO error-feedback = the
  top lever"** for PowerSGD. Its one risk is magnitude: at γ=1 with orthogonal
  terms it inflates the step to √2·‖G‖ (the documented C1 collapse) — but for a
  low-bias codec the complement is SMALL, so that inflation is mild here.
- `blend` is the more *robust* operator: a convex combination whose magnitude is
  bounded by ‖G‖, so it composes safely with Adam + grad-clip — but it is a coarser
  "trust the stale full grad more" knob that also down-weights the GOOD aligned part
  of `G`.
- **Net theoretical call:** for the HIGH-bias mask, `blend` was the clear winner
  (bounded; `inject` blew up — the C1 collapse). For the LOW-bias PowerSGD codec
  here the call is genuinely OPEN: `inject` is the closer analogue of true
  error-feedback and its inflation is mild, while `blend` is the safe-but-blunt
  fallback. That undecidedness is exactly why BOTH are run (A2=inject, A3=blend)
  rather than pre-committing to one.

## Measurement gate — measure-then-decide (operator directive 2026-06-04)

Operator decision (2026-06-04): the choice of HOW to combine the two gradient
matrices (`G` = live PowerSGD-compressed grad, `M` = stale full-grad EMA) is
**measurement-driven**, not pre-committed. EXP-23 MEASURES the geometry first and lets
it interpret A2/A3 (and motivate any future combiner) — it does NOT build a new method
blind. GRPO makes this necessary: group-normalized advantages (zero-mean cancellation
⇒ low-rank grad, issue #21) + ratio-clipping (per-token sparsity that varies with
clipfrac) mean the boundary-gradient geometry is **non-stationary**, so a fixed γ/η can
be right early and wrong later.

**Geometry suite to log (most of it is ALREADY printed by inject_matrix/blend_matrix):**
- `cos(G, M)` per-target AND aggregated — alignment of the lossy-current and stale-full
  grads (orthogonality). ALREADY printed by the inject/blend diagnostics.
- complement fraction `‖M − proj_G(M)‖ / ‖M‖` (= sin angle) — how much of the stale full
  grad is MISSING from G = exactly what `inject` adds. Derivable from the printed
  coeff/norms; surface it explicitly.
- norm ratios `‖G‖/‖M‖` and `‖G_corr‖/‖G‖` — scale-match + realized magnitude change.
  ALREADY printed.
- **staleness rotation** `cos(G_t, G_{t−1})` — how fast the true direction rotates per
  step; the proxy for whether a K=5-stale `M` still points the right way. This is the
  ONE genuinely new log (needs a small prev-grad cache). Add it ONLY as an additive,
  OFF-by-default debug diag that NEVER perturbs a gradient (Prime Directive) — do NOT
  gate the smoke on it; if it is not cheap+clean to add, DEFER it to the follow-up.
- (calibration) `cos(G, G_clean_fresh)` and `cos(M, G_clean_fresh)` — the ONLY way to
  ground both estimators against the true current gradient. The analyst may reuse
  EXP-20's clean@K fresh-full-grad traces for this rather than re-running anything.

**Caveat the analyst must respect:** within a normal step there is NO ground-truth
current full gradient (computing it is what we avoid). `cos(G,M)`/complement measure the
RELATIONSHIP between our two available estimators, not their individual error vs truth —
only the (sparse) clean step grounds that. So "measure-then-decide" = per-step G↔M
geometry + clean-step calibration.

**New combiners are ADMISSIBLE (operator: "do not say do not implement"), but
measure-first.** A new rule — e.g. a magnitude-capped *adaptive* error-feedback inject
(`inject`'s "add the missing direction" + `blend`'s bounded magnitude, with γ set from
the measured complement fraction) ≈ the MMSE combiner — is welcome as a NEW flag-gated
`correction_mode` defaulting OFF (so the Prime Directive holds). For THIS run we do NOT
build it: we measure, and IF the geometry warrants it the analyst recommends a
**follow-up issue** with its own plan + review. EXP-23 stays the clean falsification of
the two existing additive modes on PowerSGD.

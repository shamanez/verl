## Hypothesis

This follows up the EXP-37 latency×merger study (`research/runs/EXP-37/verdict.md`; report `research/reports/comm-eff-grpo/why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html`), which established empirically that the stale dense-gradient anchor is STABLE at cadence/delay_K **5/5** but BREAKS at **20/20** for BOTH merger families (signed_ema sign-replace ignites into a length explosion; delayed_ef additive stalls/degrades), while dense GRPO stayed stable+monotonic to 100 steps (EXP-37D val@100 = 0.7832). The leading thesis is that in on-policy GRPO the data distribution moves with the policy, so a delayed dense anchor is the exact gradient of a **defunct policy** — an off-policy/staleness defect, not a codec defect — and there is a policy-drift horizon τ (small K inside it = stable; large K past it = fails).

**This probe measures, on the dense GRPO trajectory itself, how fast that signal drifts in time** so we can quantify the staleness budget. The hypothesis is operationally falsifiable with the following drift thresholds, anchored to the 5/5-stable vs 20/20-broken boundary (at the locked accel surface, `ppo_mini=64`/`train_batch=128` ⇒ **2 optimizer ticks/global-step**, so a `delay_K=20` anchor is **10 global-steps** = **20 optimizer ticks** stale, whereas `delay_K=5` is **2.5 global-steps** = **5 ticks** stale):

> **H1 (gradient-space staleness budget exists and is crossed by 20-tick lag).** For the targeted decoder matrices, the per-matrix-median gradient cosine `cos(g_t, g_{t−k})` is HIGH at the 5/5-equivalent lag (`k ≈ 5` optimizer ticks: median `cos ≥ 0.35`) and has DECAYED MATERIALLY by the 20/20-equivalent lag (`k ≈ 20` ticks: median `cos ≤ 0.15`, i.e. at least a 2× drop relative to `k≈5`), with per-matrix sign-agreement falling from `≥ 0.60` at `k≈5` toward `≤ 0.55` (→ chance) at `k≈20`. **Falsified if** the cosine/sign-agreement is essentially FLAT across `k∈{5,…,20}` (no monotone decay; `cos(k≈20)/cos(k≈5) > 0.7` AND sign-agreement stays `≥ 0.60` at `k≈20`) — that would say gradient-space staleness alone does NOT explain the 5/5-vs-20/20 boundary and the danger lives elsewhere (rollout/logprob or response-behavior space), which is itself a reportable, direction-changing finding.

> **H2 (the drift is GRPO-coupled, not a pure parameter-point gap).** Weight drift `‖θ_t − θ_{t−k}‖` is smooth and roughly monotone in `k` (parameter-point gap, gap 1), but at least one **rollout/logprob-space or response-behavior** signal (rollout-vs-actor logprob gap, response-length mean/slope, advantage dispersion, pg_clipfrac) drifts on a comparable or FASTER timescale than the weights over the same `k` — evidence that the dangerous term is the distribution gap (gap 2), not just curvature×‖Δθ‖. **Falsified if** every GRPO/rollout signal drifts strictly SLOWER than (and is fully explained by) the smooth weight drift, with no distribution-specific acceleration — which would argue the staleness is curvature-bounded (the SFT-like regime) and a stale anchor is safe at larger K than EXP-37 suggested.

Because this is a diagnostic, **either outcome of H1/H2 is a usable result**: the probe's job is to *quantify the curves and locate the unsafe-staleness knee*, and to tell the next-method designer whether the anchor's danger is in gradient space, rollout/logprob space, or response behavior. The PASS/STOP predicate (below) is about whether the probe produced an interpretable dataset + report answering the issue's 6 questions, NOT about which way H1/H2 resolved.

  horizon_global_steps:   25          # the issue's suggested short horizon

Note: `g_dense` here is captured on the **dense (comm-eff OFF)** path — it is simply the actor's own backward gradient at step `t`, read dump-only before `optimizer_step`. No second/uncompressed backward is needed (unlike the comm-eff `capture_g_dense` probe, which exists because the live grad there is compressed). The **optional stale-vs-current-batch gradient comparison** (issue analysis bullet 4 / question would-be "off-policy error") is computed OFFLINE where feasible: with snapshots of `θ` and the per-step batch ids, the analyst can estimate the parameter-point gap directly from `cos(g_t, g_{t−k})`; a true "stale-weights × current-batch" re-gradient is OUT OF SCOPE for this cheap probe (it needs an extra backward) and is left as a recommendation if the offline gap analysis proves insufficient.

## Analyst predicate

Written for a **diagnostic**: PASS = "the probe produced a usable, interpretable drift dataset + report," NOT a performance threshold. Which way H1/H2 resolved does not change PASS — both directions are reportable findings.

- **PASS** iff every box in `## Success criteria` is checked: the off-parity gate passed (diagnostics proven OFF by default), the 25-step dense run completed clean, capture stayed bounded, artifacts are available locally with a complete manifest, the box is torn down, AND the report answers all 6 issue questions with concrete drift tables/plots + a located unsafe-staleness knee + a next-method recommendation. PASS means "we now have the staleness-budget measurement the next method needs," whatever it turned out to be.
- **REVISE** if at most `iterations` boxes are unchecked AND the analyst can name a concrete next-action knob: e.g. capture OOM/disk-overrun → `{knob: snapshot_cadence_N, from: 1, to: 5}` or `{knob: selected_matrices, from: 14, to: 7}` and re-run step 2 once; or a missing role (e.g. `update_vector` not dumped) that blocks one of the 6 questions → add the role and re-run. Output `next_actions:` as a yaml list of `{knob, from, to, rationale}`.
- **STOP** if: the off-parity gate FAILS and cannot be fixed within `iterations` (the instrumentation is not a no-op when disabled → the patch is unsafe to merge; fix in a fresh `code_change` cycle); OR the dense run NaNs (it should not); OR budget exhausted; OR `iterations` REVISE cycles consumed.

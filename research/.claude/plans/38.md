## Hypothesis

This follows up the EXP-37 latency×merger study (`research/runs/EXP-37/verdict.md`; report `research/reports/comm-eff-grpo/why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html`), which established empirically that the stale dense-gradient anchor is STABLE at cadence/delay_K **5/5** but BREAKS at **20/20** for BOTH merger families (signed_ema sign-replace ignites into a length explosion; delayed_ef additive stalls/degrades), while dense GRPO stayed stable+monotonic to 100 steps (EXP-37D val@100 = 0.7832). The leading thesis is that in on-policy GRPO the data distribution moves with the policy, so a delayed dense anchor is the exact gradient of a **defunct policy** — an off-policy/staleness defect, not a codec defect — and there is a policy-drift horizon τ (small K inside it = stable; large K past it = fails).

**This probe measures, on the dense GRPO trajectory itself, how fast that signal drifts in time** — across THREE spaces: (i) parameter / dense-gradient space, (ii) the **boundary-activation subspace** (the tensor the comm-eff PP codec actually compresses), and (iii) GRPO rollout/logprob/response-behavior space — so we can quantify both the **gradient-anchor staleness budget** and the **activation-codec (Q) staleness budget** before designing the next method. The hypothesis is operationally falsifiable with the following drift thresholds, anchored to the 5/5-stable vs 20/20-broken boundary (at the locked accel surface, `ppo_mini=64`/`train_batch=128` ⇒ **2 optimizer ticks/global-step**, so a `delay_K=20` anchor is **10 global-steps** = **20 optimizer ticks** stale, whereas `delay_K=5` is **2.5 global-steps** = **5 ticks** stale). **75 global steps = 150 optimizer ticks**, so lags up to k≈20 ticks are densely sampled (≈130 step-pairs contribute to the k=20 bin) and k can be pushed to ~40 ticks (20 global steps) to probe *beyond* the failure boundary; the run also crosses the **GSM8K epoch-2 boundary at ≈ global step 58** (7473 train prompts / 128 per batch ≈ 58.4 steps/epoch), the regime where EXP-37 instability emerged, so the probe captures whether activation/gradient rank & principal directions shift at the epoch boundary.

> **H1 (gradient-space staleness budget exists and is crossed by 20-tick lag).** For the targeted decoder matrices, the per-matrix-median gradient cosine `cos(g_t, g_{t−k})` is HIGH at the 5/5-equivalent lag (`k ≈ 5` optimizer ticks: median `cos ≥ 0.35`) and has DECAYED MATERIALLY by the 20/20-equivalent lag (`k ≈ 20` ticks: median `cos ≤ 0.15`, i.e. at least a 2× drop relative to `k≈5`), with per-matrix sign-agreement falling from `≥ 0.60` at `k≈5` toward `≤ 0.55` (→ chance) at `k≈20`. **Falsified if** the cosine/sign-agreement is essentially FLAT across `k∈{5,…,20}` (no monotone decay; `cos(k≈20)/cos(k≈5) > 0.7` AND sign-agreement stays `≥ 0.60` at `k≈20`) — that would say gradient-space staleness alone does NOT explain the 5/5-vs-20/20 boundary and the danger lives elsewhere (activation-subspace, rollout/logprob, or response-behavior space), which is itself a reportable, direction-changing finding.

> **H2 (the drift is GRPO-coupled, not a pure parameter-point gap).** Weight drift `‖θ_t − θ_{t−k}‖` is smooth and roughly monotone in `k` (parameter-point gap, gap 1), but at least one **rollout/logprob-space or response-behavior** signal (rollout-vs-actor logprob gap, response-length mean/slope, advantage dispersion, pg_clipfrac) drifts on a comparable or FASTER timescale than the weights over the same `k` — evidence that the dangerous term is the distribution gap (gap 2), not just curvature×‖Δθ‖. **Falsified if** every GRPO/rollout signal drifts strictly SLOWER than (and is fully explained by) the smooth weight drift, with no distribution-specific acceleration — which would argue the staleness is curvature-bounded (the SFT-like regime) and a stale anchor is safe at larger K than EXP-37 suggested.

> **H3 (activation-codec staleness budget — boundary subspace drift; NEW in v2).** At the candidate pipeline boundaries, the boundary hidden-state `h` is genuinely **low-rank** (stable rank `‖h‖_F²/‖h‖_2² ≪ H=1536`, and rank-for-90%-energy `≤ ~r=77`, the locked PowerSGD rank), and its top-`r` principal **subspace** drifts with lag `k` on a measurable timescale: top-`r` subspace overlap `o(t,t−k)` decays from near-1 at small `k` toward a smaller value at `k≈20`. The decay shape tells the codec-refresh budget: **smooth-monotone** drift ⇒ a periodic `Q`-refresh-at-cadence can track it; **oscillatory/periodic or jumpy** direction change ⇒ periodic refresh lags badly. **Falsified (and reported as such) if** either (a) the boundary activation is NOT low-rank (rank-for-90%-energy `≫ r`), which says rank-`r` activation compression is the wrong primitive regardless of staleness; or (b) the subspace is essentially STATIC over 75 steps (`o(t,t−20) ≥ 0.95`), which says `Q` can be frozen far longer than the current cadence and codec-staleness is NOT the limiter. Either resolution is a usable, direction-changing finding.

Because this is a diagnostic, **every outcome of H1/H2/H3 is a usable result**: the probe's job is to *quantify the curves and locate the unsafe-staleness knees* (gradient-anchor AND activation-codec), and to tell the next-method designer whether the danger is in gradient space, activation-subspace, rollout/logprob space, or response behavior, and whether compression should live in activation space, gradient space, or both. The PASS/STOP predicate (below) is about whether the probe produced an interpretable dataset + a strong HTML report answering the issue's questions, NOT about which way H1/H2/H3 resolved.

The **optional stale-vs-current-batch gradient comparison** (issue analysis bullet 4 / would-be "off-policy error") is computed OFFLINE where feasible: with snapshots of `θ` and the per-step batch ids, the analyst can estimate the parameter-point gap directly from `cos(g_t, g_{t−k})`; a true "stale-weights × current-batch" re-gradient is OUT OF SCOPE for this cheap probe (it needs an extra backward) and is left as a recommendation if the offline gap analysis proves insufficient.

The boundary `h`/`grad_h` dumps are the forward+backward tensors a real PP link would carry (per `powersgd_activation.py`, the codec compresses exactly `M` of shape `(N, H)` = packed tokens × hidden); the offline analysis (ADDITION 1 + 2) reads them to answer the activation-codec-staleness and gradient-rank questions without any second run.

### WHAT WE CAPTURED — strict layout + naming conventions

**Path:** `runs/EXP-38/big-math/captures/rank0/tick_<G>_<T>/<role>/<target>.pt` (all fp32, rank0-only).

- **`tick_<G>_<T>`** — `G` = GRPO **global step** (1–75); `T` = cumulative **optimizer tick**. The accel surface runs **2 optimizer ticks / global step** (`ppo_mini=64` / `train_batch=128`), so the first tick of step `G` is `T = 2(G−1)`. **21 ticks captured**, deliberately shaped for the lag-`k` axis:

### 0. Orientation — what this measures and why (the scientific frame)

EXP-38 is a **diagnostic**, not a method that beats dense. It runs ONE **dense GRPO** trajectory (comm-eff OFF; the run is its OWN reference, n=1) and captures, per snapshot, the weights `θ`, the dense gradient `g`, the applied update `Δθ`, and the pipeline-**boundary** activation `h` + its gradient `grad_h`. The goal: **measure how fast the dense GRPO signal drifts in time**, to set the **staleness budgets** for the next communication-efficient pipeline-parallel (PP) GRPO method. Anchor everything to the **EXP-37 empirical boundary**: a stale dense-gradient anchor was **STABLE at delay/cadence 5/5** but **BROKE at 20/20** (length-explosion / collapse). On this accel surface **1 global step = 2 optimizer ticks** (ppo_mini 64 / train_batch 128), so the lag axis `k` is in **optimizer ticks**: **k≈5 = the stable 5/5 anchor, k≈20 = the broken 20/20 anchor, k=40 = beyond the failure boundary**. Three falsifiable hypotheses (resolve each as NUMBERS, either way — every outcome is a usable finding):
- **H1 (gradient staleness):** `cos(g_t, g_{t−k})` HIGH at k≈5 (≥0.35) and materially DECAYED by k≈20 (≤0.15, ≥2× drop), sign-agreement 0.60→~0.55. Falsified if flat (cos(k20)/cos(k5) > 0.7 AND sign-agree ≥0.60 at k20).
- **H2 (GRPO-coupled, not pure parameter gap):** at least one rollout/logprob/response signal drifts as fast as or faster than the weights. Falsified if every GRPO signal is strictly slower than smooth weight drift.
- **H3 (activation-codec budget):** boundary `h` is low-rank (rank-for-90%-energy ≤ ~r=77 ≪ H=1536) and its top-`r` subspace overlap `o(t,t−k)` decays smoothly (periodic-refresh-able) vs jumpy. Falsified if `h` is NOT low-rank (rank ≫ r → rank-r activation compression is wrong primitive) OR the subspace is static (`o(t,t−20) ≥ 0.95` → Q can be frozen far longer).
- **Two-gap framing** (bridge to the recommendation): cos from THIS dense run = the *parameter-point gap* (gap 1, curvature-bounded, SFT also has it). If gap-1 already decays sharply by k≈20, the budget is tight even before the distribution gap (gap 2). The **σ(M)-ceiling** idea (a deterministic route beats dense only by injecting info OUTSIDE the stale+current dense-grad means) is summarized here inline — no external doc needed. **HTML style reference (exists, confirmed):** `reports/comm-eff-grpo/why-grpo-fails-sft-works.html` — model the new reports on it (self-contained, base64 plots). *(All paths in this runbook are relative to the working dir `/Users/shamane/Documents/verl/research`.

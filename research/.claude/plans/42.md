## Why this experiment (motivation + prior evidence)
The look-ahead anchor extrapolates the K-stale weights forward:
`θ̂ = (1+α)·θ[t−K] − α·θ[t−2K]` (α = fraction of the staleness K extrapolated forward;
α=1 ⇒ predict all the way to the current step). Two modes: `fixed_linear` (frozen coeffs)
and `learned_linear` (per-matrix scalar residual trained from retrospective error).

Every prior M4 run jumped to a *training/val* or *gradient* verdict and never measured the
**primitive** the whole method rests on: **does the projected weight `θ̂` actually approximate
`θ_now` better than doing nothing?** What we DO know from prior runs makes this urgent:
- **EXP-41** (fixed_linear, K=20): cell B collapsed; `anchor_align_cos` lifted (+0.027) but
  the merger over-amplified and length-exploded. (See [LOG.md] EXP-41 STOP.)
- **Prior EXP-42 α-sweep** (A25/A50/A75/L, K=20): **ALL cells collapsed via an
  *oscillating extrapolated-anchor cosine* whose amplitude scaled with α** — the extrapolated
  anchor's gradient alternated +/− alignment and a single sign-flip at the first true
  extrapolation ignited length-explosion (non-monotonic: α0.25@38 < α1.0@55 < α0.50@83).
  The learned cell dampened the *raw-stale* oscillation but NOT the *extrapolated* overshoot
  (PROGRESS.md lines 71–82).

That sign-flipping is the fingerprint of an **extrapolation that overshoots in weight space**
(predicts past the true `θ_now`, landing on the far side → the implied update direction
reverses). The ONLY way to confirm/refute that mechanism is to measure the weight prediction
directly. This experiment does exactly that, cheaply, and gates whether a gradient-accuracy
study is even worth running later.

## What is NEW in this plan (THE deliverable — most important)
### 1. A dump-only **weight-trajectory sketch** instrument (`comm_eff.probe.weight_traj`)
At **every optimizer tick** (end of step, detached, no optimizer/loss side effect), for the
**196 decoder matrices** (q/k/v/o/gate/up/down × 28 layers; LayerNorm/embed/lm_head/bias
excluded exactly as the projector excludes them):
- summon the matrix to CPU (reuse `_summon_current_full`), then write a **compact sketch**, NOT
  the full ~2.6 GB snapshot:
  - **count-sketch / feature-hash** `s_m = Σ_i σ(i)·θ_m[i] → bucket b(i)∈[k]`, `k=4096`,
    seeded per matrix-shape (deterministic, cross-rank-identical), stored **fp16**. JL/AMS
    guarantees preserve `‖Δ‖` and `⟨Δ₁,Δ₂⟩` of weight-DIFFERENCE vectors ⇒ all downstream
    norms / cosines / ratios are reconstructable from sketches (rel. std ≈ 1/√k ≈ 1.6%).
  - the matrix **mean** `μ_m` (fp32 scalar) — needed to replay the learned-residual rule
    offline (its update uses `mean(θ_now−θ̂_prev)`).
  - The count-sketch is an **implicit** projection (hash + sign), so no `k×d` matrix is
    materialized; the per-shape hash/sign index tensors live on GPU and the scatter-add is
    O(d)/tick. Per-tick I/O ≈ 196·4096·2 B ≈ **1.6 MB**; ~200 ticks ⇒ **~320 MB/regime**.
- compute the **EXACT headline metrics on-box** (full fp32, from a small CPU ring of recent
  snapshots) at the operating point Δ=10: h=10 (α=1, full catch-up) — the headline — plus h∈{5,20}
  (under/over-shoot) if the CPU ring fits: `weight_proj_ratio` + `dir_cos` aggregated over the 196
  matrices. We are happy to spend box-compute on the result we care most about — these are the
  GROUND-TRUTH headline numbers AND the calibration the MacBook validates its sketch-based sweep against.
- Output: `weights/sketch_tick_<gs>_<tick>.npz` + `weights/manifest.jsonl` + `weights/calib.jsonl`.
- **Disabled path byte-identical** (master guard `weight_traj.enabled=false` ⇒ no hook, no
  summon, no write). Independent of the prior `grad_proj` probe and of the anchor/merger.

Regime B runs **codec-only** (no anchor circuit, no spectral merger, no look-ahead) but with an
**adaptive, fast-owned basis Q** (`owns_q=false`), so the measured trajectory is the realistic
compressed-but-learning system trajectory without circular dependence on the very projector we are
evaluating. **A frozen basis (`owns_q=true` + anchor off) is INVALID** — it makes the codec a fixed
random projection and the run collapses; Q must update.

### Metrics (per (method, regime, Δ, h), averaged over all valid anchor ticks s)
Let `θ_stale=θ[s]`, `θ_old=θ[s−Δ]`, `target=θ[s+h]`,
`θ̂_fix = θ_stale + (h/Δ)(θ_stale − θ_old)` (α=h/Δ), `θ̂_learn = θ̂_fix + r_m`.
- **W1 `weight_proj_ratio = ‖θ̂−target‖ / ‖θ_stale−target‖`  ← HEADLINE.** `<1` ⇒ projection
  lands CLOSER than the raw-stale weights; `=1` ⇒ no help; `>1` ⇒ overshoot (HURTS). The
  **crossover horizon h\*** (largest h with ratio `<1`) is the key number per regime/method.
- **W2 `dir_cos = cos(θ_stale−θ_old, target−θ_stale)`** — pure trajectory straightness
  (α-independent); `<0` ⇒ the past update points *away* from the future update ⇒ the sign-flip
  that ignited the prior collapses. Directly tests the extrapolation-overshoot hypothesis.
- **W3 `weight_relerr = ‖θ̂−target‖/‖target‖`** — absolute prediction error.
- **per-matrix p10/p50/p90 of W1** across the 196 matrices — WHERE projection helps vs hurts.
- **W4 per-matrix linearity R²** (regress `target−θ_stale` on `θ_stale−θ_old`) — tests the
  code-comment claim "RLVR weights move ~linearly (R²~0.9)" on OUR GRPO trajectory.
- **fixed vs learned** at each (Δ,h): does the learned scalar-mean residual improve W1/W3 over
  fixed (it cannot help W2 by construction — a uniform shift barely moves direction).

## Hypothesis
**H1 (premise test):** at the anchor's operating horizon (h=Δ=K=10 ticks, i.e. α=1 full
catch-up), the projected weights are closer to `θ_now` than raw-stale — **median
`weight_proj_ratio < 1`** — in BOTH regimes, for at least `fixed_linear`.
**H2 (overshoot test):** the prior collapses are explained by extrapolation overshoot —
`weight_proj_ratio` rises past 1 and/or `dir_cos` goes `<0` as h grows toward/over K, with the
crossover earlier at larger α (=h/Δ).
**H3 (regime effect):** activation compression changes predictability — Regime B's crossover
horizon and `dir_cos` differ measurably from Regime A's (we quantify, no sign assumed).
**Falsification:** if median `weight_proj_ratio ≥ 1` at every horizon `h ≥ 1` in BOTH regimes,
linear weight extrapolation never beats doing nothing *in weight space* on this landscape — the
whole look-ahead premise is falsified at the weight level, and **any gradient-accuracy follow-up
is mooted** (a gradient cannot be more accurate than the weight it is computed at).

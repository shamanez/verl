EXP-42 / dense run / weight behavior v2

The dense GRPO trajectory is globally near-linear and low-rank, but the look-ahead's two-point slope overshoots

A deeper GPU-free look at the plain GRPO run (codec off), all computed from the per-tick weight-trajectory count-sketch. Five studies: the low-rank structure of the weight-displacement subspace, the per-matrix projectability and crossover horizon, an optimal-coefficient sweep against the naive rule, the link between local linearity and projectability, and the learned-vs-fixed residual effect.

Headline

The per-tick weight updates of the 196 decoder matrices live in a low-dimensional subspace: the median participation ratio is 7.6 out of a possible 159, and a median of 26 components capture 90 percent of the per-tick displacement energy. By a global straight-line fit the trajectory is substantially linear (through-origin extrapolation R squared 0.85; one dominant direction holds 69 percent of the centered energy), which is the RLVR-linear claim, just measured globally rather than step to step.

Yet the look-ahead anchor still helps only out to about 11 ticks (median crossover) because it estimates the trajectory direction from a noisy two-point slope. The naive coefficient alpha = h/Delta overshoots: at h=10 a damped alpha = 0.53 cuts the median ratio from 0.972 to 0.836, and at h=20 the optimal alpha keeps the ratio below 1 (0.897) where the naive rule fails (1.173). A simple damped coefficient is the actionable lever.

(a) Low-rank structure of the weight-displacement subspace

For each matrix we stack the per-tick displacement vectors theta[t] - theta[t-1] (there are 159 of them) and take the eigenvalues of their Gram matrix, which the linear count-sketch preserves up to the ~1.6 percent noise floor. The participation ratio (median 7.6) and the 90-percent component count (median 26) both say the updates explore only a handful of directions out of the 159 available. Sketch noise inflates the small eigenvalues, so it biases the effective rank upward: the true subspace is at least this low-rank, probably lower.

Reconciling the RLVR-linear claim: the prior report measured local consecutive-step linearity (R squared 0.80 at one tick, decaying to 0.32 at the K=10 scale) and read this as the trajectory curving. The global line fit here shows the opposite face of the same data: one direction holds 69 percent of the energy and a single straight line explains R squared 0.85 of the displacement (through-origin). Both are true. The trajectory is one dominant slow drift plus per-step noise; the local metric sees mostly the noise at fine scale, the global metric sees mostly the drift. The cited RLVR result reports a global line at R squared about 0.9 over hundreds of steps; our analogous global figure (0.85) is in that ballpark, and since sketch noise inflates the residual it is a lower bound on the true linearity. The gap to 600 steps is then about horizon and motion size (80 steps, 0.057 percent total drift from an already-tuned model), not a qualitative difference in shape.

What this can and cannot test. The count-sketch flattens each weight matrix to a vector before sketching, so it preserves displacement norms, cosines and the temporal Gram matrix, but it does not preserve the singular spectrum of a single weight matrix in its native rows-by-cols shape. The LoRA-style reading of 'RLVR updates are low-rank' (the update matrix Delta W has few large singular values) is therefore not computable from this data and is not claimed here. What we measure is the temporal low-rank structure: the trajectory of flattened updates lives in a low-dimensional subspace. The embeddings, RMSNorm gains and biases were not collected in this run, so all of (a) is the decoder set only.

(b) Per-matrix projectability and crossover horizon

For each matrix we take the median weight_proj_ratio over all valid anchors at the operating spacing Delta=10 and the naive coefficient, and read off the largest horizon with median ratio below 1 (its crossover h*). The distribution is tight: crossover h* ranges 9 to 14 ticks with median 11, and the ratio at h=10 spans only 0.956 to 0.985 (median 0.972). Projectability is a property of the whole decoder, not a few outliers. Per-matrix differences of about one tick in h* are within the ~1.6 percent sketch noise, but the ranking below is supported by ratio gaps larger than the floor.

(c) Optimal coefficient: is there a better-than-naive linear extrapolation

At each horizon we sweep a single global coefficient alpha and find the value that minimizes the median weight_proj_ratio over all matrices and anchors, then compare it to the naive alpha = h/Delta that the look-ahead uses. A better-than-naive coefficient clearly exists, and the gain grows with horizon.

The optimal coefficient is well below the naive one at every horizon (roughly 0.53 at h=10 versus the naive 1.0, and 0.74 at h=20 versus the naive 2.0). Interpretation: the two-point slope theta_stale - theta_old over-states the persistent drift because it also captures per-step noise, so the naive rule extrapolates too far. Damping alpha corrects the over-step. The gain is far above the ~1.6 percent sketch floor, so it is real. Caveat: this alpha is fit on the same trajectory it is scored on, so the table is an oracle upper bound on the achievable gain. But the optimal alpha is stable across horizons (about 0.5 to 0.75), so a single fixed damped coefficient near 0.5 would capture most of it without any online estimation, which is a concrete, deployable change to the look-ahead rule.

(d) Does more-linear mean more-projectable

Across the 196 matrices, the fine-scale linearity R squared (one tick) correlates with projectability in the expected direction: more-linear matrices project further (Spearman 0.45 versus crossover h*) and land closer (Spearman -0.51 versus the ratio at h=10, negative because lower ratio is better). With n=196 a correlation above about 0.14 is significant at the 5 percent level, so these moderate correlations are well clear of noise. The relationship is real but loose: local linearity explains part of projectability, not all of it, because projectability also depends on how the per-step noise inflates the two-point slope (study c).

(e) Learned-linear vs fixed-linear residual effect

The learned variant adds a per-matrix scalar mean-shift residual, updated retrospectively (r left-arrow clip(r + 0.1 mean(theta_true - theta_hat), +/- 1e-3)) and replayed through the all-ones sketch. Quantified on the dense run: the residual grows to at most 2.7e-09 in magnitude (far from its 1e-3 clip), its contribution to the projection has relative norm 1.0e-04 (median) against the target displacement, and the resulting change in weight_proj_ratio is 0.0e+00 (median), at most 6.5e-05. Every one of these is below the ~1.6 percent sketch noise floor, so the learned residual is statistically indistinguishable from doing nothing here. The mechanism is clear: a single scalar added uniformly to every element of a high-dimensional matrix barely moves the norm or direction of a displacement vector, and the dense run's per-matrix mean drifts so smoothly that the fixed extrapolation of that mean already has almost no retrospective error to correct. The learned residual is inert on the dense run.

Scope, provenance, and caveats

  • Source: regimeA per-tick weight sketch, runs/EXP-42/regimeA/weights (196 decoder matrices q/k/v/o/gate/up/down, 160 ticks, k=4096). Ticks are contiguous (True). Two ticks per global step.
  • All metrics computed on the MacBook from the sketch, GPU-free. No GPU, no training, no provisioning. The count-sketch is linear and norm-preserving, so all displacement norms, cosines and Gram matrices reconstruct from it with relative std about 1/sqrt(k) = 1.6 percent; this floor is stated at each study where it matters.
  • Not computable from this data, so not claimed: the matrix-native singular spectrum (LoRA-style rank) of an individual weight matrix, which the flatten-then-sketch step destroys; and anything about the embeddings, RMSNorm gains or biases, which were not collected for this run (decoder matrices only).
  • Performance context: this is a healthy plain-GRPO control, final validation accuracy 0.7695 on GSM8K (near-converged, flat over the run).

Why the stale-anchor comm-eff method fails on on-policy RL (GRPO) but works for SFT

A scientific verdict synthesized from this project's own runs and the implemented mechanism (verl/workers/comm_eff/spectral_filter.py, state.py). Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, GSM8K, vanilla GRPO, accel surface, 100 steps.

Established has run evidence in this program Hypothesized mechanism-consistent, not directly isolated here

Headline verdict. The stale anchor exports one assumption from SFT: a K-step-old full gradient is a good proxy for the current full gradient. That assumption is true under stationarity and false under on-policy non-stationarity. In SFT the loss is a fixed expectation over a frozen dataset, so g(θt−K) and g(θt) are two lagged estimates of descent on the same objective — bounded-lag, error-feedback-recoverable, telescoping. In GRPO the gradient is an expectation over samples drawn from the current policy πθt; the anchor's gradient is the exact, self-consistent on-policy gradient of πθt−K — a valid policy-gradient estimate for a policy that no longer exists, transplanted into θt's update with no importance-sampling correction.

The merger then injects that wrong-distribution signal as a persistent, low-variance, cross-rank-identical, biased force. GRPO — uniquely — exposes a reward-hackable degree of freedom (autoregressive response length under a normalized, verifier-only advantage with no teacher forcing) along which that persistent force compounds. The instability is not a property of compression, of the codec, or of data-revisiting; it is the interaction of (a) a persistent off-policy carrier with (b) GRPO's free length variable. Staleness K is the control knob: small K keeps the anchor inside the policy-drift horizon (near-dense, stable); large K pushes it past that horizon and the run fails. This is why "it works for SFT" does not transfer.

0Decoding the staleness: this is not textbook off-policy RL

It is tempting to file this under "off-policy RL is hard." It is worse and more specific than that. In textbook off-policy RL you optimize the current policy θt using trajectories from an old behavior policy μ, but with an importance-sampling correction πθt/μ that keeps the estimator unbiased for ∇J(θt). You trade bias for variance, but you are still estimating the current gradient.

Our anchor gradient does the opposite trade. Decode what it actually is:

  • ganchor = ∇J evaluated at the stale weights θt−K on the stale trajectories those weights generated (paired replay, EXP-29). Paired replay makes it the exact, self-consistent on-policy gradient of the old policy πθt−K — a perfectly valid PG estimate.
  • It is transplanted into θt's update with no IS reweighting to the current policy. So it is not an unbiased-but-noisy estimate of ∇J(θt). It is a clean, low-variance, high-bias gradient pointing toward the old policy's ascent direction.

0.1 — Bias, not variance (and why that is worse here)

IS-corrected off-policy converts staleness into variance (a noisy but correct-in-expectation estimate of the current gradient). Our method converts staleness into bias (a precise estimate of the wrong gradient). Low variance plus persistence is more dangerous than noise: noise averages out across steps; a consistent, low-variance pull toward where the policy was does not — it integrates. This is exactly the carrier condition (§2): a fixed-direction force summed over T ticks. Established that the cross-run collapse discriminator is merger-carrier presence, not grad noise — plain PowerSGD (same noisy grad class, no merger) is clean on the same substrate while the merger spikes (canonical-anchor-comm-eff-base §post-mortem).

0.2 — Two distinct gaps, not one

The transplant carries two independent mismatches, where SFT has only one:

  1. Parameter-point gap θt−K → θt — the gradient was taken at old weights. This is the only gap SFT has, and it is curvature-bounded: ∥g(θt−K)−g(θt)∥ = O(K·∥Δθ∥·L).
  2. Distribution gap πθt−K → πθt — the trajectories, and the group-relative advantage A(τ) computed on them, are from a policy the current policy may never generate. The old trajectories can sit off the current policy's support (a different mode), so the anchor rewards/penalizes sequences πθt would not even produce. Established by construction (paired replay pairs stale θ with the stale batch it generated, EXP-29; the async target is a single slow anchor lagging a fast swarm, async-anchor-single-node-fast-swarm).

SFT has gap 1 only, and it is bounded by curvature; GRPO has gap 1 and gap 2 stacked, and gap 2 is unbounded by smoothness because the data distribution itself moved.

0.3 — delayed_ef is one step further removed

The two mergers transplant different stale quantities:

  • signed_ema transplants sign(M) — the direction of the old-policy gradient. Its assumption: "stale gradient sign ≈ current gradient sign."
  • delayed_ef (B2) transplants δ = Mrep − Gcomp_ring(t−K) — the codec error (full minus compressed gradient), both measured at θt−K, added to the current compressed gradient. Its assumption is not "stale gradient ≈ current gradient" but "stale codec error ≈ current codec error."

Is that weaker or stronger under non-stationarity? Hypothesized. The codec-error assumption is structurally weaker to violate: the off-principal residual the rank-77 projector drops is a property of the gradient's subspace geometry, which is slower-varying than the gradient's policy-dependent magnitude/sign, and δ is a difference of two quantities at the same old policy so the leading policy-dependent term partly cancels. That is why delayed_ef is near-dense at low latency (EXP-30: 0.0008 under dense) and why it is direction-preserving rather than direction-replacing. But the cancellation is only partial: at large K, δ is the residual between a stale-policy anchor and an even-staler ring entry, so it becomes a persistent additive pull toward a two-lags-old policy — a carrier of the same off-policy type, applied additively every tick (see the two-symptoms subsection §2.1).

0.4 — SFT immunity, restated cleanly

With a fixed exogenous dataset there is no policy. The "wrong-policy" bias term (gap 2) is identically zero: every gradient is taken over the same frozen distribution. So g(θt−K) and g(θt) are gradients of the same objective at two nearby points, bias bounded by curvature·∥Δθ∥. That single deleted term — gap 2 → 0 — is the whole difference between "works in SFT" and "ignites/drags in GRPO."

0.5 — Paired replay: a double-edged sword

EXP-29 paired replay was introduced to make M a valid estimate — it removed the stale-weights×current-batch confound by pairing the stale weights with the batch those weights generated. This decode reframes what that achieved: it makes M a valid estimate of the wrong thing. A self-consistent old-policy gradient is lower variance than the confounded stale-weights×fresh-batch signal it replaced — but it is still biased toward the old policy. Lowering the variance of a biased signal makes the carrier cleaner and more persistent, i.e. more ignition-prone / more reliably wrong-directioned, not less. This is why EXP-29's "valid M" did not rescue the mergers (signed_ema still dominated/unstable; B2 reaches only parity): validity was never the missing ingredient — on-policy-ness was, and paired replay deliberately fixes the policy to the stale one. Established tension: valid-M circuit ran in EXP-30/32/33 and merger instability remained structural (50.4% sign-disagreement at the first warm step; β/α/delay sweeps all null for surpass).

1Stationary vs non-stationary target — the load-bearing distinction

The merger forms a correction from the anchor's stale gradient. The two production mergers (spectral_filter.py):

  • delayed_ef (B2): δ(t) = Mrep(t) − Gcomp_ring(t−K), Gcorr = Gcomp + λ·δ (additive, direction-preserving).
  • signed_ema: Gcorr = α·Gnoisy + (1−α)·|Gnoisy|·sign(M) (sign from the stale anchor EMA).

Write the descent gradient as g(θ; D) where D is the data distribution the gradient is taken over.

  • SFT. D is a frozen dataset D₀. Every token's target is fixed. So g(θt−K; D₀) and g(θt; D₀) are gradients of the same function at two nearby points; their difference is the bounded smoothness lag, which error-feedback telescopes (Σt Gcorr ≈ Σt gfull(t−K) + drift). The stale gradient is a valid lagged estimate of the same descent direction.
  • GRPO (on-policy). D = D(πθt) — rollouts are sampled from the current policy and the advantage is group-relative on those samples. The anchor replays πθt−K's batch, so M = g(θt−K; D(πθt−K)): a gradient on a different distribution from a defunct policy (see §0). It is not a lagged estimate of the current gradient; it is off-policy by construction.

The σ(M) ceiling sharpens this: any deterministic Φ(Gcomp, M) is σ(M)-measurable ⇒ capped at dense — the merger can at best reconstruct the dense gradient on stale data, never inject information dense lacks (theory.md, surpass-dense-sigma-m-ceiling-and-routes). Under SFT, "the dense gradient on stale data" is a good target; under on-policy GRPO, faithfully reconstructing the stale-policy gradient faithfully reconstructs an off-policy step.

2The RL-specific ignition channel — why a persistent carrier explodes in GRPO and not SFT

A stale, low-variance correction is dangerous only if there is a direction along which it can accumulate unchecked by the reward. GRPO supplies one; SFT does not.

The carrier (persistence condition). The carrier law (issue #30, canonical-anchor-comm-eff-base): ignition needs the residual carrier's autocorrelation time >> cadence — heuristically ∥Σt et∥ ~ λ·T·∥G∥, a fixed-direction force summed over T ticks. Two implemented sources of persistence: (1) EMA memory βanc — long-memory β=0.95 was the ignition carrier; β=0 removes it. (2) Staleness K itself — even at β=0 the held-δ / the sign track a gradient K ticks behind the policy; at large K the staleness is the carrier.

The reward-hackable attractor (the RL-only part). GRPO with verifier-only reward, group-normalized advantage, no KL, no entropy bonus, no teacher forcing leaves response length a nearly free variable: longer responses are reward-flat over a wide band, and token-mean loss aggregation gives a tail-amplification ratchet (~86×, exp25-collapse-gradient-flow). A persistent tangential force rectifies along this "correct-but-longer" direction until the ratchet locks in.

  • Established the killer is length-hacking, not low entropy: dense is the lowest-entropy run (≈0.12–0.16) and the most stable; entropy is a follower (×3 confirmations).
  • Established the discriminator is merger-carrier presence, not grad noise or codec (plain vs ef_r2 controlled contrast).

Why SFT lacks this channel. SFT has fixed targets and teacher forcing: sequence length is not a free variable to inflate for reward (there is no reward, only fixed cross-entropy to a fixed continuation). The same persistent carrier in SFT merely biases the step slightly off the fresh direction (bounded, telescoping); there is no reward-flat direction to rectify into, so it never ignites. The carrier is necessary but not sufficient — GRPO supplies the sufficient second ingredient (the free length variable); SFT does not. Hypothesized (mechanism-grounded; this program has no in-house SFT run, so the SFT side rests on the method's prior SL-validation plus the established absence of the length channel).

2.1 — Two symptoms, one cause: how the two merger families fail differently at 20/20

Same root cause (off-policy staleness at K > τ), opposite symptom — set by the merger's geometry:

sign-replacing

signed_ema → ignition

Replacing the sign with the stale anchor's destroys per-coordinate sign cancellation (50.4% sign-disagreement at the first warm step). The result is sign-SGD-with-a-stale-sign: full-magnitude steps in a fixed stale direction → entropy collapses (→0.08), length explodes, val crashes.

EXP-37 (20/20, β0.5): terminal collapse ~step 61. EXP-37C (20/20, β0): oscillating collapse 35–41 → recover → re-collapse 88–100.

additive, direction-preserving

delayed_ef → non-convergent drag

Adding a stale codec-error term keeps Gcomp's direction, so it cannot sharpen into a sign-spiral — a higher ignition threshold (as predicted). But the stale off-policy correction acts as a persistent wrong-direction drag: entropy stays high and rises, reward stalls, the policy stays diffuse and cannot converge. Degradation, not ignition.

EXP-37E (20/20, β0.5), live @ step 80: entropy ≈2.0 and rising, length flat ~200 (no ignition), score stuck ~0.52.

This is the cleaner statement of the latency failure: K > τ makes the correction off-policy; the merger's geometry then routes that wrong-directioned force into either a sharpening spiral (sign-replace) or a diffusing drag (additive). Both are the same off-policy-staleness disease wearing different clothes.

3Latency as the control knob — why 5/5 is near-dense and 20/20 fails

Staleness is in optimizer ticks; 5/5 ≈ 2.5 global steps, 20/20 ≈ 10 global steps (batch 128 / mini 64 = 2 ticks/step). Define the policy-drift horizon τ: the number of steps over which πθ moves enough that its gradient distribution is materially different.

  • SFT: τ ≈ ∞ (target never moves) ⇒ latency-tolerant; any fixed K is fine.
  • GRPO: the policy moves every step ⇒ τ is small. K < τ ⇒ the anchor is still near-on-policy (stable); K > τ ⇒ the anchor is a wrong-distribution signal (fails). This is the crossover the 2×2 traces out.

Hypothesized that direction-preservation gives it a higher ignition threshold than sign-replacement, so it drags rather than ignites at 20/20 — which is exactly what live EXP-37E shows (§2.1).

4Why "it works for SFT" doesn't transfer — the imported assumption, stated precisely

Imported premise: M ≈ g(θt) — a K-stale full gradient is a good estimate of the current full gradient, so correcting the compressed fast gradient toward M moves the optimizer toward the true descent direction.

This premise is equivalent to stationarity of the gradient field over K steps.

  • Under SFT it holds: the field is g(θ; D₀); over K small steps it changes by a bounded smoothness term ⇒ M is a good proxy ⇒ the merger is free variance-reduction / free off-subspace recovery (exactly why error-feedback and stale-sign-SGD work in SL).
  • Under on-policy GRPO it fails: the field is g(θ; D(πθ)), which changes through θ and through the distribution πθ induces. M estimates a different field. The premise is violated in proportion to K/τ — which is why latency is the knob (§3) and why no reweighting/accumulating/perturbing/de-noising of M rescues it (σ(M) ceiling; EXP-31 tournament all-null).

So the transfer fails not because the merger is buggy in GRPO but because its correctness certificate (telescoping toward a stationary descent direction) has no on-policy analogue. Folding a faithful reconstruction of a stale-policy gradient into an on-policy step is, by construction, an off-policy update — and GRPO has a reward-hackable direction along which off-policy updates compound.

6Falsification & predictions

What would overturn this thesis (it must survive all of these):

  • F1 — On-policy fresh anchor (delay_K=0) is also unstable. Thesis predicts a fresh on-policy M = g(θt; D(πθt)) would be stable & near-dense. delay_K=0 is forbidden as a training config (unrealistic for a decentralized link) so this is a thought-experiment / diagnostic-only probe — but if a measurement-only fresh anchor still failed, the off-policy-staleness thesis would be wrong and the defect intrinsic to the merger.
  • F2 — Freezing the rollout policy (or IS-corrected off-policy GRPO) does NOT fix it. Thesis predicts that fixing rollouts to a reference policy (stationary field, gap-2 → 0) — or adding the IS reweighting that makes the estimator unbiased for ∇J(θt) — would remove the failure. If either still failed, the non-stationarity / missing-IS argument is falsified.
  • F3 — Lower latency does NOT always rescue. Thesis predicts failure probability monotone in K. If some K < 5/5 still failed, or 20/20 were sometimes stable independent of merger, the K/τ crossover breaks. (EXP-37B's 5/5 transient self-correcting excursion at steps 79–86 is expected near-threshold margin, not a falsification.)
  • F4 — A non-length objective still fails under the same stale carrier. Thesis predicts removing the free length variable removes the ignition attractor (consistent with the KL-guardrail result: KL bounds length and converts the α=0 catastrophe to ~parity without making the stale correction helpful). If length-penalized GRPO still spiraled, "length is the channel" fails.

Predictions:

  • EXP-37E (running): delayed_ef, 20/20, β0.5, 100 steps. 20/20 is past τ, so degradation was predicted — but because delayed_ef is additive/direction-preserving (higher ignition threshold than sign-replacement), the prediction was a milder failure mode than ignition. Live data at step 80 confirms it precisely: no length explosion (length flat ~200), entropy high and rising (≈2.0), score stuck ~0.52, val@25/50/75 = 0.649/0.676/0.581 — the non-convergent drag mode (§2.1), not the spiral. High-latency degradation is visible before any back-half event, consistent with a wrong-target correction depressing learning even while "stable." A clean, stable, near-dense val@100 would be a partial falsification of "latency past τ always destabilizes" (not of the core stationarity argument); the current trajectory does not point there.
  • The only stable comm-eff operating point remains 5/5 (K < τ). Surpassing dense requires injecting information outside σ(M) (cross-rank 2nd-moment / curvature routes) — not a better way to fold in M.

One-line restatement

A stale full gradient is a lagged estimate of the same descent direction under SFT's frozen target (latency-tolerant, telescoping) but the exact, low-variance, biased on-policy gradient of a defunct policy under on-policy GRPO — transplanted with no importance-sampling correction; GRPO additionally hands that persistent off-policy carrier a reward-flat direction (response length) to compound along, so the import that works in SL ignites (sign-replace) or drags (additive) in RL, and latency K relative to the policy-drift horizon τ is the dial between near-dense (5/5) and failure (20/20).

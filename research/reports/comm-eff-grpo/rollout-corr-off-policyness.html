in the Comm-Efficient GRPO Trainer

Headline. The big rollout_corr gap is the gap between dense and comm-eff, not between the stable and the collapsing comm-eff runs. rollout_corr measures one kind of off-policyness: the gap between the inference forward and the training forward. The off-policyness that actually breaks K=20 lives in the anchor gradient and is invisible to this tab.

1. What rollout_corr measures

Every step, two log-prob vectors are computed over the response tokens and compared:

  • rollout_log_probs: what vLLM recorded while generating, using a clean full-rank forward at the generator weights.
  • old_log_probs (the "actor" probs): what the FSDP actor gets when it re-forwards the same tokens.

In this fork the actor re-forward is not clean. The fast circuit no longer uses random activation masking; the codec is PowerSGD-style low-rank projection (config: mask.enabled=false, powersgd.enabled=true, rank=77). A forward hook replaces each boundary block's hidden-state output M with its rank-77 projection M_hat = (M @ Q) @ Q.T (powersgd_activation.py:173), keeping only 77 of 1536 dimensions, about 5 percent. The actor recomputes old_log_probs through this projected forward, while vLLM generated through the clean full-rank forward, so the two disagree. So:

Terminology. The verl code calls the compressed fast forward the "masked path" (for example transformer_impl.py:1209: "old_log_probs are from the MASKED path", versus the anchor's "UNMASKED forward"). That is a generic label for "codec on" versus "codec off", inherited from when PRF masking was the codec. In these runs it means the PowerSGD projection, not random masking, which is disabled.

rollout_corr here = vLLM full-rank forward vs the actor's rank-77 projected forward. It is a meter for how much the codec distorts the policy's own forward pass.

The tab keys:

  • rollout_actor_probs_pearson_corr: correlation of the two prob vectors. 1.0 = perfect agreement.
  • rollout_probs_diff_mean / max: mean and max of |actor_prob - rollout_prob| in probability space.
  • rollout_corr/kl is approximately training_log_ppl - rollout_log_ppl: how much more surprised the trainer is by the sampled tokens than the sampler was. It is the probability mass the projected forward loses on the tokens that were actually drawn.

2. Two kinds of off-policyness

Gap A

The normal off-policyness we measure

Sampling, or train vs inference, mismatch. The data was drawn by vLLM's full-rank policy, but the gradient is taken with respect to the rank-77 projected actor policy. Sampler does not equal trainer, so the run is off-policy in the importance-sampling sense.

This is exactly what rollout_corr shows. It is near zero in dense and a large, roughly constant codec tax in every comm-eff run. The loss partly defends against it by preferring rollout_log_probs as the ratio denominator (transformer_impl.py:1237).

Gap B

The anchor-gradient staleness (our custom setting)

The comm-efficient trainer corrects the compressed gradient with an anchor gradient. That anchor is the exact on-policy gradient of a policy that is K steps old, computed on K-step-old data, then applied at the current step with no importance reweighting. It is a valid gradient for the wrong, now defunct, policy.

This staleness grows with delay_K. It is what breaks K=20. It does not appear in rollout_corr, because that tab compares forward passes, not gradient directions.

4. Why rollout_corr does not explain the collapse

Read the three charts together. In the back half (steps 50 to 100) the K=20 score falls from 0.61 to 0.45 and the response length spikes near the end, yet its pearson (about 0.12 to 0.23) and its kl (about 5 to 6) are flat and basically equal to the stable K=5 run. If the train-inference gap were driving the failure, it would blow up as the run collapses. It does not.

So rollout_corr confirms that the codec makes the trainer's forward a meaningfully different network than the sampler (Gap A, present in K=5 and K=20 alike), but it cannot see the cause of the K=20 collapse. That cause is Gap B, the anchor-gradient staleness, which lives in the gradient and shows up instead in score, entropy, and length. Attributing the collapse to rollout_corr would be a misread.

5. The ideal setting

Recommended operating point

Keep Gap A handled, keep Gap B small

  • Importance reference. Use the true sampler rollout_log_probs as the ratio denominator, not the projected old_log_probs. This is already the default and it neutralizes most of Gap A even though the forward gap is large.
  • Codec rank. rank=77 (about 5 percent of 1536) is the locked operating point. Lowering it deepens the forward distortion in Gap A. Raising it costs communication, which defeats the purpose. The forward tax is the price of compression, not a bug to chase to zero.
  • Anchor latency, the binding knob. Keep cadence and delay_K low. K=5 is the stable regime; K=20 breaks both merger families (signed_ema ignites, delayed_ef stalls). Stay in the bias-tolerable zone where K is small relative to how fast the policy moves, roughly a couple of global steps of staleness.
  • Merger. Stay on the EMA family (signed_ema), alpha about 0.25, beta_anc anywhere in 0 to 0.5 (a flat tie; harm only appears at 0.75 and above).
  • Anchor owns Q. Refreshing the PowerSGD basis from the clean anchor pass is what keeps the projection well fit. At low latency this also keeps the early kl spike short.

The unresolved tension. The decentralized pipeline-parallel target wants high K, because a rare anchor sync is what makes the method cheap. Stability wants low K. Today latency, not the merger, is the binding constraint: at K=20 every merger fails. The open research direction is to make the anchor staleness-tolerant, so it behaves as a slow lagging reference that a fast swarm can use without the stale-gradient bias taking over, which would let K grow without igniting Gap B.

# Why the stale-anchor comm-eff method fails on on-policy RL (GRPO) but works for SFT

**Scope.** A scientific verdict, synthesized from this project's own runs and the
implemented mechanism (`verl/workers/comm_eff/spectral_filter.py`,
`state.py`). It answers: *why does folding a K-stale full gradient `M` into a
compressed fast gradient `G_comp` train stably under SFT but destabilize on-policy
GRPO?* Claims are tagged **[ESTABLISHED]** (has run evidence in this program) or
**[HYPOTHESIZED]** (mechanism-consistent, not yet directly isolated here).

---

## Headline verdict

The stale anchor exports one assumption from SFT: *a K-step-old full gradient is a
good proxy for the current full gradient.* That assumption is **true under
stationarity and false under on-policy non-stationarity.** In SFT the loss is a
fixed expectation over a frozen dataset, so `g(θ_{t−K})` and `g(θ_t)` are two lagged
estimates of descent on the *same* objective — bounded-lag, error-feedback-
recoverable, telescoping. In GRPO the gradient is an expectation over samples drawn
from the *current* policy `π_{θ_t}`; the anchor's gradient is for `π_{θ_{t−K}}`
(or a replayed stale batch), i.e. an **off-policy gradient for a policy that no
longer exists.** The merger then injects that wrong-distribution signal as a
*persistent, low-variance, cross-rank-identical* force. GRPO — uniquely — exposes a
reward-hackable degree of freedom (autoregressive response length under a normalized,
verifier-only advantage with no teacher forcing) along which that persistent force
rectifies into a length-explosion / entropy-sharpening spiral. **The instability is
not a property of compression, of the codec, or of data-revisiting; it is the
interaction of (a) a persistent off-policy carrier with (b) GRPO's free length
variable.** Staleness `K` is the control knob: small `K` keeps the anchor inside the
policy-drift horizon (near-dense, stable); large `K` pushes it past that horizon and
the carrier ignites. This is why "it works for SFT" does not transfer.

## 1. Stationary vs non-stationary target — the load-bearing distinction

The merger forms a correction from the anchor's stale gradient. The two production
mergers (`spectral_filter.py`):

- `delayed_ef` (B2): `δ(t) = M_rep(t) − G_comp_ring(t−K)`, `G_corr = G_comp + λ·δ`
  (additive, direction-preserving; `M_rep` is the paired-replay stale anchor grad).
- `signed_ema`: `G_corr = α·G_noisy + (1−α)·|G_noisy|·sign(M)` (sign taken from the
  stale anchor EMA `M`).

Write the descent objective's gradient as `g(θ; D)` where `D` is the data
distribution the gradient is taken over.

- **SFT.** `D` is a *frozen* dataset `D₀`. The label/target for every token is fixed.
  Hence `g(θ_{t−K}; D₀)` and `g(θ_t; D₀)` are gradients of the **same** function
  evaluated at two nearby parameter points. Their difference is `O(K·‖Δθ‖·L)`
  (Lipschitz/smoothness lag), a *bounded* error that error-feedback telescopes away:
  `Σ_t G_corr ≈ Σ_t g_full(t−K) + drift`, and the drift is the only residual. The
  stale gradient is a **valid lagged estimate of the same descent direction.**

- **GRPO (on-policy).** `D = D(π_{θ_t})` — the rollouts are sampled from the *current*
  policy, and the advantage is computed group-relative on *those* samples. The anchor
  replays `π_{θ_{t−K}}`'s batch (`replay_paired_batch=true` pairs the stale weights
  with the batch *those* weights generated — confirmed valid-M, EXP-29). So
  `M = g(θ_{t−K}; D(π_{θ_{t−K}}))`: a gradient on a **different data distribution**,
  generated by a **policy that no longer exists.** It is not a lagged estimate of
  `g(θ_t; D(π_{θ_t}))`; it is an **off-policy gradient.** There is no stationary
  function whose descent both are estimating — the function itself moved with the
  policy. **[ESTABLISHED]** that `M` is off-policy by construction
  (`async-anchor-single-node-fast-swarm`: the anchor "is ALWAYS behind, by
  construction"); **[ESTABLISHED]** that the comm-eff method is "SL/SFT-validated but
  dominated/unstable in GRPO" (`anchor-gradient-ema-beta0-grpo`,
  `no-merger-floor-0p63-not-0p74`).

The σ(M) ceiling sharpens this: any deterministic `Φ(G_comp, M)` is σ(M)-measurable
⇒ **capped at dense** — the merger can at best *reconstruct* the dense gradient on
stale data, never inject information dense lacks (`surpass-dense-sigma-m-ceiling-and-routes`,
`theory.md`). Under SFT, "the dense gradient on stale data" *is* a good target.
Under on-policy GRPO, "the dense gradient on a stale policy's data" is the wrong
target, and reconstructing it faithfully reconstructs an off-policy step.

## 2. The RL-specific ignition channel — why a persistent carrier explodes in GRPO and not SFT

A stale, low-variance correction is only dangerous if there is a direction along
which it can accumulate without being checked by the reward. GRPO supplies exactly
such a direction; SFT does not.

**The carrier (the persistence condition).** The merger injects a correction whose
direction changes *slowly relative to how often it is applied.* The carrier law
(issue #30, `canonical-anchor-comm-eff-base`): ignition needs the residual carrier's
autocorrelation time ≫ cadence — heuristically `‖Σₜ eₜ‖ ~ λ·T·‖G‖`, i.e. a
fixed-direction force summed over `T` ticks. Two implemented sources of persistence:

1. **EMA memory** `β_anc`: `M ← β·M + (1−β)·G_anchor`. Long-memory `β=0.95` *was* the
   ignition carrier (`exp25-collapse-gradient-flow`); `β_anc=0` (short hold) removes
   that memory and was the safe default. **[ESTABLISHED]** (`exp33-beta-anc-sweep`:
   harm only at β≥0.75).
2. **Staleness lag itself** `K`: even at `β_anc=0`, the held-δ in `delayed_ef` and the
   sign in `signed_ema` track a gradient that is `K` ticks behind the policy. At large
   `K` the *staleness* becomes the carrier — the correction is a persistent pull
   toward an old policy's preferred direction.

**The reward-hackable attractor (the RL-only part).** GRPO with vanilla,
verifier-only reward, group-normalized advantage, **no KL, no entropy bonus**, and
**no teacher forcing** leaves the autoregressive **response length** as a nearly
free variable: producing longer responses is reward-flat over a wide band (the
verifier scores correctness, not brevity), and the token-mean loss aggregation
gives a tail-amplification ratchet (`exp25-collapse-gradient-flow`: ~86× tail
amplification, dose sets the lag near-linearly). When a persistent tangential force
exists, it rectifies along this reward-flat "correct-but-longer" direction until the
ratchet locks in: response length runs away, entropy collapses *as a follower*, and
val falls.

- **[ESTABLISHED]** the killer is **length-hacking, not low entropy**: dense is the
  *lowest-entropy* run (≈0.12–0.16) and the *most stable*; entropy is a follower of
  the length spiral (×3 confirmations, `entropy-collapse-alpha0-signed-ema`,
  `exp25-collapse-gradient-flow`). EXP-37's tail: `response_length/mean` 189→683,
  entropy 0.81→0.42, `pg_clipfrac` 0.028→0.125, val 0.648→0.444 (EXP-37 verdict).
- **[ESTABLISHED]** the discriminator is **merger-carrier presence**, not grad
  noise: on the *same* PowerSGD+anchor substrate, plain (no merger) is clean while
  the merger emits long-tail spikes (controlled single-variable contrast,
  `canonical-anchor-comm-eff-base` §post-mortem). Compression is benign; the merger
  is the carrier.

**Why SFT lacks this channel.** SFT has *fixed targets* and *teacher forcing*: the
length of the supervised sequence is not a free variable the model can inflate for
reward — there is no reward, only a fixed cross-entropy to a fixed continuation. The
same persistent stale-gradient carrier in SFT simply biases the step slightly off
the fresh descent direction (a small, bounded, telescoping error); there is no
reward-flat direction for it to rectify into, so it never ignites. **The carrier is
necessary but not sufficient — GRPO supplies the sufficient second ingredient (the
free length variable); SFT does not.** **[HYPOTHESIZED, mechanism-grounded]** —
this program has no SFT runs of its own; the SFT side rests on the method's prior
SL-validation plus the established absence of the length channel.

## 3. Latency as the control knob — why 5/5 is near-dense and 20/20 collapses

Staleness is measured in optimizer ticks; cadence/delay `5/5` ≈ 2.5 global steps,
`20/20` ≈ 10 global steps (batch 128 / mini 64 = 2 ticks/step,
`anchor-cadence-delayk-unit-optimizer-ticks`). Define the **policy-drift horizon**
`τ`: the number of steps over which `π_θ` moves enough that its gradient distribution
is materially different.

- **SFT:** `τ ≈ ∞` (the target never moves) ⇒ the method is *latency-tolerant*; any
  fixed `K` is fine because `g(·; D₀)` barely changes the *target*.
- **GRPO:** the policy moves every step ⇒ `τ` is small (a handful of steps). When
  `K < τ` the stale anchor is still a near-on-policy reference (the policy hasn't
  drifted out from under it); when `K > τ` the anchor's gradient is for a meaningfully
  *different* policy — a wrong-distribution signal — and the carrier ignites.

The empirical 2×2 (signed_ema, this accel series) is exactly this crossover:

| latency | β_anc | back-half (steps 50–100) | val | run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5/5 | 0.5 | **STABLE** (one self-correcting excursion) | val@100 = 0.7347 | EXP-37B (PASS) |
| 20/20 | 0.5 | **TERMINAL** collapse ~step 61, no recovery | val@50 0.648 → collapse | EXP-37 (STOP) |
| 20/20 | 0.0 | **OSCILLATING** collapse 35–41 → recover → re-collapse 88–100 | val 0.68→0.54→0.70→0.35 | EXP-37C (STOP) |
| dense | — | STABLE, monotonic | val@100 = 0.7832 | EXP-37D |

**[ESTABLISHED]** the collapse is **latency-driven, not epoch/data-revisit-driven**:
EXP-37B sails through the GSM8K epoch-2 boundary (~step 58) at 5/5 unharmed, while
*both* 20/20 draws destabilize regardless of β (EXP-37B and EXP-37C verdicts). The
epoch boundary is the natural confound for "is this just data revisiting?" — it was
controlled out. Dense (EXP-37D) trains the *same* data for the *same* 100 steps and
is monotone-stable, so it is not the data either: the instability is specific to the
compressed stale-anchor path.

**β_anc trades onset-time for recoverability at high latency, but neither β is stable
at 20/20** (EXP-37C verdict): `β=0.5` (EMA) delays onset (~step 61) but makes the
collapse *terminal* (the accumulated stale sign locks in); `β=0` (no memory) ignites
*earlier* (~step 35) but recovers (then re-ignites). Both share the root cause:
`K=10`-step staleness. This is the load-bearing reading — at low latency `β` lives in
a flat free-averaging region (EXP-33), but raising `K` past `τ` makes the *staleness*,
not the EMA, the carrier, so the β knob can no longer save it.

**Why `delayed_ef` is near-dense at 5/5 yet at risk at 20/20.** `delayed_ef` is
additive and **direction-preserving** (`G_corr = G_comp + λ·δ`, no sign replacement) —
it keeps `G_comp`'s direction and only adds the dropped codec residual, so at small
`K` `δ` is a faithful, telescoping error-feedback term and the run is near-dense
(EXP-30: val@50 0.7528, **0.0008 under dense** — the best realistic result). At large
`K`, `δ = M_rep − G_comp_ring(t−K)` is the residual between a stale-policy anchor and
an even-staler ring entry: `δ` becomes a persistent pull toward a policy two lags
back, i.e. a carrier of the same off-policy type, applied additively every tick.
**[HYPOTHESIZED]** that direction-preservation gives `delayed_ef` a *higher ignition
threshold* than `signed_ema`'s sign-replacement (sign-replacement destroys
per-coordinate sign cancellation outright — 50.4% sign-disagreement at the first
warm step, `exp25-collapse-gradient-flow`), so 20/20 `delayed_ef` may degrade more
gracefully than 20/20 `signed_ema` — but it is still past the drift horizon. **This
is the open EXP-37E question.**

## 4. Why "it works for SFT" doesn't transfer — the imported assumption, stated precisely

The method imports one load-bearing premise from the SFT/SL setting where the
sign-merger and stale-anchor were validated:

> **Imported premise:** `M ≈ g(θ_t)` — a `K`-stale full gradient is a good estimate
> of the current full gradient, so correcting/replacing the compressed fast gradient
> toward `M` moves the optimizer toward the true descent direction.

This premise is *equivalent to stationarity of the gradient field over `K` steps.*
- Under SFT it holds: the field is `g(θ; D₀)`, and over `K` small steps it changes by
  a bounded smoothness term ⇒ `M` is a good proxy ⇒ the merger is **free
  variance-reduction / free off-subspace recovery** (which is exactly why
  error-feedback and sign-SGD-with-stale-sign work in SL).
- Under on-policy GRPO it fails: the field is `g(θ; D(π_θ))`, which changes not only
  through `θ` but through the *data distribution `π_θ` induces.* Over `K` steps that
  distribution can move arbitrarily (and does, fastest along the length axis once any
  pull exists). So `M` estimates `g(θ_{t−K}; D(π_{θ_{t−K}}))`, a different field. The
  premise is violated **precisely in proportion to `K/τ`** — which is why latency is
  the knob (§3) and why no reweighting/accumulating/perturbing/de-noising of `M`
  rescues it (σ(M) ceiling, EXP-31 tournament all-null).

So the transfer fails not because the merger is "buggy" in GRPO but because its
correctness certificate (telescoping toward a stationary descent direction) has no
on-policy analogue. Folding a faithful reconstruction of a stale-policy gradient into
an on-policy step is, by construction, an off-policy update — and GRPO has a
reward-hackable direction along which off-policy updates compound.

## 6. Falsification & predictions

**What would overturn this thesis (it must survive all of these):**

- **F1 — On-policy fresh anchor (`delay_K=0`) is *also* unstable.** Thesis predicts a
  fresh, on-policy `M` would be stable (it would be `g(θ_t; D(π_{θ_t}))` — the right
  target; merger → near-dense). `delay_K=0` is **forbidden as a training config**
  (unrealistic for a decentralized link, `canonical-anchor-comm-eff-base`) so this is
  a *thought experiment / diagnostic-only* probe — but if a measurement-only fresh
  anchor still ignited, the off-policy-staleness thesis would be wrong and the defect
  would be intrinsic to the merger regardless of staleness.
- **F2 — Freezing the rollout policy (or off-policy GRPO) does NOT remove the
  instability.** Thesis predicts that if rollouts were generated from a *fixed*
  reference policy (making the gradient field stationary, SFT-like) the carrier would
  stop igniting. If a frozen-rollout / fully off-policy GRPO variant still spiraled,
  the non-stationarity argument would be falsified and the cause would lie elsewhere
  (e.g. the codec, or GRPO's advantage normalization per se).
- **F3 — Lower latency does NOT always rescue.** Thesis predicts ignition probability
  is monotone in `K`. If some `K < 5/5` still ignited, or if `20/20` were sometimes
  stable independent of merger, the `K/τ` crossover story would break. (Note EXP-37B's
  5/5 *transient* excursion at steps 79–86 that self-corrected is the *expected*
  near-threshold behavior — a margin, not a falsification.)
- **F4 — A non-length objective (e.g. fixed-length or length-penalized GRPO) still
  ignites under the same stale carrier.** Thesis predicts removing the free length
  variable removes the attractor (consistent with the KL-guardrail result: KL bounds
  length and converts the α=0 catastrophe to ~parity *without* making the stale
  correction helpful, `exp25-collapse-gradient-flow`). If length-penalized GRPO still
  spiraled, the "length is the channel" claim would fail.

**Predictions:**

- **EXP-37E (running): `delayed_ef`, latency 20/20, β_anc=0.5, 100 steps.** Under this
  thesis, 20/20 is past the drift horizon, so the run is **expected to degrade and
  likely ignite in the back half (steps 50–100)** — but, because `delayed_ef` is
  additive/direction-preserving (higher ignition threshold than `signed_ema`'s
  sign-replacement, F15), the prediction is a **milder / later / possibly recoverable
  failure than EXP-37 (signed_ema 20/20), not a clean PASS.** Current state (step ~30):
  val@25 = 0.6490 — already well below dense's val@25 0.777 and below EXP-37B's 0.738,
  i.e. **high-latency degradation is visible before any ignition**, consistent with
  the off-policy-staleness premise (a wrong-target correction depresses learning even
  while the run is still "stable"). The decisive window is steps 50–100. A clean,
  stable, near-dense val@100 from EXP-37E would be a **partial falsification** of the
  "latency past `τ` always destabilizes" clause (it would show direction-preservation
  can tolerate `K=10`), though not of the core stationarity argument.
- **The only stable comm-eff operating point remains 5/5** (`K < τ`), where the stale
  anchor is still inside the policy-drift horizon. Surpassing dense requires injecting
  information *outside* σ(M) (cross-rank 2nd-moment / curvature routes,
  `surpass-dense-sigma-m-ceiling-and-routes`) — not a better way to fold in `M`.

### One-line restatement

A stale full gradient is a *lagged estimate of the same descent direction* under
SFT's frozen target (latency-tolerant, telescoping) but an *off-policy gradient for a
defunct policy* under on-policy GRPO; GRPO additionally hands that persistent
off-policy carrier a reward-flat direction (response length) to rectify into, so the
import that works in SL ignites in RL, and latency `K` relative to the policy-drift
horizon `τ` is the dial between near-dense (5/5) and collapse (20/20).

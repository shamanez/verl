EXP-27 damps the ef_powersgd residual (clip 1.0→0.5, decay 0.9→0.5) and runs for 100 steps. The dose was capped exactly as designed. The run ignited anyway at step 61, reached val@50 = 0.7202, and was killed at step 68. This tab records what happened, why, and what came next. Full analysis: runs/EXP-27/MECHANISM_ANALYSIS.md + runs/EXP-27/RUN_COMPARISON.md.

Mechanism: not true error feedback — a persistent tangential carrier

The implemented update (per matrix, per tick) is G_corr = G_t + e_t where e_t = δ·e_{t−1} + clip(comp_t) and comp_t = M_t − ⟨G_t,M_t⟩/‖G_t‖² · G_t. Three structural facts determine everything:

  1. comp_t ⊥ G_t exactly (by construction: spectral_filter.py:378–379). Every injected force is tangential — it neither helps nor hurts descent, but pushes sideways.
  2. M has ~50-global-step memory (\(\beta=0.95\), refresh every 2.5 global steps). The tangential push points in a direction that is effectively constant over the run length.
  3. The projection is nearly vacuous: \(\cos(G_{\text{anchor}}, G_{\text{comp}}) \approx 0.01\)–\(0.06\) (Step-A, EXP-26), so comp_t ≈ M — this is direct injection of the stale EMA, not residual correction.

A persistent tangential force on a reward-flat "correct-but-longer" manifold integrates linearly over time: \(\|\Sigma e_t\| \sim \lambda \cdot T \cdot \|G\|\). There is no telescoping cancellation (unlike classic Stich/Karimireddy error feedback), so the cumulative transport grows with run length. Dose magnitude controls only the lag, not whether ignition occurs.

Damping = delay, not prevention. EF dose ratio ef r1/EXP-27 = 0.200/0.092 = 2.17×; lock-in step ratio = s30/s61 = 2.03× — near-exactly linear. EXP-27 ignited at its dose minimum (~0.02, steps 45–67). Cutting clip/decay further would extend the lag further but cannot prevent transport along the reward-flat direction. Every per-step health metric (cos(G,G+e) ≥ 0.982, score 0.73–0.84) was correct and certifies only the descent component — it says nothing about the tangential one. Full derivation: runs/EXP-27/MECHANISM_ANALYSIS.md §§(a)–(h).

Carrier vs substrate: the single-knob isolate

plain (u1v94opv) differs from ef r2 (tilwe80t) in exactly one knob: spectral.enabled (False vs True). Same PowerSGD codec, same stale-anchor refresh, same locked surface.

  • plain (merger OFF): len/mean slope −1.46/step, len/max never exceeds 826 after s30, zero 16384 pins after warmup, entropy stable at 0.48–0.55. Zero carrier signature.
  • ef r2 (merger ON): repeated long-tail spikes (16384@s27, 7817@s32, 4061@s47) that plain never produces. First-passage-lucky (spikes never clustered consecutively in 50 steps) — but the emission is present.

The PowerSGD+anchor substrate is stable on its own. The merger/carrier is what injects the reward-flat force. Honest caveat: plain is a 50-step censored observation; the airtight version requires a plain@100 run (Prediction P2 below).

What’s next: decision tree

Top fix (core-eligible): rebuild EF as true error feedback on the codec’s own dropped residual. At the boundary, the upstream stage keeps e ← (dL/dÂ + e) − C(dL/dÂ + e) sender-local (zero extra communication — memory never crosses the boundary). Telescoping then gives cumulative-applied = cumulative-true ± bounded residual — no exogenous carrier, no O(T) integral. Predicted: P(no ignition) ≈ 0.85; P(val ≥ 0.7414) ≈ 0.3. This retires M from the fast path (anchor keeps Q-ownership only).

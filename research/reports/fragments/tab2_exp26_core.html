EXP-26: The Core Idea — Measure the Geometry Before You Spend on the Next Guess

EXP-25 spent a full multi-arm training campaign to falsify one bad idea (the "signed_ema" merger). EXP-26 refused to make the same trade: before committing any expensive training run, it ran a cheap geometry audit on real gradients to decide which guess was worth the GPU-hours. That single discipline — diagnose first, then spend — is the whole story of this experiment, and it led to the best realistic communication-efficient result the program has produced (val@50 of 0.7210), still 2 points short of parity, with a precise reason why.

The setup, in one paragraph

The trainer compresses the activations that cross a pipeline-stage boundary by projecting each one onto a shared rank-77 basis \(Q\) (the activation space is 1536-dimensional, so this is a 20× squeeze). Compression loses information, so an anchor circuit runs alongside: every few optimizer ticks it does one full, uncompressed forward/backward from a deliberately stale weight snapshot, uses that to refresh and own \(Q\), and maintains a running average \(M\) of the anchor's true gradient. A merger then folds \(M\) into the cheap compressed gradient \(G_{\text{comp}}\) before the optimizer sees it, producing the corrected gradient \(G_{\text{corr}}\). EXP-25's merger replaced the sign of the compressed gradient with the sign of \(M\); EXP-26 set out to understand exactly why that failed and what to try instead. The substrate wiring is in CODE_WALKTHROUGH.md (the anchor "owns Q" rule, the merger math, and the ordering invariant).

The meta-idea. After an expensive falsification, do not "guess a merger and burn 50 GPU-hours." Instead, dump the real tensors the question turns on — the activations, their reconstructions, the basis \(Q\), the compressed gradient, the merger's output, and the anchor's gradient — in full fp32, from a run that lasts only about 10 optimizer ticks, and let the geometry decide which expensive arm runs next. The plan (research/.claude/plans/26.md) makes Step A a hard gate: Steps B/C/E do not launch until the audit returns a machine-readable decision.

Step A: three questions, one cheap diagnostic

The audit (research/runs/EXP-26/stepA_decision.md) tested three hypotheses about where EXP-25's deficit actually lived. Each one is a claim about angles and energy in gradient space, not about a training curve — that is the point of measuring geometry.

HypothesisClaimWhat Step A measuredVerdict
H1 The merger corrupts the update direction; compression itself is harmless. Angle between the merger's output and its own compressed input, \(\cos(G_{\text{comp}}, G_{\text{corr}}) = 0.717\) (about a 44° rotation). Confirmed — the merger really does swing the update sideways.
H2 The activation basis \(Q\) captures the activations well but misses most of the GRPO update energy. Activation capture 0.9985 vs. update-energy capture 0.318; about 68% of the update energy lives outside \(Q\). True (caveated) — the basis content is implicated.
H3 The sign-replacement disagreement is structural (a coin-flip), not a staleness artifact. Sign-agreement between \(M\) and \(G_{\text{comp}}\): 0.500 / 0.523 / 0.520 across delay settings 0 and 5. Confirmed — even a perfectly fresh anchor agrees on the sign only half the time.

Read together, the story is unambiguous. H3 says the sign term is structurally hopeless: a fresh anchor disagrees on the sign of the update as often as a fair coin, so no amount of fixing staleness can rescue it. H1 says the merger's actual harm is a directional rotation, and H2 says the basis itself leaves most of the real update energy on the table. The emitted decision was go_C_then_B with retire_sign_replacement(confirmed): drop the sign term forever, and because the basis content is suspect, test a better basis (Step C) before the new merger (Step B).

An honest caveat baked into the audit. The textbook discriminator — the angle between the compressed update and a true dense update — turned out not to be cleanly measurable here. The anchor's reference gradient comes from a clean policy-gradient loss, while the fast path's gradient comes from the clipped PPO loss, and PowerSGD compresses the boundary activation gradient rather than the weight gradient. Those two mismatches push the literal cosine toward zero for reasons that have nothing to do with compression. The team documented this rather than papering over it, and switched to a confound-free discriminator, \(\cos(G_{\text{comp}}, G_{\text{corr}})\), the merger versus its own input, which never references a dense gradient (research/runs/EXP-26/stepA_verdict.md).

The staged sequence that followed

Everything after Step A was conditional on Step A. The flow below is the actual order of operations — note that two of the four stages cost no extra training runs at all.

  1. Step A — geometry audit (about 10 ticks). Dump real fp32 tensors from three reference configs (dense, plain compression, the old signed_ema merger) on identical prompts. Decide H1/H2/H3. Outcome: go_C_then_B + retire sign-replacement.
  2. Step C — basis-content sweep (one short run). Because H2 was open, six candidate "families" for what \(Q\) should span were built passively inside a single run and scored against the same captured reference gradients (research/runs/EXP-26/STEP_C_SPEC.md). The geometry winner ("hybrid") then got one real training arm — and failed: val@50 of 0.3730. The basis that maximized update-energy capture anti-converted, because the vLLM rollouts run uncompressed, so a basis that sacrifices activation fidelity for update energy destroys train-vs-rollout consistency (research/runs/EXP-26/stepC_screen_report.md). H2's strong form is dead.
  3. Step B — the new merger. The successor "ef_powersgd" merger re-injects the energy the projection dropped, with no sign term at all. Arms: error-feedback, plain compression, and a dense reference. This produced the headline result.
  4. Step E — measure the bytes (no extra runs). Byte counters wired into the Step B/C runs measured the actual boundary traffic: a ratio of 0.0506, roughly a 19.8× reduction, read straight from the live training runs.

The new merger's idea is worth stating plainly. Projecting onto the rank-77 basis throws away whatever component of the gradient lives outside that subspace. Rather than guess at a correction, the error-feedback merger keeps a running, clipped, detached buffer of exactly that dropped residual and adds it back next time, in the same direction as the compressed update:

$$ e_t = \operatorname{clip}\!\big(\, \texttt{ef\_decay}\cdot e_{t-1} + (M - P_{G_{\text{comp}}}(M)),\ \ \texttt{ef\_clip}\cdot\lVert G_{\text{comp}}\rVert \big), \qquad G_{\text{corr}} = G_{\text{comp}} + e_t $$

Here \(M\) is the anchor's gradient average and \(P_{G_{\text{comp}}}(M)\) is the part of it already captured by the compressed update, so \(M - P_{G_{\text{comp}}}(M)\) is the off-subspace energy the projection missed; \(e_t\) accumulates that residual, clipped relative to the compressed gradient's norm and decayed by ef_decay. Crucially there is no \(\operatorname{sign}(\cdot)\) anywhere — the correction adds energy in the existing direction instead of flipping it. The limiting case (zero clip, zero decay) is bit-identical to plain compression, which the code enforces as a test (verl/workers/comm_eff/spectral_filter.py).

The guardrails that made the numbers trustworthy

None of this would mean anything if the diagnostic quietly cheated. Two classes of gate bound every stage. The realism guardrails kept the experiment honest about the decentralized target: the anchor is the only thing that updates \(Q\) and the fast path can only read it; the only full uncompressed pass lives inside the anchor; the anchor staleness is mandatory (never zero on the training path); and there is no "clean step" that secretly transmits a full-rank gradient. The correctness gates, probed before any GPU-hours were spent on a sweep, proved the instrumentation was inert: off-path parity (the capture hooks change no numbers), the measurement-only probes never feed the optimizer (verified bit-for-bit on the optimizer state), and the dumped tensors are genuinely fp32 (reconstruction error matched the logged scalar to within \(4.5\times10^{-5}\)). Every one of these was a hard gate: a failure aborts before the expensive work.

One-screen outcome

The new merger (ef_powersgd) is the best realistic communication-efficient result the program has produced. It preserves the update direction (\(\cos(G_{\text{comp}}, G_{\text{corr}}) = 0.9558\), versus the old merger's 0.717), beats the falsified EXP-25 signed_ema by 1.4 points and the plain codec on the same realistic substrate by 7.7 points, with no deterministic collapse on the promoted run. But it lands at val@50 0.7210 against a parity target of 0.7414 — short by 2.04 points — so the terminal verdict is REVISE, carried forward as issue #27 (research/runs/EXP-26/verdict.md). What the staging bought. The audit retired a whole class of mergers (anything sign-based) on one cheap run, screened six basis candidates in a single passive run instead of six training runs, and measured the 19.8× byte saving with zero additional spend. The one expensive failure — the hybrid basis arm — was itself informative: it proved that a basis tuned for update-energy capture wrecks the uncompressed rollout path, closing off H2 empirically. Tab 5 carries the strategic implications; the takeaway here is the method, which is to let measured geometry, not intuition, choose where the GPU budget goes.

Where we are

What is proven. The substrate is realistic and mechanically correct: the anchor is the only thing that updates the projection basis, the fast path can only read it, the only full uncompressed pass lives inside the anchor, and there is no hidden full-rank "clean step." Compression is real and large: the measured boundary-activation traffic is a ratio of 0.0506, about a 19.8× reduction. And the central thesis of EXP-26 holds — error-feedback closes most of the merger gap. Removing the sign term and re-injecting the energy the projection dropped lifts the result +7.7 points over the plain codec on the same substrate and preserves the update direction almost perfectly: \(\cos(G_{\text{comp}}, G_{\text{corr}}) = 0.956\) for error-feedback versus 0.717 for the old sign-replacement merger.

What is falsified. Two ideas are dead, each on clean evidence. Sign-replacement is structurally hopeless: even a perfectly fresh anchor agrees on the sign of the update only about half the time (0.500–0.523), so no amount of fixing staleness rescues it. And the "tune the basis for update energy" idea is empirically falsified: the basis that maximized GRPO update-energy capture (Step C's hybrid \(Q\)) anti-converted to val@50 0.3730, because the vLLM rollouts run uncompressed — a basis that sacrifices activation fidelity for update energy destroys train-versus-rollout consistency. Geometry wins do not convert when generation is uncompressed.

What is newly worrying. Three findings temper the optimism and should shape the next decisions.
  • The stale-anchor \(Q\) refresh is itself a drag. Plain compression on the realistic substrate (0.6437) lands below the frozen-\(Q\) floor (0.6914). The substrate's own basis-refresh mechanism degrades training, and the merger is partly compensating for that drag rather than purely correcting compression noise. This inverts EXP-25's "the merger is net-harmful" framing.
  • Stochastic ignition at clip 1.0. Of two identical error-feedback runs, one fired the full length-explosion fingerprint (response length pinned at 16384, entropy collapse) and ran out of memory at step 42; the other completed clean. The residual dose grows with anchor staleness (0.32 rising to 0.47), and ignition risk is dose-correlated — a real, undamped hazard.
  • Single-seed noise on close calls. The promoted run's mid-curve val swung between 0.6232 and 0.6740 at the same step across realizations — roughly 5 points. When a comparison is within 2–3 points, one seed is not enough to call it.

What's next: the committed move (issue #27)

Issue #27 (research/.claude/plans/27.md, status planned, awaiting operator approval) is the REVISE child of EXP-26. It changes three knobs and nothing else — the code is already merged to vast-ai-workload, so this is config-only:

  1. Cap the residual dose — error-feedback clip 1.0 → 0.5. Ignition risk is dose-correlated, and the dose climbs with staleness; halving the per-tick injection damps the stochastic length-explosion while keeping the direction-preserving correction that bought +7.7 points.
  2. Forget faster — error-feedback decay 0.9 → 0.5, so the dropped-energy buffer cannot accumulate into a large delayed kick as the basis goes stale.
  3. Run longer — 50 → 100 steps. The 0.674→0.721 curve was still climbing with shrinking response lengths and no peak-then-crash; parity is only 2.0 points away on a monotone-up trajectory.

The gates are explicit (research/runs/EXP-26/verdict.md next_actions): PASS if best val@{50,75,100} ≥ 0.7414; STOP if best val ≤ 0.7210 (damping helped nothing) or ignition fires even at the damped settings; REVISE (one final cycle) if it lands strictly between. One REVISE iteration remains on this lineage after #27.

How to move forward: the decision tree beyond #27

The branch we take depends entirely on where #27 lands. Ranked by likelihood and payoff:

  1. If #27 PASSES (≥ 0.7414). This is parity at ≈20× less boundary communication — the M6 milestone claim is complete: stable, parity, measured savings, one launcher. The follow-through is to promote the canonical launcher per the parent plan's rule (a human decision, not automatic), then consider secondary claims around exploration/pass@k diversity (PowerSGD carries real, uncompressed-corroborated policy diversity that has not yet converted to reward — see runs/EXP-25/PATH_TO_SURPASS_DENSE.md). Scaling to other models or datasets is out of the fixed control surface and is an operator decision, not a researcher one.
  2. If #27 lands between 0.7210 and 0.7414. One final REVISE — the obvious single knob is clip 0.5 → 0.25, or decay-only damping — and then the lineage stops by iteration budget. The write-up becomes: error-feedback is the right primitive, it closes the merger gap and preserves direction, but it asymptotes a couple of points under parity on this substrate. That is an honest and publishable negative-leaning result.
  3. If #27 is ≤ 0.7210 or ignites. Damping the merger did not help, so the substrate question becomes primary: why does the stale-anchor \(Q\)-refresh underperform a frozen basis (plain 0.6437 < floor 0.6914)? Three candidate lineages, ranked by cost:
    • (a) Cheapest untested combination — frozen-after-warmup \(Q\) plus error-feedback. The arithmetic is suggestive: the frozen-\(Q\) floor is 0.6914 and error-feedback added +7.7 points over plain on the refreshing substrate; if that gain transfers, 0.6914 + 0.077 already clears parity. This isolates the basis-refresh drag from the merger and is the first thing to try.
    • (b) Classical per-step error feedback. Accumulate the fast path's own compression residual rather than the anchor-EMA proxy — closer to the Stich/Vogels theory the current merger approximates.
    • (c) Variance-controlled unbiased masking plus error feedback. The EXP-25 forward-primitive recommendation: a zero-mean, variance-tunable perturbation as the forward codec, paired with error feedback on the backward path.

Explicitly out of scope until parity is in hand: chasing a surpass-dense edge (the surpass thesis is hypothetical and rests on a conversion mechanism we have not demonstrated — see runs/EXP-25/SURPASS_DENSE_STRATEGY.md), changing the GRPO objective, or relaxing any realism invariant. Parity first; everything else is a secondary claim layered on top of it.

Risks and craft lessons worth carrying

  • Budget is real. EXP-26 cost roughly $316 of box time. Staging and audit-gating (diagnose-then-spend) avoided a six-arm training sweep by screening basis candidates passively in a single run — that discipline is what keeps the lineage affordable.
  • Stochastic ignition argues for standing safeguards. The collapse-watch on entropy/response-length and expandable_segments memory allocation should be defaults on every run, not opt-ins; the parent's clip-1.0 OOM is the case study.
  • Single seeds limit close calls. When a result is within 2–3 points of a gate, add seeds or extend the horizon before calling it. #27's longer 100-step horizon partly addresses this for free.

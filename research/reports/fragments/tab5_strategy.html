Where We Are Now: Parity Reached, Surpass Open

As of 2026-06-16 the program has hit its core milestone. The communication-efficient GRPO trainer is stable, reaches parity with the dense control within evaluation noise, demonstrates a large measured communication saving, and is reproducible from one canonical launcher. The default and state of the art is error feedback — the EXP-30 B2 merger (delayed_ef, λ=1, βanc=0): GSM8K val@50 ≈ 0.74–0.75, inside the dense band of 0.75–0.78, at roughly 5% of the fast-path gradient-communication cost. The sign method (signed_ema) that earlier experiments declared dead now learns — on the corrected anchor circuit it is stable and near-parity — but it is dominated by error feedback and is not the default. The open question is no longer parity — it is whether anything can surpass dense. This tab is the honest current scoreboard, the one insight that unlocked the learning (βanc=0, below), and the to-dos that remain.

The insight that unlocked it — βanc = 0

The anchor maintains an EMA of its gradient, \(M \leftarrow \beta_{\text{anc}}\cdot M + (1-\beta_{\text{anc}})\cdot G_{\text{anchor}}\), and the merger feeds that \(M\) into the correction. The single most important setting we found is \(\beta_{\text{anc}} = 0\): \(M\) becomes the freshest anchor gradient only, with no accumulation of older ones. That is what gives good results.

Why — the RL insight (the biggest finding). RL policy gradients are non-stationary: a gradient is only valid at the policy that produced it. Accumulating old policy gradients (\(\beta_{\text{anc}}>0\)) folds in stale, off-policy, biased directions, which hurts — and is exactly the kind of persistent stale direction that integrates into the reward-flat length-explosion ignition (the EXP-27 tab). This is the opposite of supervised learning, where momentum / EMA (\(\beta\approx0.9\)) helps because the loss surface is stationary. So “politely remembering” old policy gradients is the wrong instinct here; using only the freshest one (\(\beta_{\text{anc}}=0\)) is right. Both the SOTA error-feedback merger (B2) and the now-working sign method run at \(\beta_{\text{anc}}=0\).

What is settled

  • The substrate is realistic and mechanically correct. The anchor is the only thing that updates the projection basis \(Q\), the fast path can only read it, the only uncompressed pass lives inside the anchor, and there is no hidden full-rank “clean step.”
  • The anchor's gradient is now honest (#29) — the key enabler. Before #29 the anchor forwarded the current batch through its delay_K-stale weights, producing an off-policy “wrong-trajectory” gradient. Since EXP-29 it grades the trajectories its own stale weights generated — on-policy for those weights, so the importance ratio is \(\equiv 1\) (a valid stale-policy REINFORCE gradient), not a biased off-policy estimate. The operator's framing: the anchor stops politely deferring to mismatched current-policy data and uses its own honest gradient. Every pre-#29 verdict that rested on the old wrong-data confound is superseded (see below).
  • Parity is reached (within evaluation noise). EXP-31's apples-to-apples dense rerun put the bar at 0.7506; B2's 0.7400–0.7528 lineage sits inside the dense band. The “0.044 gap to dense” earlier experiments chased was a wrong-reference artifact.
  • The saving is real and measured. Boundary-activation traffic is cut ~19.8× on the fast path (byte ratio ~0.0505), ~4× once the anchor's own traffic is honestly amortized in — read straight off the live runs, not estimated.
  • It reproduces from one launcher. The exact B2 knob set is frozen as the canonical baseline (runs/EXP-31/B2_baseline/) that every later arm builds on.

Works, but dominated

  • Sign-replacement (signed_ema) now learns — superseded verdict. EXP-25 declared it “net-harmful at every \(\alpha\), dead.” That verdict rested on the off-policy invalid-M anchor: the merger was steering toward a wrong-trajectory gradient. Re-run on the corrected (#29 valid-M, \(\beta_{\text{anc}}=0\)) circuit, signed_ema \(\alpha=0.5\) is stable and near-parity: val@50 0.7271 (0.7278@25 → 0.7271@50, no length-hack ignition; EXP-32, W&B cwz1hu5p). So the sign method is works-but-dominated, not dead — it sits below error feedback (B2, ~0.74–0.75) and above the no-merger floor (0.6300), so it is a valid corrector but not the default. The sign-disagreement geometry is real, but with an honest anchor the agreeing half carries enough signal to learn.

What is closed or falsified

  • Update-energy / hybrid basis (Step C) — a basis tuned for gradient energy anti-converts because rollouts run uncompressed (EXP-26, val 0.373). Dead.
  • The M-fed exogenous-carrier merger class — any correction that injects a persistent stale direction integrates into a reward-flat length-explosion (EXP-27 ignition at step ~61). B2's endogenous residual avoids this; the carrier family is retired.
  • The entire anchor-usage axis for surpass — re-weighting, accumulating, perturbing, and de-noising the correction \(\delta\) all cap at parity, because \(\delta\) reconstructs the dense gradient on stale data and you cannot beat dense by manipulating a stale copy of dense (EXP-31, four levers, all null → STOP). Do not re-propose anchor-usage levers.

Next steps

The two method questions are answered: error feedback (delayed_ef) stays the default / best, and the sign method is closed as works-but-dominated — EXP-32 gave it its first stable number on the corrected circuit, and it lands below error feedback. What remains is confirmation and the surpass frontier.

  1. (Optional) Confirm the non-stationarity hypothesis with an explicit \(\beta_{\text{anc}}>0\) run. The \(\beta_{\text{anc}}=0\) headline is an inference from the carrier-ignition mechanism; one run with \(\beta_{\text{anc}}>0\) on the B2 substrate would directly show that accumulating old policy gradients hurts, turning the non-stationarity argument from theory into a measured ablation. Cheap, bounded, needs no new code.
  2. Upgrade parity from “within noise” to “established” (cheap, bounded). Both the dense bar and B2 are single draws inside a ~±0.024 band. Seed replicates of both arms would pin the two bands and turn the statistical tie into a multi-seed result. It needs no new code.
  3. Surpass requires re-scoping the locked surface — a new plan + operator decision, not a lever. The deep mechanism says a comm-efficient method can only exceed dense by injecting a signal dense itself lacks, and the anchor-usage axis cannot supply it (EXP-31 STOP). The remaining frontier — low-but-nonzero odds — is the additive off-principal sub-basis route: a forward signal that captures the stable off-principal mode the act-basis \(Q\) misses (avoiding Step-C's update-energy trap). Adjacent operator-decision candidates: a generation-side diversity change (higher \(n\), rollout temperature) converting to a pass@k or relocated-mode edge — the only compression-specific bet, but it touches the sacred uncompressed generation path — or a fundamentally different decentralized-RL objective. Each is a deliberate change to the fixed control surface, not a researcher tuning step.
Conventions in force. 55-step training is now standard — a 5-step buffer past val@50 so the final validation accuracy syncs to W&B before the run exits. \(\beta_{\text{anc}}=0\) (freshest anchor gradient only) is the default for every merger. The B2 knob set in runs/EXP-31/B2_baseline/ is the substrate every new arm holds fixed.
The honest bottom line. Communication-efficient GRPO matches dense at ~5% of the gradient-communication cost, on a realistic decentralized-pipeline substrate, reproducibly — the M6 milestone in everything but a multi-seed parity band. Error feedback is the default that gets there; the sign method now learns too once the anchor is honest, just not as well. The unlock was conceptual as much as mechanical: make the anchor's gradient a valid policy gradient (#29) and keep only the freshest one (\(\beta_{\text{anc}}=0\)), because RL gradients are non-stationary and accumulating stale ones hurts. The surpass-dense thesis, as far as the anchor-usage axis can test it, is falsified: you cannot out-perform dense by being a cleverer copy of dense. Any next step toward surpassing is a deliberate change to the locked surface, made with eyes open, not another merger to tune.

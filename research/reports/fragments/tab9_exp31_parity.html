EXP-31: Parity Confirmed, and the Anchor-Usage Axis Exhausted

EXP-31 set out to surpass dense and returned two results instead. First, a measurement that reframed the whole scoreboard: re-running the dense control apples-to-apples — same box, same code, same seed — put the dense bar at 0.7506, not the 0.7839 EXP-30 had chased, which makes the SOTA B2 a statistical tie with dense. Second, a clean falsification: a four-lever tournament over every admissible way to use the stale anchor gradient came back null across the board. The honest verdict is STOP on the anchor-usage axis — parity is real and reproduced, but nothing in how we re-weight, accumulate, perturb, or de-noise the anchor signal pushes past dense.

Result 1 — the dense-reference reframe (parity is real)

EXP-30 left one binding loose end: its “dense = 0.7839” reference was a single draw measured on a different box and configuration than the comm-eff arms. EXP-31 closed it by re-running the dense control on the exact EXP-31 box, seed 0, with the identical disable_custom_all_reduce setting every comm-eff arm uses. The result:

Dense on this task is best read as a band ≈ 0.75–0.78 (single-draw rollout nondeterminism is ~±0.024), and B2's 0.74–0.75 lineage sits squarely inside it. The headline of the whole program follows: communication-efficient GRPO already matches dense at ~5% of the gradient-communication cost. The “0.044 gap to dense” EXP-31 was chartered to close did not exist — it was an artifact of comparing against a high draw on the wrong machine.

The remaining honesty. Both numbers are single draws inside a ~±0.024 band, so this is statistical parity, not a multi-seed proof that the means coincide. Pinning the dense and B2 bands with seed replicates is the one measurement that would upgrade “parity within noise” to “parity established.” That, and the surpass question, are the only things left open — see the final tab.

The sub-basis probe: faster, but not a better optimum

Before the tournament, EXP-31 tested its most mechanistically-motivated surpass idea: a rank-2 sub-basis correction that adds back the off-principal gradient mode the act-basis \(Q\) misses (the stable-rank-2 object finding F3 had flagged). Mechanically it worked — it captured ~88–90% of the off-principal energy and was bitwise-B2 at rank 0 — and it accelerated early learning (+0.036 over B2 at step 25). But at constant weight it over-amplified near convergence and regressed by step 50; a decaying weight removed the regression but also tempered the early gain. Every variant clustered at ~0.70–0.74 — none cleared the dense band.

The lesson that set up the tournament. The sub-basis speeds the path to the optimum but does not find a better optimum. That is the recurring shape of every comm-eff correction the program has built: it can recover what compression lost and converge faster, but it converges to the same place dense does. To go past dense you need a signal dense itself lacks — which is exactly the hypothesis the four-lever tournament was designed to test and exhaust.

Not one lever cleared even the val@25 continue-to-improve bar. The best (L4) sat 0.0045 below B2; the worst (L2 at μ=0.9) actively regressed by 0.15. L1 was gated out on its pre-registered condition: a control variate needs the anchor to be correlated with the compressed gradient to cancel variance, but the program has measured that correlation at ~0 (the same orthogonality that closed every blend gate), so an M-baseline would add variance, not reduce it. The process criteria all passed — off-path parity bitwise, byte-ratio identical to B2, B2 itself reproduced the band, no ignition or OOM — and an 8-agent adversarial code review confirmed every lever did exactly what its math intended. These are trustworthy nulls, not code artifacts.

Why all four are null — the mechanistic close

You cannot beat dense by manipulating a stale reconstruction of dense. The correction \(\delta\) reconstructs the dense gradient evaluated on stale data. Re-weighting it (L3), accumulating it (L2), perturbing it (L4), or de-noising it (L1) all operate on that same stale estimate of the dense gradient — so each, at best, recovers dense and lands at parity, and at worst (L2 μ=0.9) just piles staleness on staleness. There is no interior optimum to tune toward, because the target the correction reconstructs is dense. To exceed dense, the comm-efficient path would have to inject a signal dense genuinely lacks — and no way of using the anchor produces such a signal.

What surpass would actually require (and why it is out of scope here)

The deep mechanism rules out the entire “manipulate δ” family. Every credible surpass route now lies outside the anchor-usage axis and outside this experiment's locked surface:

  • a different forward basis that captures the stable-rank-2 off-principal mode the act-basis \(Q\) misses — but that is the forward \(Q\), a locked / Step-C axis (and Step-C's update-energy basis already anti-converted, EXP-26);
  • a generation-side diversity change (higher \(n\), rollout temperature) converting to a pass@k or relocated-mode edge — explicitly locked off here, since generation runs uncompressed and is sacred;
  • a fundamentally different decentralized-RL objective, not a re-use of the same stale dense estimate.

None is a non-blocked knob in EXP-31, so there is no admissible REVISE — a future attempt has to re-scope the locked surface with a new plan and an operator decision, not iterate a lever. That is what makes STOP the honest verdict: the anchor-usage axis is complete and exhausted, and the program is at defensible parity. Where that leaves us is the final tab.

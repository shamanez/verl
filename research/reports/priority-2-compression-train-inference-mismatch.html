Reducing the compression-induced train–inference mismatch (“Gap A”)

Distilled from the 2026-06-23 compression-mismatch study. The forward-pass codec distorts the recomputed log-probs vs vLLM’s rollout log-probs — how much does it matter, and how do we shrink it?

Verdict: the compression-induced mismatch is tolerable, not fundamental. “Gap A” (the codec’s forward-pass distortion) is a bounded off-policy bias — a ~0.04 parity tax that is constant in stable and collapsing runs alike, and GRPO absorbs it. It is not the cause of collapse.

The real blocker is the other mismatch — Gap B = anchor-gradient staleness (= Priority 1). Gap B converts the mismatch into bias you cannot cancel, and is tolerable only while staleness K stays under the policy-drift horizon τ.

Why Gap A is benign

  • Generation is clean. Rollouts run the uncompressed model on clean tokens; compression only reconstructs the activations/gradient, not the sampled trajectories. So rewards and trajectories are computed on clean text — trajectory corruption is low.
  • It’s a bounded gradient bias. Small codec reconstruction error yet a measurable recompute-vs-vLLM gap (rollout-corr ρ ≈ 0.1–0.3); it shifts the gradient estimate by a constant, IS-correctable amount — not a divergence trigger.
  • σ(M) ceiling. No merger can launder staleness (Gap B) into a win; Gap A is the addressable one here.

The lever — shrink / correct Gap A

The standard corrector is truncated importance sampling against the vLLM rollout log-probs, currently available but unused (old_log_prob is recomputed common-mode, not vLLM-referenced). Either reduce the forward-pass distortion directly, or switch the corrector on, to drive the ~0.04 codec tax toward zero. Issue #40’s FP8 rollout-only probe isolates the precision component of this gap.

The k-collapse — un-rotate the stale anchor gradient by projecting the weights forward

Distilled from the EXP-37 latency×merger map and the EXP-38 dense-drift probe (#38). Full run dirs de-bloated; numbers below are the durable record.

The problem. The anchor circuit computes its full gradient on delay_K-stale weights. That gradient goes orthogonal fast: its cosine to the live gradient decays 0.51 → 0.18 (@k=5, our operating delay) → ~0 (@k=10) → −0.01 (@k=20). Magnitude is preserved and sign becomes a coin-flip, so it is a pure rotation, not a decay — by k ≈ 10–20 the anchor is unusable. EXP-37 confirmed this operationally: the anchor circuit is stable at cadence/delay 5/5 but breaks at 20/20.

The fix. Don’t consume the stale gradient as-is — project the weight trajectory forward (continue the anchor’s own momentum) and realign the gradient there, so it approximates the gradient at the current weights.

Big-Math is the stress case: near-orthogonal even at k=1 (cos 0.018). Report tasks separately, never average.

Why it’s fixable

  • Un-rotate, not rescale — norm ratio ≈ 1.0 at every lag; only the direction is wrong.
  • Curvature is the signal — a tiny weight move (‖Δθ‖ ≈ 0.0009 at k=1) already rotates the gradient to 0.51 cosine ⇒ high local curvature; the rotation is essentially a Hessian-vector product H·Δθ.
  • Low-dimensional target — gradient stable-rank ≈ 3, rank90 ≈ 50 ⇒ a tractable subspace to model.

The two upgrades over the fixed-linear baseline

Seeded by Pluralis’s Nesterov Method for Asynchronous Pipeline Parallel Optimization (arXiv:2505.01099), which does delay-correction with a fixed, linear, hyperparameter-free weight-space look-ahead. Our two upgrades:

  • Linear → learned projection of the gradient dynamics — the only route that can surpass dense, because it captures curvature (beyond a diagonal Adam rescale). A deterministic function of past gradients is σ(M)-measurable ⇒ caps at parity.
  • Error feedback — the anchor’s periodic refresh is delayed ground truth; the residual r = g_true − ĝ both trains the projection online and is accumulated so projection bias can’t compound (same trick as the B2 delayed_ef merger, one level up).

Kill-gate (GPU-free, the next step)

On the stored (θ, g) pairs, does any projection rung lift cos@k5 from 0.18 to ≥ 0.40, and is the lift off-diagonal (a genuine rotation, not a per-coordinate rescale — the “diagonal trap”)? If no rung clears it → STOP, zero GPU spent. Conditional bar if it passes: projected-anchor merger ≥ B2 delayed_ef (≈0.74) at ~5% gradient-comm; stretch = surpass dense (≈0.766).

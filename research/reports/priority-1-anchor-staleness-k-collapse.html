The k-collapse — extrapolate the anchor’s weights forward (Nesterov-style), don’t patch its gradient

Distilled from the EXP-37 latency×merger map and the EXP-38 dense-drift probe. Full run dirs de-bloated; numbers below are the durable record.

The problem. The anchor computes its full gradient on delay_K-stale weights θt−K. That gradient rotates to orthogonal by k ≈ 10–20 (cosine 0.51 → 0.18 @k=5 → ~0 @k=10 → −0.01 @k=20); norm is preserved, so it is a pure rotation. By k ≈ 10–20 it is unusable. EXP-37 confirmed it operationally: stable at cadence/delay 5/5, breaks at 20/20.

The fix — extrapolate the weights, not the gradient. Predict the anchor’s future weights θ̂t ≈ θt and compute the gradient at that look-ahead point: g(θ̂t). If the weight prediction is good, g(θ̂t) ≈ g(θt) — the live gradient — for free. This is exactly Nesterov: gradient taken at the extrapolated point.

Why weights, not gradients

  • Valid by construction. g(θ̂t) is a real gradient at a real weight point — no “is this even a legitimate gradient?” worry that haunts directly rotating/synthesising a gradient vector.
  • The supervision is clean and free. What we predict (future weights) is exactly what the fast circuit hands the anchor at sync (see below). A future gradient ground-truth is not naturally available.
  • Tractable target. Weights move little and smoothly (‖Δθ‖ ≈ 0.0009 at k=1); the gradient subspace is low-rank (stable-rank ≈ 3, rank90 ≈ 50). The rotation is essentially H·Δθ — curvature the projector can learn.

The two upgrades over AsyncPP’s fixed-linear look-ahead

Seeded by Pluralis’s Nesterov Method for Asynchronous Pipeline Parallel Optimization (arXiv:2505.01099), whose weight-space look-ahead is fixed, linear, hyperparameter-free. Our two upgrades:

  • Linear → learned, per-block weight-projection. A small projector Pφ per block predicts θ̂t from the anchor’s weight trajectory. Per-block (not per-parameter) keeps the trainable count tiny and is naturally cross-rank-identical. Modelling the trajectory non-trivially is what captures curvature — the only route that can surpass dense (beyond a diagonal Adam rescale).
  • Supervision from the fast circuit. At each sync the true current weights θt arrive from the fast circuit. The residual θt − θ̂t is a supervised error signal that trains Pφ online, so the prediction becomes accurate over time and adapts to the actual (variable) staleness.
predict θ̂t = Pφ(anchor trajectory)  →  use g(θ̂t) in the merger  →  at sync, train Pφ on θt − θ̂t  →  repeat

Kill-gate (GPU-free, the next step)

On the stored (θ, g) pairs: (1) can a per-block projector predict θt from the trajectory (low weight-prediction error)? (2) does the gradient at the predicted weights lift cos@k5 from 0.18 to ≥ 0.40, and is the lift off-diagonal (a genuine rotation, not a per-coordinate rescale — the “diagonal trap”)? If no rung clears it → STOP, zero GPU. Conditional bar if it passes: look-ahead-anchor merger ≥ B2 delayed_ef (≈0.74) at ~5% gradient-comm; stretch = surpass dense (≈0.766).

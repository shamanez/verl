The setup in one slide

What "communication-efficient" means here

  • Split the model across pipeline stages. The expensive traffic is the activations & gradients crossing each stage boundary. Rollouts (vLLM generation) are ordinary — out of scope for compression.
  • At each boundary we apply a per-(token, dimension) mask — drop a fraction p of the crossing tensor (reference p = 0.9). Same mask is pinned per prompt across all forward passes in a step.
  • Two optional repairs for the information we drop: (A) a clean (unmasked) pass every K steps; (B) an anchor network + spectral correction.
  • Method OFF ⇒ byte-identical to upstream verl. Loss is vanilla GRPO, no KL, no entropy.
One model, one optimizer, one batch — two coupled circuits.

Where we are

Four headlines

stable

Masked training + a clean pass every K never diverged — on easy or hard data. No NaNs, no gradient explosion, importance-sampling ratios stay tame.

easy ✓ / hard ✗

Reaches dense parity on GSM8K with ~95% of boundary traffic cut. On harder Big-Math it stalls — learns far less than the dense control.

no free repair

The anchor + spectral correction doesn't help in RL — and the code is correct. It's a modeling mismatch, not a bug. (Details: slide 7.)

why

RL gradients are sparse, contrastive and non-stationary — the opposite of what masking + low-rank correction assume. The theory is the rest of this talk.

Result · stability

Masked + clean-every-K is stable — and we understand why

A clean-resettable sawtooth, not a ratchet. The drift never accumulates.
  • No divergence, no NaN, no gradient explosion in any masked+clean run.
  • Clipping & importance sampling stay tame. Clip-fraction sits low and never creeps toward saturation across a K-window. The only "huge"-looking number is the raw masked grad-norm — finite, clipped, and Adam-scale-invariant. Judge on reward, not grad-norm.
  • Why IS stays near 1: the mask is pinned per prompt, so the same mask is in both the old-policy and the train forward. The ratio reflects the real weight update — not mask noise. Resample the mask per pass and this blows up.
  • Why no ratchet: the clean step re-anchors the deployed policy every K — drift is repaired before it compounds.

Result · easy vs hard

Tolerable for elicitation, fatal for learning

  • GSM8K (easy): masked + clean@K converges to dense parity. The cost is speed — it re-anchors more slowly between clean steps — not final quality.
  • Big-Math (hard): masked + clean@K stalls flat, while the dense control finds a real, modest gain. EXP-19 vs EXP-20. The headroom exists — the masked gradient just can't reach it.
  • The gap widens with difficulty: easy tasks hide the degradation, hard tasks expose it.
Mechanism preview: the harder the task, the smaller and sparser the true learning signal — so the same mask noise drowns it. (Slide 6.)

Theory I · the root cause

RL gradients are intrinsically sparse

  • Group-advantage zeroing. GRPO normalizes reward within each prompt's group. If all rollouts in a group tie (all-right on easy, all-wrong on hard), the advantage is exactly zero → that whole prompt contributes no gradient. The useful batch is a thin band of "sometimes-right" prompts. It thins as the model improves — and is thinner from the start on hard data.
  • Sparse, binary reward. A 0/1 correctness signal is a small, noisy nudge — not a dense regression target. The true gradient norm is small (and measurably smaller on harder data).
  • Contrastive, not corrective. The signal lives in directions that separate good from bad trajectories — typically low-variance, specific directions, not the high-variance bulk.
  • Non-stationary. On-policy data + a moving policy ⇒ the "right direction" reshuffles every step.

Contrast with SFT

SFT: a fixed target. The gradient is dense (every token labelled), stationary, aligned across the batch, and concentrated in a stable low-rank subspace. We literally update along the principal directions.

RL: a sparse, contrastive, moving signal. The useful direction is not the dominant subspace — it's a thin, shifting set of directions that the bulk hides.

Theory II · why the SFT repair doesn't transfer

Anchor + spectral correction can't help an RL gradient

Masking a sparse signal pushes the masked gradient nearly orthogonal to the true one.

The correction is a linear reweighting of the masked gradient in the basis of M (clean-gradient EMA):

M = β·M + (1-β)·G_clean # EMA M = U S Vᵀ # SVD G_filt = U·d(UᵀG_mask V)·d·Vᵀ # reweight
  • You can't recover a direction the vector doesn't contain. If G_mask ⊥ G_true, no reweighting of G_mask creates the missing signal.
  • The clean gradient is used as geometry, never applied. What actually works (clean-every-K) applies the true gradient. The filter only borrows its basis — and a stale, EMA-lagged, non-stationary basis at that.
The code is correct & unit-tested (EMA, two-sided Tikhonov, α=1 no-op all verified). This is a modeling mismatch, not an engineering bug.

Theory III · the train–inference gap

The masked policy is decorrelated, yet recoverable

  • During masked steps, the masked training forward is nearly decorrelated from the deployed (vLLM) policy — correlation near zero. The masked forward is a different function; on a per-token basis it barely resembles what we sample with.
  • Every clean step snaps it back to near-perfect agreement (train ≈ inference). That's the sawtooth from slide 4.
  • Why it doesn't compound: because the mask is pinned and systematic (not random per pass), the gap is a consistent offset, not accumulating noise. The clean step erases it before the next window.
  • Honest caveat: the gap is flat in direction, but its absolute level drifts up slowly — that tracks the improving policy's sharpening, not weight corruption.

THE DEPLOYED POLICY STAYS VALID

The policy we ship (unmasked weights) is always healthy.

The decorrelation lives only inside the masked forward — an intermediate computation, never the thing we evaluate or deploy.

So a scary-looking instantaneous gap is compatible with a perfectly stable model.

Theory IV · the question you raised

Is no-KL GRPO an implicit KL regularizer?

Yes — but be precise about which KL. Two different things wear the same name:

  • KL-to-reference (the β·KL term we removed): keeps the policy near the SFT model. We genuinely dropped this — nothing pins us to the base model.
  • KL-to-previous-iterate (a trust region): still present, implicitly. GRPO is a proximal / trust-region method even with β = 0.

So: implicit trust region — yes; implicit anchor-to-reference — no.

Where the implicit trust region comes from

  • PPO clipping caps the ratio at 1±ε — a one-sided proxy for a per-step KL bound (the TRPO → PPO lineage). Our clip-fraction is low: the region is active but gentle.
  • On-policy + group-mean-zero advantages ⇒ each update redistributes mass within a group without a net push — a small, natural-gradient-like step.
Why this matters for masking: a bounded per-step move absorbs the mask's gradient error. The mask corrupts direction within the trust region but can't produce an unbounded update. This is a third reason masking survives in RL — alongside per-prompt pinning and clean re-anchoring.

Assumptions & caveats

What we're assuming — and where to push

  • Spectral was OFF in the latest runs. EXP-17/18/19/20 test plain masked + clean-every-K. The "fails by orthogonality" result is from the earlier spectral diagnostic — consistent, but a different run.
  • Elicitation vs learning is a spectrum, not a binary. "GSM8K = elicit, Big-Math = learn" is a useful framing, with n = 1 per task.
  • Reasoning vs retrieval. At 1.5B, RLVR gains on Qwen may be circuit-level retrieval of memorized skills, not new reasoning — observationally hard to separate. Doesn't change the comms result; does qualify the word "reasoning."
  • The "small true gradient" story is inferred from clean-step gradient magnitudes and reward sparsity, not from a direct decomposition of the gradient into signal + bias + noise.
  • Near-rank-1 stability assumes the masked update mostly perturbs magnitude along the capability direction rather than rotating off it. Plausible, contingent.
  • Partial controls. The dense Big-Math control ran far enough to show the gain but didn't finish; treat exact margins as provisional.

So what · next

The decisive test & the design lesson

The mask-rate sweep

Theory predicts a threshold p*: lower the drop rate → more true signal survives → SNR clears the floor → Big-Math should learn again. GSM8K should stay insensitive at high p. This is the experiment that decides generalization.

Apply, don't project

The repair that works injects the true gradient (clean-every-K). The one that doesn't only borrows its geometry. Future repairs should add signal, not reweight a signal that's already gone.

Banked wins

Stable masked GRPO with per-prompt pinning + periodic clean re-anchor; a verified spectral implementation; and a clear, falsifiable account of why RL ≠ SFT here.

Masked boundary training is stable in GRPO because the loss is an implicit trust region and we pin + periodically re-anchor — but the supervised spectral repair can't transfer, because RL's learning signal is sparse, contrastive, and nearly orthogonal to what masking leaves behind.
